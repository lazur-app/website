You can use dictation on Mac in Word by clicking the Dictate button in the Home tab or by using the macOS system-wide dictation feature. Both methods allow you to convert your speech into text directly within your document.

## Using the Microsoft 365 Dictate Button

If you have a Microsoft 365 subscription, the most direct way to use voice input is the built-in Dictate tool. This feature is integrated into the Word interface. It is designed specifically for drafting text and handles formatting commands better than generic system tools.

First, open your document in Word. Ensure you are signed in to your Microsoft 365 account. Navigate to the Home tab on the ribbon. You will see the Dictate button, represented by a microphone icon, in the Voice group on the far right. Click this button to start listening. If this is your first time, macOS will ask for permission to access your microphone. Click OK to proceed.

Once the button is active, a small red recording dot appears. You can now speak your content. To stop, click the Dictate button again or press Option + F1. If your function keys are mapped to system controls like volume or brightness, you may need to press Fn + Option + F1 instead.

## Using macOS System Dictation

If you do not have a Microsoft 365 subscription, or if you prefer a tool that works across all apps, use the macOS system-wide dictation. This feature is free and built directly into your Mac. It does not require a specific app subscription.

To set this up, go to System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation. Toggle the switch to On. You can choose your preferred language and the shortcut key to trigger the feature. The default is often pressing the Fn key twice or holding it down.

To use this in Word, click inside your document where you want the text to appear. Press your chosen shortcut key. A microphone icon will appear on your screen, indicating that the Mac is listening. Speak clearly and at a moderate pace. Press the Escape key or your shortcut again to stop the process. This method is useful if you need to dictate into other apps like Apple Notes or email clients as well.

## Comparison of Dictation Methods

Choosing the right method depends on your workflow and subscription status. The Microsoft 365 Dictate tool is optimized for document structure. It often handles punctuation and formatting more naturally for long-form writing. However, it requires an active internet connection at all times.

System-wide macOS dictation is more versatile. It works in any text field on your Mac, not just Word. Depending on your version of macOS, it may offer better offline performance. Use the table below to decide which fits your needs.

| Feature | Microsoft 365 Dictate | macOS System Dictation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Subscription Needed | Yes | No |
| Works in other apps | No | Yes |
| Internet Required | Yes | Sometimes |
| Formatting Support | High | Moderate |

## Troubleshooting Common Dictation Issues

If you find that dictation on Mac in Word is not working, start with your microphone settings. Go to System Settings > Privacy & Security > Microphone. Ensure that Microsoft Word is toggled to the on position. If the app is not listed, you may need to restart Word.

Another common issue is the keyboard shortcut not triggering the dictation window. If you are using the Microsoft Dictate button, check that you are logged in. If you are using system dictation, check your keyboard settings to ensure the shortcut is enabled and not conflicting with other apps. Sometimes, restarting the Word application resolves minor glitches that prevent the microphone from activating.

## Tips for Better Dictation Accuracy

Dictation works best when your audio source is clean. Use a dedicated headset or a high-quality built-in microphone for the best results. Background noise can lead to errors that require manual correction. Speak in complete sentences and try to avoid filler words like um or ah.

If you are struggling with accuracy, try to enunciate clearly. You can also use punctuation commands. For example, say "comma" or "period" to insert those marks. Practice these commands to improve your drafting speed. For more on improving your speed, check out our guide on [speaking speed](/blog/speaking-speed-words-per-minute).

## Why Dictation Saves Time

Many users find that dictating their initial thoughts is faster than typing. While the average typing speed is around 52 words per minute, speaking speed is often double that. Even with the time spent correcting errors, the net gain can be significant. If you find your typing speed is low, consider trying a [typing speed test](/tools/typing-speed-test) to see where you stand.

Over the course of a year, moving to a voice-first drafting process can save you over 100 hours of effort. This is particularly useful for long reports or academic papers where the volume of text is high. Start by dictating your rough drafts and using the keyboard only for final edits and formatting adjustments.
