Dictation on macOS allows you to convert your spoken words into text within any application that has an editable text field. It is a built-in, free feature that provides a quick way to draft documents, emails, and messages without using your keyboard. ## How to enable dictation on macOS To get started with dictation on your Mac, you must first ensure the feature is active. Open the Apple menu and select System Settings. Click Keyboard in the sidebar. You may need to scroll down to find this section. Locate the Dictation section and toggle the switch to On. Click Enable in the confirmation prompt that appears. Within this same menu, you can also configure your preferred Microphone source and the languages you intend to use. If you have multiple microphones connected, ensure the correct one is selected to get the best results. ## How to use dictation effectively Once enabled, you can start dictating by pressing the designated keyboard shortcut. The default is often pressing the Fn or Globe key twice. If your keyboard has a dedicated microphone key, you can use that instead. You can customize this shortcut at any time by going to System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation > Shortcut. When you are ready to stop, press the shortcut key again, press the Escape key, or click Done in the feedback window. You can also start dictation in many apps by selecting Edit > Start Dictation from the menu bar. ## Understanding the 30-second limit One of the most common questions involves how long you can speak before the Mac stops listening. While there is no fixed time limit for a single session, the feature will automatically stop after approximately 30 seconds of silence. This is a safety measure to prevent the system from recording background noise indefinitely. If you pause to think for longer than half a minute, you will need to restart the dictation process. ## On-device processing and privacy Apple has focused on privacy by enabling on-device processing for dictation on modern Apple Silicon Macs. This means your audio does not need to be sent to Apple servers to be transcribed. This happens automatically on M1 chips and later, provided your language and configuration are supported. This approach keeps your voice data on your machine. ## Using dictation for multitasking A great feature of modern macOS is the ability to multitask while dictating. On Apple Silicon Macs, you can continue typing on your keyboard while dictation is active. The cursor will pause during your typing and resume when you stop. This allows you to mix voice and text input fluidly. ## Managing punctuation and formatting Modern versions of macOS support automatic punctuation. This means the system will insert commas, periods, and question marks based on your speaking cadence. This feature is enabled by default. If the system misses a mark, you can dictate it manually by speaking the name of the punctuation, such as saying comma or period. ## Limitations of built-in dictation It is important to remember that Apple Dictation is a transcription tool. It is not designed to be a full writing assistant. It does not automatically remove filler words, correct grammar, or restructure complex sentences. If you find yourself needing specialized vocabulary or professional-grade voice-to-code features, you might find third-party tools more effective. Also, ensure Voice Control is not enabled in your Accessibility settings, as it may override the standard dictation functionality. ## Troubleshooting common issues If you encounter problems, check your microphone settings first. If you are still having trouble, you can look into specific guides for [troubleshooting dictation on Mac](/blog/dictation-on-mac-not-working-troubleshooting).
