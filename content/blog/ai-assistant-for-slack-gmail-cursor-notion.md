No single AI assistant covers Slack, Gmail, Cursor, and Notion equally well, but Notion Agent comes closest. It connects to Slack and Gmail, Cursor covers coding requests that start in Slack, and a voice assistant on your Mac can handle the rest.

This guide shows what each app's AI can do in 2026, what plan you need, where the limits sit, and how to split the work so no tool gets more access than it needs.

## Why one AI assistant rarely covers all four apps

Each app was built for a different kind of work. Slack holds conversations. Gmail holds requests, approvals, and follow-ups. Cursor is a code editor with an agent inside it. Notion holds the notes, specs, and plans that come out of the other three.

Most vendors build AI for their own app first. That is the sensible order for them. For you, it means four assistants, four plans, and four sets of permissions. A single tool that reads a Slack thread, drafts the Gmail reply, and turns the outcome into a Notion brief is still uncommon.

The cost of switching adds up. Slack's 2024 Workforce Index surveyed 10,045 workers across six countries between March 6 and 22, 2024. It found that desk workers spend about a third of their day on tasks they describe as low-value or not meaningful to their job.

The takeaway is practical. An assistant helps most when it removes the copying, pasting, and tab switching around those tasks. A smarter chat window in another tab does not remove any of that.

| App | Built-in AI option | What it can reach | What it needs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Slack | Slack AI features and Slackbot | Slack conversations, with features that depend on your plan | Check Slack's pricing page for your tier |
| Gmail | Gemini in Gmail | Threads, drafts, search, and your primary calendar | An eligible Google Workspace or Google AI plan |

Read the table as a map, not a promise. Vendors change plan names and features often. Confirm the current details on each vendor page before you pay for anything or roll a tool out to a team.

Slack needs extra care here. Its pricing page lists AI features by tier, and the Free and Pro listings do not agree with each other. Check the page, then confirm with Slack before you assume a feature is included.

## Slack: what an AI assistant should do in a thread

Slack is where most of your reading happens. The jobs that save real time are narrow and specific:

- Summarize a long channel thread into decisions, owners, and open questions.
- Draft a reply that matches the tone of the people in the thread.
- Pull action items out of a decision and paste them into a task list or a Notion page.
- Write a short standup update from the last day of messages.

Here is a worked example. Your team argued for a day about a launch date. Ask the assistant for three things: the final decision, the owner, and the question still open. Then ask for a two-sentence reply for the channel. You read the output, change one word, and post it. The assistant did the reading. You kept the decision.

Notion Agent can also post in Slack as you, covered later in this guide. That is a different job, and it needs a stricter review step.

Before you upgrade anyone's plan, compare the tiers. Slack lists four plans on its pricing page. The prices below come from that page.

| Slack plan | Price per user per month | What the pricing page says about AI |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free | $0 | AI listing is inconsistent with Pro, so confirm with Slack |
| Pro | $8.75 billed monthly, or $7.25 billed annually | AI listing is inconsistent, so confirm with Slack |
| Business+ | $18 billed monthly, or $15 billed annually | Labeled "Advanced AI" with summaries, Slackbot, AI search, and daily recaps |
| Enterprise+ | $45, billed annually through sales | Adds enterprise search |

The mistake to avoid is buying a higher tier for one AI feature without checking that the feature is in that tier. Read the current page, then ask Slack to confirm in writing if the purchase is for a team.

## Gmail: what Gemini and Notion Agent can do with your inbox

Gemini in Gmail is what Google calls a collaborative inbox assistant. Google's Help page says it can summarize threads, draft and refine messages, organize email, search, and propose meeting times. It requires an eligible Google Workspace or Google AI plan. Personal accounts reach it only through Google Workspace Experiments, which Google describes as a trusted tester program.

The same help page lists limits that matter in daily use:

- Gemini can only give information about your primary calendar and create events on it.
- Gemini cannot create new Gmail labels.
- Requests that match more than 10,000 email threads are not processed.
- A confirmed bulk action can be undone for 60 seconds only.
- Web answers need a phrase such as "Use Google Search" in the prompt.

The 60-second undo window is the one to remember. If you ask Gemini to archive a large set of threads, check the result right away. After a minute, the undo option is gone.

Notion Agent reaches the same inbox by a different route. After you connect Gmail under Settings > Notion AI > AI connectors, Notion Agent can search your inbox, draft and send emails, archive or trash messages, manage labels, and unsubscribe from senders. Notion says write actions, such as sending, ask you to confirm before the agent acts. The personal Gmail connector is free on all plans.

A worked example helps. Ask for "a reply that says Thursday works and asks for the deck." The agent drafts it. Read the recipient line and the subject before you confirm. A reply sent to the wrong thread costs more time than the draft saved.

## Cursor: send coding requests from Slack

Cursor is a coding tool. Its Slack integration is the only piece that touches your team's chat. Setup takes three steps:

1. Connect Slack from the integrations page in Cursor.
2. Install the Cursor app in Slack.
3. Mention @Cursor in a channel with your request.

The commands are short:

- "@Cursor [prompt]" starts a Cloud Agent, or adds a follow-up in a thread.
- "@Cursor agent [prompt]" forces a new agent.
- "@Cursor help" lists the commands available right now.

Three edge cases catch teams:

- Channel settings apply only to public channels, so plan your setup around that limit.
- Privacy Mode (Legacy) is not supported, because Cloud Agents need temporary code storage. If your security review depends on that mode, check before you connect.
- Team Pools, which are self-hosted pools with team and channel defaults, require an Enterprise plan.

Cursor is not an email or docs assistant. Its Slack documentation covers coding agents, and this guide found no Cursor connector for Gmail or Notion. Do not build a workflow that depends on one.

Here is a worked example. Someone posts a bug in a public channel with a stack trace. Mention @Cursor with the file path and the behavior you expect. A Cloud Agent starts the work, and it can open a pull request. You review that pull request like any other change. For writing prompts and explaining errors on your Mac, see the [Cursor page for Mac](/apps/cursor).

## Notion: connectors and who gets them

Notion reaches the most of the four apps, but it comes with rules. Notion's help page says third-party apps connect to Notion AI only on the Business or Enterprise plan. The exception is the personal Gmail AI Connector, which is free for all plans. Connecting also expects you to be a workspace owner with admin rights in the app you connect.

Once your Slack account is connected, the Slack connector lets Notion Agent search Slack. It can also post messages, reply in threads, edit messages it posted, and add or remove reactions. Notion says the agent acts as you, so anything it posts shows up as coming from you. It can only reach what you can already see.

That limit helps. An agent with your permissions cannot read a private channel you are not in. The risk is different. A message can go out under your name in seconds, with no draft step in between.

Notion's Slack documentation does not say whether Slack writes ask for confirmation. Its Agent page states the confirmation rule for Gmail only. Test it before you rely on it. Send a throwaway message in a private channel you own, and see what the agent asks first.

Credits matter too. Notion's Agent page says that on Business and Enterprise, Auto uses Standard usage, while choosing a model uses Select usage. Check your team's usage before you turn the agent on for everyone.

## Which first tool fits which job

Use this table to pick a starting point. It matches the tool to the job, not to the longest feature list.

| Job | Best first tool | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Summarize a busy Slack thread | Slack's own AI, if your plan includes it | It stays in the app where the thread lives |
| Draft a Gmail reply | Gemini in Gmail, or Notion Agent with Gmail connected | Gemini needs an eligible plan. The personal Gmail connector is free |
| Send a coding request from chat | Cursor with @Cursor in Slack | It starts a Cloud Agent from the thread |
| Turn notes into a brief | Notion Agent | It works where the page already lives |
| Ask about something on screen, or write in any app | A Mac voice assistant | It works in whatever app is open |

If you can only afford one tool, pick the job you repeat most each day. For most people, that is Slack or the inbox. Start there.

## Mistakes people make when they connect everything

Most failures are not technical. They are habits. Watch for these five:

1. Connecting all four accounts on day one. Start with one. Add the next app after the first one has earned its place.
2. Trusting the summary. Salesforce's writeup of the 2024 Slack survey says 93% of desk workers do not consider AI outputs completely trustworthy for work tasks. Treat a summary as a draft of your reading, not a record of it.
3. Skipping practice. The same writeup says only 15% of desk workers strongly agree they have the training to use AI effectively. It also says workers trained to use AI are up to 19 times more likely to report that AI improves productivity. Practice on low-stakes threads before you rely on a tool for anything important.
4. Forgetting the agent acts as you. Notion's Slack connector posts under your name. Make a rule that nothing gets sent, posted, or replied to until you have read it.
5. Assuming plan coverage. Confirm the tier on the vendor page before you buy or invite a team. Slack's AI listing, Notion's connector rules, and Google's Gemini requirements all change.

## A simple first week with four apps

You do not need to test everything at once. This schedule keeps the risk small:

- Day 1: Pick the job that wastes the most time. For most people, that is the Slack thread or the inbox. Connect only that app and test with a low-stakes message.
- Days 2 and 3: Use the summary on one busy channel. Note what it missed. Missed decisions are the most common failure, so look for them first.
- Day 4: Try @Cursor in one public channel on a small fix. Review the pull request yourself.
- Day 5: Check what the assistant posted or sent. Look at your Sent folder in Gmail and the page history in Notion. Decide what to keep.

Add the next app only after the first one has run cleanly for a week. Write down which tool did what. If something goes wrong, you will know which connection to check.
