Voice assistant machine learning powers real-time screen context by interpreting your active applications through accessibility data and visual models. This allows your Mac to understand what you are looking at and provide relevant help without you needing to manually describe your screen. When you use a modern voice assistant, it acts as a layer that sits between your intent and your open windows. It uses the same underlying technology as tools like VoiceOver to read the structure of your apps. This is often called the accessibility tree. It tells the assistant exactly what buttons, menus, and text fields are visible. If an app does not provide this information, the assistant switches to computer vision. This is where the machine learning model scans the pixels of your screen to identify elements. It can recognize a button, a search bar, or a specific document title just by looking at the layout. This dual approach ensures that the assistant works across almost any app on your Mac. You can ask questions about your current task, and the assistant provides answers based on the specific context of that window. This capability is changing how we interact with our computers.
