To dictate in Apple Notes on Mac, turn on Dictation in System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation. Then click in a note, press your Dictation shortcut or the Microphone key, and start talking. Say “new line” or “new paragraph” to break text, and press Escape when you finish.

Dictation is a Mac system feature, so the same setup works in every app. Apple says it covers composing messages and notes, but not dictating into search boxes. This guide covers the setup, the shortcut, spoken punctuation, the 30-second silence limit, accuracy, and the mistakes that waste time. It also explains how dictation differs from the audio recording feature in Notes.

## Turn on Dictation before you open Notes

You only do this once. The setting applies to every app that accepts typed text. Apple’s Dictation page is labelled macOS 27. Menu names may sit in different places on older versions, and I did not check those versions, so compare the steps with what you see.

1. Open System Settings from the Apple menu.
2. Click Keyboard in the sidebar.
3. Click Dictation and turn it on.
4. If a prompt appears, click Enable.
5. Choose whether to share audio recordings with Apple.

Read the last step before you click through. Apple gives you that choice. Take a minute with it if you dictate private notes.

Dictation is not available in all languages or regions, and features may vary. If your language is not supported, no setting will fix that. Check Apple’s support pages for your language before you troubleshoot anything else.

## How to start dictation inside an Apple Note

Once Dictation is on, you have three ways to start it. The cursor must sit in the body of the note, because dictated words go in at the cursor. A cursor in the title line will not catch your speech the way you want.

| Start method | What you do | When it helps |
|---|---|---|
| Microphone key | Press the Microphone key, if your keyboard has one | Fastest option, and nothing extra to remember |
| Dictation shortcut | Press the shortcut you chose in Dictation settings | Best when your hands already sit on the keyboard |
| Edit menu | Choose Edit > Start Dictation | Useful when you forget the shortcut |

Here is a worked example. Open a note called Inbox and click after the last line. Press the shortcut once. Then say: “Call Dana about the invoice new line Ask if the March figures changed.” Notes shows two lines. Depending on auto-punctuation, you may also see a period at the end of each one.

Press Escape to stop. Apple lists Escape, the Microphone key, and the Dictation shortcut as ways to stop. Any of the three works, so use whichever is closest to your hand.

## The Dictation shortcut for Apple Notes on Mac

Apple does not say which key starts Dictation by default. Its page only says you pick one from the Shortcut pop-up menu in Dictation settings. Third-party guides disagree with each other. FluidVox says press Fn twice. Other guides point to F5 on some newer MacBooks, or to Control twice with an external keyboard. Check what your own Mac does instead of trusting a guide, this one included.

To set a shortcut you control:

1. Open System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation.
2. Open the Shortcut pop-up menu.
3. Pick a shortcut, then test it inside a note.

Pick a key you can press without looking. Avoid a key another app already uses. If two apps want the same key, one of them will not respond when you press it.

The common mistake is setting a shortcut and never testing it. Test it in Notes on a quiet afternoon. Do not find out it is wrong in the middle of a meeting.

## Punctuation and new lines by voice in Notes

Dictation reads spoken commands as well as words. Apple’s page lists these working on Mac:

| Say this | You get |
|---|---|
| “new line” | One line break, the same as pressing Return once |
| “new paragraph” | A paragraph break, the same as pressing Return twice |
| “question mark” | A question mark at that point in the text |
| “heart emoji” | A heart emoji |

Apple also supports other spoken punctuation names, such as “exclamation mark.” Use the names you would say out loud in a normal sentence, and keep the list small enough to remember.

Auto-punctuation is the other half of the story. In supported languages, Dictation adds punctuation and capitalization on its own. You can turn that off in Dictation settings.

Leave auto-punctuation on if you talk in full sentences. Turn it off if it adds marks you did not want, or if you prefer to speak every comma yourself.

One mistake is saying “new paragraph” after every sentence. The note ends up full of empty space and is hard to scan. Use it when the topic changes, and let “new line” handle smaller breaks.

## How long Dictation keeps listening

Apple gives you two rules to plan around.

- You can dictate text of any length without a timeout.
- Dictation stops by itself when no speech is detected for 30 seconds.

Here is what that means in practice. Say you pause for 40 seconds to remember a client’s name. Dictation stops at the 30-second mark. The words already in the note stay where they are. Press the shortcut again and carry on from the cursor.

For quick capture, that limit helps you. Speak one idea at a time, then pause. Restart the shortcut for the next thought. You get cleaner notes this way, and each one is easier to find later.

## Accuracy, language, and privacy on Mac

Apple Intelligence adds an on-device model that improves spelling, punctuation, and capitalization in dictation. Apple says it works in English on Mac models with M3 or later and at least 12GB of unified memory. To check your chip and memory, choose Apple menu > About This Mac.

| Requirement | What Apple says |
|---|---|
| Language | English |
| Chip | M3 or later |
| Memory | At least 12GB of unified memory |

I could not confirm from the sources I checked whether M1 and M2 Macs get the improved model. Check Apple’s page for your exact Mac before you count on it. Your Mac still dictates without the improved model, so the basic workflow in this guide stays the same.

On privacy, Apple says general dictation is handled on your device and is not sent to Siri servers. One third-party guide says that on Mac, languages not processed on the device need a connection to Siri servers. The sources differ. Confirm for your language before you dictate anything sensitive, such as medical or legal notes.

## Dictation or the audio recording feature in Notes

Apple’s Notes guide describes a separate feature. You record audio inside a note, and Notes generates a transcript you can search. Dictation does something different. It types your spoken words at the cursor.

| | Dictation | Record and transcribe |
|---|---|---|
| What you get | Typed text at the cursor | Audio plus a searchable transcript |
| Good for | Quick capture and drafts | Meetings, lectures, and interviews |

The sources also disagree on timing. Apple’s guide says the feature gives “live audio transcripts.” FluidVox says the transcript is generated afterward and is not live. Test it in a note before you rely on it for anything that matters.

## A quick capture routine for Apple Notes

Quick capture means getting a thought out of your head before it slips away. This routine works for many people. Adjust it to your own day.

1. Keep one note called Inbox open in a window on your desktop.
2. When a thought hits, press your Dictation shortcut and speak.
3. Press Escape when you finish, or let the 30-second rule stop it.
4. Before you stop work, open Inbox and move each item into its real note.

Short captures keep you clear of the 30-second limit. They also stay easy to sort later. A long, rambling capture is harder to split apart than five short ones.

The common mistake is trying to dictate a whole day into one note. The result is a wall of text with no breaks. Start a fresh capture each time a new thought begins.

Your own speed matters more than any study. Check how fast you type and how fast you speak with the [typing speed test](/tools/typing-speed-test) and the [speaking vs typing test](/tools/speaking-vs-typing-test). Then decide which method fits a capture.

## Does dictating into Notes save time?

The closest published study is not about Apple Notes. It compared clinical notes written by emergency medicine residents, some dictated and some typed. The WestJEM study covered about 75,500 notes across two periods, 2018 to 2019 and 2021 to 2022. Of the dictated notes, 77.9% were completed within 24 hours, against 70.9% of typed notes.

Adjusted odds ratios for on-time completion were 1.47 for 2018 to 2019 and 2.93 for 2021 to 2022. The study did not report completion times in minutes. It is observational and retrospective, so it shows a timing difference, not a cause. It says nothing about consumer dictation or about your typing speed.

Apple’s Dictation page does not claim that dictation is faster than typing. Treat any vendor claim about words per minute with suspicion, and measure your own speed instead.

## Proofread what you dictated

Dictation makes errors that typing rarely makes. Names, numbers, dates, and product terms are the usual trouble spots. You might see a similar-sounding name where you meant a different one, or a figure with a digit off.

Build a short check into the end of each capture:

- Read every name and check it against your contacts.
- Read every number and date out loud, once.
- Fix jargon by typing it yourself, so the correction is quick and clean.

Thirty seconds of checking is cheaper than a wrong figure sent to a client.

## Common mistakes people make with Notes dictation

- Setting a shortcut and never testing it inside a note.
- Trying to dictate into a search box, where Apple says dictation is not supported.
- Dictating private content without reading the audio sharing choice in setup.
- Speaking in one long stream and losing track when the 30-second rule stops it.
- Trusting a shortcut from a guide without checking it on your own Mac.
- Saying every punctuation mark out loud when auto-punctuation would handle most of them.

## Fixing Apple Notes dictation problems

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing happens when you press the shortcut | Dictation is off, or the shortcut is set to a different key | Turn Dictation on in System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation, then test the shortcut in a note |
| Dictation stops mid-thought | No speech for 30 seconds | Restart the shortcut and carry on from the cursor |
| Extra or wrong punctuation | Auto-punctuation is on and clashes with your spoken marks | Turn auto-punctuation off in Dictation settings, or speak the marks yourself |
| Your language is missing | Not every language is supported | Check Apple’s support pages for your language |
| Dictation does nothing in a search field | Apple says dictation covers messages and notes, not search boxes | Dictate into the note body, then copy the text where you need it |
| The shortcut triggers something else | Another app or setting uses the same key | Pick a different shortcut in Dictation settings |

If the shortcut does nothing at all, check that Notes can use the microphone. Open System Settings > Privacy & Security > Microphone and confirm that the app is allowed. Then try again in a fresh note.
