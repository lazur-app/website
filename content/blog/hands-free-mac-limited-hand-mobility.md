To control your Mac without your hands, turn on Voice Control. Go to Apple menu > System Settings > Accessibility > Voice Control, switch it on, then say commands such as “Show numbers” or “Click Save.”

## What Voice Control does on a Mac

Voice Control is Apple's built-in feature for running your Mac with spoken commands. Apple describes it as a way to operate your Mac, navigate apps and dictate text. That last part causes confusion. Dictation turns your speech into words. Voice Control also moves the pointer, presses buttons, scrolls and opens menus.

The difference decides which tool you need. If you only want to write, dictation is enough. If you need to click, scroll and move between windows, you need Voice Control. Our guide on [Voice Control vs dictation on Mac](/blog/voice-control-vs-dictation-mac) explains why the two do not always run at the same time.

Apple's customization page says Voice Control “listens only for selected commands.” A phrase that is not on the list does nothing. Voice Control is not a general chat tool. When a phrase fails, check the command list before you decide the Mac is broken.

## What you need before you turn it on

You need three things. You need a Mac running a release that Apple's guide covers, a working microphone, and internet access the first time you enable Voice Control.

Apple's guide has a version selector that lists macOS 27, Tahoe 26, Sequoia 15 and Sonoma 14. Pick the version that matches your Mac before you follow any menu path. Menu names can change between releases, so the page you read should match what you see on screen.

The first time you turn it on, the Mac must connect to the internet for a one-time file download from Apple. Apple does not publish the size of that download or how long it takes. Plan for it. Do not wait until you have a deadline and a weak connection. After the download, Voice Control works without an internet connection.

## How to turn on Voice Control for the first time

Work through the steps in order. The first setup takes the longest, because of the download. Everything after that is quick.

1. Connect the Mac to the internet. The first enable needs the one-time download.
2. Click the Apple menu, then click System Settings.
3. Click Accessibility in the sidebar. Scroll down if you do not see Voice Control.
4. Click Voice Control, then switch it on.
5. Wait for the download to finish. The menu bar icon shows a not listening state while the feature is off.
6. Say “Show commands” to see what the current app accepts.
7. Say “Stop listening” when you want a pause, and “Start listening” to resume.

You can also ask Siri to turn Voice Control on by saying “Turn on Voice Control.” Siri only starts the feature. It does not replace the command list, so you still need to learn the commands.

## The commands you will use most

Start with a small set. Most people need only a handful of commands for ordinary work. Apple's reference lists these examples.

| Say this | What it does |
|---|---|
| “Show numbers” | Puts a number on every item you can click |
| “Show names” | Puts each item's name on screen instead |
| “Click Save” or “Click 12” | Clicks an item by its name or number |
| “Show grid” | Overlays a numbered grid on the screen |
| “Show window grid” | Limits the grid to the active window |
| “Hide grid” | Removes the grid |
| “Scroll Down” or “Scroll up” | Scrolls the active window |
| “Move cursor right 5 pixels” | Nudges the pointer by a set distance |
| “Press OK” or “Click Done” | Presses a button in a dialog |
| “Drag [item] to [location]” | Moves an item to a drop location |
| “Show commands” | Lists what the current app accepts |
| “Stop listening” or “Start listening” | Pauses or resumes Voice Control |

Leave a short pause, about half a second, between chained commands. Say “Click Save,” wait, then say “Scroll Down.” If you run them together, the second command may not register. Small pointer nudges help when an item sits just outside the click target. Use them in small steps rather than one big move.

## Labels, numbers and the grid

Labels are the fastest way to click. Say “Show numbers,” and Voice Control puts a number on each item it can click. Say “Click” followed by the number, or just say the number once the labels are on. If you prefer words, say “Show names” instead. Use “Hide numbers” or “Hide names” when the screen gets crowded.

Text fields get their own labels. Say “Show text numbers” to label text input areas, so you can choose which box takes your words.

Some items have no name at all. Icons on a toolbar and controls in custom apps often look like this. For those, say “Show grid.” The screen fills with numbered boxes. Say a grid number to narrow the area, then keep going. When the grid cannot narrow any further, saying a bare number performs a click.

Use “Show window grid” when several windows are open. The grid stays inside the active window, so the numbers are easier to read. Say “Hide grid” when you finish.

Here is a worked example. You want to click a small icon in the Mail toolbar that has no name. Say “Show grid.” Then say the number of the box that holds the icon. Look at the smaller grid that appears, and say the number inside the box that holds the icon. Repeat until a bare number clicks.

## Dictation, spelling and command modes

Voice Control has modes. Dictation mode is on by default, and it enters words as you say them. Spelling mode enters characters one at a time, which helps with names, file names and short unusual words. Command mode responds only to commands. Apple also lists Swift mode for writing Swift code, which most people will never need.

| Mode | What it does | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| Dictation (default) | Enters words as you speak them | Writing emails, notes and documents |
| Spelling | Enters characters one at a time | Names, file names and short unusual words |
| Command | Responds only to commands | Clicking and navigating without stray text |

The common mistake is staying in dictation mode while you try to click. Your commands can land in a text box as plain words. If clicks do nothing and words appear on the page, check the mode first. Apple's command reference is the place to confirm the exact phrase that switches modes on your version of macOS.

For long messages, dictation is the better fit. For a step-by-step approach, see [how to write emails by voice on Mac](/blog/how-to-write-emails-by-voice).

## Microphone, language and overlay settings

Most of the tuning happens in System Settings > Accessibility > Voice Control. Open it after the first setup, then adjust three things.

**Microphone.** Choose a microphone from the list, or leave it on Automatic. A specific microphone you pick by name is more predictable, especially when your Mac switches inputs during a call.

**Language.** You can change the language or add one. After you download extra languages, switch between them from the Voice Control menu bar icon.

**Overlay.** Voice Control can always show item names, numbers or the numbered grid on screen. Turn on “Fade overlay after inactivity” to dim the overlay after a set period. Adjust the delay with the “Fade by” slider. Start with a longer delay. Shorten it once you know which labels you rely on.

## Other hands-free options Apple lists

Voice Control is not the only option. Apple's mobility page lists several tools that work with a keyboard, a pointer or switches.

| Feature | Where to find it | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Voice Control | System Settings > Accessibility > Voice Control | Speaking commands to click, scroll and dictate |
| Full Keyboard Access | System Settings > Accessibility > Keyboard | Navigating with keyboard shortcuts instead of a mouse |
| Accessibility Keyboard | System Settings > Accessibility > Keyboard | Typing with an on-screen keyboard |
| Switch Control | System Settings > Accessibility > Switch Control | Operating the Mac with one or more switches |
| Pointer Control | System Settings > Accessibility > Pointer Control | Controlling the pointer |
| Dwell | Accessibility > Keyboard, then the Info button next to Accessibility Keyboard, then Dwell settings | Controlling the pointer by eye or head tracking |

Choose one to start with, then try a second only after the first feels comfortable. Voice Control suits you if speaking is easy and you want to move by voice. Full Keyboard Access suits you if pressing keys is easier than moving a mouse. Dwell suits you if you move the pointer with where you look and how long you look.

## How many people this could affect

Numbers give you scale, not a verdict on your situation. I could not find a national figure for limited hand mobility, so the numbers below cover related groups.

CDC reports that 18.9% of US adults aged 18 and older had any difficulty walking or climbing steps in 2025. That measure covers legs and stairs, not hands, so treat it as context. CDC's older 2019 figure is more useful for scale. It shows 9.0%, or 22.7 million adults, had a lot of difficulty or could not do at least one of six domains. CDC notes that the 2019 and 2025 figures are not directly comparable.

A 2025 study in JMIR mHealth and uHealth surveyed 218 primary care patients. Of the 47 people with a physical disability, 34.0% had never used a digital voice assistant, compared with 21.1% of people without one. The gap was not statistically significant (P=.09). The study covered digital voice assistants in general, not Mac Voice Control. Treat it as a hint, not a rule.

## Mistakes people make with hands-free Mac control

- **Expecting natural-language Voice Control on the Mac yet.** Apple's May 19, 2026 announcement describes natural-language Voice Control for iPhone and iPad, in English in the US, Canada, the UK and Australia. The section covers iPhone and iPad only, so do not build your Mac plan around it.
- **Following old menu names.** Older articles say System Preferences. Current Apple pages say System Settings, so use that name.
- **Running chained commands together.** Leave about half a second between commands, or the second one may be missed.
- **Forgetting the mode.** If clicks do nothing and words appear, you are probably in dictation mode.
- **Guessing commands.** Voice Control listens only for selected commands. Say “Show commands” in each app you use often.
- **Setting it up in a hurry.** The first enable needs the one-time download. Do it before you need the Mac to respond to your voice.

## What to practice first

Try these in Notes or Safari, one at a time. Do not rush through them.

- Turn on Voice Control and wait for the download to finish.
- Say “Show numbers,” then click a link by saying its number.
- Say “Show grid,” narrow the grid to a blank area, then say a bare number to click.
- Say “Scroll Down,” pause for a moment, then say “Scroll up.”
- Say “Show commands,” then write down the five commands you use most.
- Open the overlay settings and set “Fade overlay after inactivity” to a delay you like.
- Say “Hide numbers” and “Hide grid” when you finish, so the screen stays clear.
