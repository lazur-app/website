Learning software usually means a YouTube video recorded on a different version, or a help article with screenshots that don't quite match your screen. You pause, squint, hunt for the button, and lose ten minutes on a two-click task.

There's a better way: ask, and have the answer **drawn on your actual screen**.

## Why tutorials are slow

A tutorial shows you *someone else's* screen. You're constantly translating: their menu is in a different place, their version has a different icon, their sidebar is collapsed. The instructions are fine; the mapping is the hard part.

## Being shown instead of told

Lazur, an AI buddy for Mac, skips the mapping. Hold ⌥ ⌘ and ask "how do I turn on dark mode?":

1. Lazur looks at your screen and **circles "Appearance"** in System Settings.
2. You click it. Lazur looks again.
3. It **circles "Dark"**.
4. You click. It tells you you're done.

It works the same way in Figma, Notion, your company's admin panel, Xcode, or a web dashboard you've never seen. If you click somewhere else, it adapts and points at what to do from where you are.

## Quick "where is it?" answers

Not everything is a multi-step task. Often you just can't find something:

- "Where's the share button?"
- "Where's Control Center?"
- "Which of these is the export option?"

Lazur circles it, with a small label, so you can see it in a second, even for icons that have no text.

## When it helps most

- **Starting a new job** with unfamiliar internal tools.
- **Switching from Windows** to Mac.
- **Rarely used settings** you set once a year and forget.
- **Helping a parent or colleague**: install Lazur on their Mac and they can ask instead of calling you.
