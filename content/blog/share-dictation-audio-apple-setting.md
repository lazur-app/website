No, you do not have to share dictation audio with Apple to use Dictation on your Mac. The setting is optional, and Apple says it does not store your audio unless you opt in.

That answer leaves out the details that matter. The switch is labeled Improve Siri & Dictation on some Macs and Improve Siri & Apple Intelligence on others. This guide explains what the switch covers, what changes when it is on or off, and how to decide. You can change your mind later, so this is not a one-time choice.

## What the "share audio recordings" choice controls

When you set up a Mac, you may see two buttons: Share Audio Recordings and Not Now. Share Audio Recordings lets Apple store audio from your Siri and Dictation sessions. Not Now avoids sharing. Apple says you can change this later under Privacy & Security.

Apple describes opting in as agreeing to let it store and review samples of your interactions. That covers the audio, the text transcripts, and related request data. Apple says employees review the audio, and that they work under confidentiality obligations.

Here is the key point. The setting decides whether Apple may keep your recordings and have people review a sample of them. It does not decide whether Dictation works. Keep that in mind as you read the rest.

Siri and Dictation share one switch. Many people assume the toggle only affects Siri. It also covers Dictation. The same choice applies whether you ask Siri a question or dictate a paragraph.

## How to find and change the setting on your Mac

Menu labels can shift between macOS releases, so your screen may read a little differently. Start with this path:

Apple menu > System Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements

1. Click the Apple menu in the top left corner and choose System Settings.
2. Click Privacy & Security in the sidebar.
3. Scroll down and click Analytics & Improvements.
4. Turn the Siri and Dictation toggle on or off. The label reads Improve Siri & Apple Intelligence or Improve Siri & Dictation.
5. Look at the toggle state, then close System Settings.

Apple describes one combined toggle for the Mac. The pages I checked do not show a separate Dictation-only switch. If your Mac shows a different label, look for Siri under Analytics & Improvements.

Settings live on each Mac, so a work laptop and a personal laptop can differ. If you use both, check each one. Do not assume the choice you made on one machine carried over to the other.

## What happens when the setting is on

Apple's legal page says that when Improve Siri & Dictation is on, Apple "may store all of your interactions with Siri and Dictation, including the audio data and transcripts." That is the trade. You give Apple your recordings, and Apple may keep them.

Apple ties that data to a random identifier that your device generates. Apple says the identifier is "not tied to your Apple Account or email address," and it changes multiple times per hour. That makes the stored record harder to link to you. It is still a record of what you said.

Retention is the next question. Apple says data may be kept for up to two years when the setting is on. Reviewed interactions may be kept longer. Treat two years as a ceiling, not a promise.

Why does Apple want the data? Engadget reports that Apple says reviews help fine-tune Siri, Search, Voice Control, Translate, and automatic speech recognition models. Only a subset of interactions is chosen for review, not all of them.

### A worked example

Say you dictate a short Slack reply to a colleague named Priya about a launch date. Your message is one sentence. Apple's policy covers more than that sentence. Apple says Dictation may send contextual data, such as contact names and app names. Apple also says request data may be retained and used for up to two years to improve its services.

If the setting is on, that is the period Apple describes. If it is off, Apple says it does not store your audio unless you opt in. The message is the same in both cases. The rules around it are what change.

## What happens when the setting is off

Apple's legal page says, "Your audio data is not stored by Apple unless you opt in." Turning the setting off puts you in that default position for future sessions.

Two gaps remain. Apple says turning off Siri and Dictation deletes transcripts stored on the device that personalize the experience. Its pages do not say what happens to data already sent to Apple. If you turned the setting on last year and off today, Apple's pages do not tell you whether that older data is removed. If that matters to you, contact Apple Support and ask directly.

Engadget published a piece on September 15, 2026 that advises turning the setting off. Its headline also tells readers to check devices if the setting was on before. That second step is easy to forget.

## Does Dictation send your voice to Apple anyway?

This is where people get tangled. The setting is about storage and review. Whether speech leaves your Mac is a separate question.

Apple's Mac Dictation guide says dictation on Apple silicon Macs is processed on-device for supported languages. Your Keyboard settings show whether dictation is processed on the device and not sent to Siri servers. Check it yourself:

1. Open System Settings > Keyboard.
2. Find the Dictation section.
3. Read the note about where processing happens.

Three more details fill in the picture.

- Dictation is not available in every language or region. Check the language list in Apple's guide before you rely on it.
- Apple Intelligence dictation adds spelling, punctuation, and capitalization through an on-device model. It is English only, on M3 or later Macs with at least 12GB of unified memory.
- Third-party apps have their own rules. If an app uses Speech Recognition for transcription, Apple says the audio may be sent to Apple.

One caveat. The Apple Mac Dictation guide I checked covers Apple silicon only. Some third-party write-ups say Intel Macs send dictation to Apple. I could not confirm that, so check Apple's current guide for your model. For more on what private dictation means in practice, read [on-device speech to text on Mac](/blog/on-device-speech-to-text-mac).

## The 2019 grading story and why it still comes up

Most of the worry traces back to 2019. In July 2019, Apple said less than 1% of daily Siri activations were used for grading. Those clips were typically only a few seconds long.

On August 1, 2019, Apple said it was suspending Siri grading globally. It also said a future software update would let users choose whether to take part. I could not confirm when that choice shipped or what its default is. Check your own Mac before you rely on it.

Read the 1% figure as history, not as today's review rate. It describes activity in 2019. Do not use it to judge what happens now.

## Dictation limits that matter more day to day

The setting is not the only thing that shapes how Dictation behaves. Apple says Dictation has no length timeout, so you can talk for as long as you like. It does stop on its own after 30 seconds without detected speech.

Here is what that looks like. You are drafting a paragraph and stop to think for longer than 30 seconds. Dictation ends. Start it again and keep going. If you pause often, expect to restart it now and then. Restarting is a small cost compared with the privacy question, but it is worth knowing before you draft a long document by voice.

## Reading Apple's privacy pages without getting lost

Apple has several pages on this topic, and they do not all say the same thing. Here is how I would use them.

- The Apple Support article on turning Improve Siri & Dictation on or off tells you where the toggle lives and what opting in means.
- The Apple Legal page on Improve Siri and Dictation covers storage, the random identifier, and the two-year period.
- The Apple Legal page on Siri, Dictation & Privacy covers the default ("not stored unless you opt in") and what third-party apps may send.
- The Mac Dictation guide covers on-device processing, language support, and the 30-second silence rule.

Read the legal pages for the rules and the support article for the steps. When two pages seem to disagree, trust the more specific one and note the gap. The gap about data already sent is the one that stays open.

## Should you turn it on? A quick decision table

Most people do not need to think hard about this. The table below gives a simple default for each situation.

| Your situation | Suggested setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| You dictate client, legal, or medical notes | Off | Apple says employees may review a sample of stored audio when the setting is on. |
| You dictate personal messages and notes | Off, unless you want to help | Apple says data may be kept for up to two years when the setting is on. |
| You want to help improve Siri and Dictation | On, after you read Apple's pages | Apple says reviews help fine-tune several speech and voice models. |
| You are not sure | Off | Apple says audio is not stored unless you opt in, and you can change it later. |

My suggestion is simple. If your work includes private or client material, keep the setting off. If you want to help, read Apple's pages first, then decide.

## Mistakes people make with this setting

- Clicking Not Now and assuming Dictation is broken. Apple describes Not Now as avoiding sharing. It does not describe it as turning Dictation off.
- Believing that turning the setting off erases everything. Apple's pages do not confirm that for data already sent.
- Looking for a Dictation-only switch. Apple describes one combined toggle for the Mac.
- Reading the 2019 figure as current. It is history.
- Forgetting other apps. A third-party app that transcribes audio has its own rules, and Apple's switch does not govern them.

## If you already turned it on

If the setting was on before, work through these steps once.

1. Turn the Siri and Dictation toggle off under Analytics & Improvements.
2. Open Keyboard settings and read the Dictation note about on-device processing.
3. Review any app that transcribes audio for you, and read its privacy page.
4. Contact Apple Support if older data is a concern, since Apple's pages do not say whether data already sent is deleted.
5. Recheck the toggle after a major macOS update, because labels and menus can move.
