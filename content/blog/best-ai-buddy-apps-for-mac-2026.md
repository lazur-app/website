In 2026, the best AI assistant for Mac is less about which model is smartest and more about **where the assistant lives**. The new wave sits on your screen, sees what you see, talks with you and takes actions, instead of waiting in a chat tab for you to explain everything.

Here's how the main options compare, with honest notes on who each one is for. Prices and features change quickly; check each product's site before you decide.

## Lazur

Lazur is an **AI buddy that lives in your MacBook's notch**. Hold ⌥ ⌘ and ask about anything on screen; it answers out loud. Ask where something is and it **draws a circle around it**. Ask how to do something and it **walks you through one click at a time**, waiting for you between steps.

It also handles the small stuff by voice: timers that tick in the notch, reminders, calendar events, music, opening apps and links, running Shortcuts. Longer jobs, like comparing products or reading a pile of pages, run in the background in Chrome and come back with a report. And hold fn to dictate clean text into any app.

**Best for:** people who want help *in* their apps all day, especially learning software and getting small tasks done without switching windows.
**Privacy:** listens and looks only while you hold the key; audio and screenshots aren't stored.

## Clicky (HeyClicky)

Clicky, from Y Combinator's Spring 2026 batch, sits **right next to your cursor** and sees your screen. You talk to it and it walks you through what you're doing. Saying "Clicky Agent" starts an agent that researches or builds in the background, and it connects to tools like Notion, Gmail and Calendar.

**Best for:** people who like a companion that follows the pointer, and teams that want agents connected to their work tools.
See our [Lazur vs Clicky comparison](/blog/lazur-vs-heyclicky).

## Muse for Mac (Meta)

Meta brought its Muse assistant to the Mac in September 2026. It works with local files and native apps, such as Messages, Calendar, Notes and Mail, to carry out tasks like organising folders or summarising your day, and asks for approval before sensitive actions. At launch it rolled out first in the US.

**Best for:** people already using Muse on their phone who want the same assistant on the desktop.
See [Lazur vs Muse](/blog/lazur-vs-muse-for-mac).

## ChatGPT and Claude desktop apps

Both are excellent for long conversations, writing and analysis. They live in their own windows, so for screen-specific questions you share a screenshot or window first. They're the best choice when the work *is* the conversation.

## Apple Intelligence

Built into supported Macs, free, and private by design. It handles writing tools and Siri requests well, but it's not designed to see an arbitrary app and guide you through it.

## How to choose

- **You learn new apps often** → pick one that points at things and guides step by step (Lazur).
- **You want agents tied to your SaaS tools** → Clicky's connectors are worth a look.
- **You live in Meta's apps** → Muse.
- **Your work is long-form thinking** → ChatGPT or Claude alongside any of the above.
