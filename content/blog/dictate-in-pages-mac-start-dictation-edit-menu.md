Click where you want the text in Pages, then choose Edit > Start Dictation. Speak your sentences, say each punctuation mark by its name, and click Done below the microphone when you finish.

This guide covers the whole job. You get the menu path, the first-time setup, the punctuation rules, the settings that change how Dictation behaves, and the fixes for the problems people hit most. It ends with a quick way to test whether dictating actually saves you time.

## Turn on Dictation before you open Pages

Pages uses the same Dictation feature as the rest of macOS. If Dictation is off, Edit > Start Dictation will not help. Turn it on first.

Open System Settings, click Keyboard in the sidebar, and go to Dictation. Turn it on, then click Enable. Apple asks whether to share audio recordings to help improve Siri and Dictation. That choice is yours. You can change it later under Privacy & Security.

Two checks save you time later:

- **Language.** Dictation is not available in every language or region. Features vary by place, and Apple lists the details on its macOS feature availability page.
- **Microphone.** Dictation listens through the microphone your Mac uses. If you use a headset or a desk microphone, select it in System Settings > Sound before you start.

## How to dictate in Pages on Mac, step by step

This is the method Apple documents for Pages. Once you have done it twice, it takes about as long as opening a menu.

1. Open your document and click the spot where the text should go. Dictation inserts text at the insertion point, so click first.
2. Choose Edit > Start Dictation from the menu bar.
3. If this is your first time, follow the guided setup that appears. Read each screen. You only do this once.
4. Speak at a steady pace. Look at the microphone under your text to confirm it is listening.
5. When you finish, click Done below the microphone.

Dictation has more than one way to start. Use the one that fits how you work.

| Method | Where it is set | When it helps |
|---|---|---|
| Edit > Start Dictation | Pages menu bar | You are already in Pages and want a quick start |
| Dictation keyboard shortcut | System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation | You switch between Pages, Mail and Notes all day |
| Microphone key | Keyboards that have a Microphone key | You want one press and no menu |

Apple's Mac Help page does not name a default shortcut. Some third-party sites list a double press of Fn or Control. Your Mac may be set differently, so check the shortcut in System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation. Change it if it clashes with a shortcut in another app.

## Set up Dictation in Pages the first time

The guided setup appears only on first use. It is short, so follow each screen.

If you skipped it, or it never appeared, you can still turn Dictation on from System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation. The steps in the section above work the same way after that.

Before you write anything important, run a test. Open a blank page in Pages and dictate two short sentences. Put a comma in one and end the other with a period. Read the output. If the words and marks look right, you are ready. If not, check the microphone and the Auto-punctuation setting covered below.

Dictation writes at the insertion point, and that point does not move on its own. If you click in the middle of a finished sentence, the new words land there. Look at the cursor before you speak. A misplaced block of dictated text is one of the easiest mistakes to miss, and it is slow to find later.

## Punctuation when you dictate in Pages

Apple's Pages guide says to add punctuation by speaking its name. Say comma for a comma and apostrophe for an apostrophe. Keep each mark as its own word, with a short pause before and after it.

Here are three examples. The output column shows what a clean run should look like. Your voice, your microphone and your room will change the result.

| What you say | What you should see |
|---|---|
| *Thanks comma Sam period* | Thanks, Sam. |
| *That is Maya apostrophe s draft* | That is Maya's draft. |
| *Are we ready question mark* | Are we ready? |

Watch for the word itself. If you want the word comma in a sentence, Dictation may read it as punctuation. Check each paragraph after you dictate it, and fix any surprise by hand. That is faster than trying to say around the problem.

## Auto-punctuation: keep it on or turn it off

In supported languages, Dictation inserts commas, periods and question marks as you speak. Apple's Mac Help page describes this behavior. You do not have to say those marks yourself.

To change it, open System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation and turn off Auto-punctuation. After that, say each mark by name, as in the examples above.

Here is the difference on a short memo. With Auto-punctuation off, you say the marks:

You say: *Hi Priya comma the budget draft is done period Can you check the totals question mark*

Pages can show: Hi Priya, the budget draft is done. Can you check the totals?

Turn Auto-punctuation on, and you can say *Hi Priya the budget draft is done Can you check the totals* and let Dictation add the marks in supported languages. Check the result either way. Dictation makes mistakes, and you are the one who signs the memo.

For most writing, keep Auto-punctuation on. It saves you words and reads smoothly. Turn it off when you need exact control, such as a list of product codes, a set of names with unusual punctuation, or a short list of file names.

## What Apple Intelligence changes on macOS 27

On macOS 27 with Apple Intelligence, Dictation uses an on-device model. Apple says it improves spelling, punctuation and capitalization. The improvement has conditions, so check them before you count on it.

| Requirement | What Apple lists |
|---|---|
| macOS | macOS 27 with Apple Intelligence |
| Language | English |
| Chip | M3 or later |
| Memory | At least 12GB of unified memory |

Some third-party sites list different hardware rules for macOS 27. Use Apple's page as the source. If your Mac does not meet these rules, you will not get the on-device improvement. Dictation still works, and the punctuation rules above still apply.

## Fix the problems people hit most with Dictation

Most problems have one of four causes: the 30-second timeout, Auto-punctuation, Voice Control, or the microphone. Start with the table.

| What you see | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Dictation stops in the middle of a thought | No speech for 30 seconds | Choose Edit > Start Dictation again and keep talking |
| Commas and periods appear where you did not want them | Auto-punctuation is on | Turn it off in System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation |
| Start Dictation does nothing | Dictation is off, or Voice Control is on | Turn on Dictation. Voice Control replaces standard Dictation, so turn it off if you want Dictation |
| Words come out wrong | Background noise or a distant microphone | Move closer to the microphone, quiet the room, and fix the rest by hand |
| The same wrong word keeps appearing | Dictation keeps missing one term | Fix it once, then search the draft with Find for the same error |

The 30-second rule catches many people. Dictation does not know you are thinking. It hears silence and stops. If you pause to look something up, you lose the session. Keep a note open for the lookup, then return to Pages.

Two mistakes repeat often. The first is stopping and restarting after every sentence. Keep going until you reach a natural break, then click Done. Restarting every sentence costs more time than fixing a few words at the end.

The second is dictating in a noisy room with the laptop microphone across the table. A closer microphone and a quieter room help more than most settings.

## Is dictation in Pages faster than typing?

It can be. Test it on your own work before you decide.

One study measured speech and keyboard entry on touchscreen phones, not on Macs. Ruan and colleagues compared the two for short messages in two languages. English speech entry reached 153 words per minute. English keyboard entry reached 52 words per minute, so speech was 2.93 times faster in that test.

Those numbers do not describe Pages. Short phone messages are not long documents. Editing takes time too. Still, the gap is wide enough to test.

Here is what the study rates would mean for a hypothetical 1,530-word draft. At 153 words per minute, speaking it takes about 10 minutes. At 52 words per minute, typing it takes about 29 minutes. Add the time you spend fixing mistakes to each side before you compare.

Run your own version of the test. Time yourself typing a 200-word paragraph on one topic. Then dictate a second paragraph on a similar topic, and count the fixes in each. You can also check your typing speed with our [typing speed test](/tools/typing-speed-test), or compare the two methods with the [speaking versus typing test](/tools/speaking-vs-typing-test).

## A checklist before you share a dictated document

Dictated text needs a review pass. A short checklist makes that pass faster.

- **Names and terms.** Check every name, product and place. Dictation may pick a word that sounds right but is wrong.
- **Numbers.** Check each figure against your notes. Spoken numbers may come out in a different form than you expect.
- **Paragraph breaks.** Dictation gives you the words. Add breaks, indents and spacing by hand.
- **Formatting.** Bold, italics, headings and lists are not part of the spoken text. Add them after you finish speaking.
- **Quotes and brackets.** Check quotation marks and parentheses. They are easy to miss, so look at them twice.

Read the draft once for meaning. Then read it again for the list above. Two short passes are easier to finish than one long one.
