Dictation on your MacBook Air might stop working due to a temporary system glitch or a setting conflict. You can usually fix this by toggling the Dictation feature off and on again or checking your microphone privacy permissions. When you find your Mac is not responding to your voice, it is easy to feel frustrated. Most issues stem from simple software misconfigurations rather than hardware failures. Follow this guide to diagnose and resolve the problem systematically. ## Check Your System Settings First The most common reason for dictation failures is a simple setting that has been toggled off. Go to the Apple Menu > System Settings > Keyboard. Look for the Dictation section. If the switch is on, turn it off. Wait for about 10 seconds. Then, turn it back on. This simple action forces your Mac to refresh the speech recognition service and reload the language model. If you have multiple languages enabled, ensure the primary language is set correctly. Sometimes, a corrupted language pack can stop the whole system from listening. ## Verify Microphone Privacy Permissions Your Mac protects your privacy by restricting which apps can access your microphone. If you are trying to dictate into a specific app like Slack or Microsoft Word, that app must have explicit permission. Go to System Settings > Privacy & Security > Microphone. You will see a list of apps that have requested access. Ensure the toggle next to the app you are using is turned on. If it is already on, try turning it off and back on again. You must quit and relaunch the app for these changes to take effect. If the app is not in the list, the app itself might be the problem. Try using a native Apple app like TextEdit to see if dictation works there. If it works in TextEdit but not in your target app, the issue is with that app's compatibility. ## Select the Correct Input Source Your MacBook Air has a high-quality built-in microphone array. However, if you have a Bluetooth headset connected, your Mac might be trying to use its microphone instead. Go to System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation. Check the Microphone source dropdown menu. If it is set to an automatic selection, try choosing the MacBook Air Built-in Microphone directly. Bluetooth devices often use a hands-free profile that drops to a lower sample rate. This can significantly reduce the accuracy of your transcription. If you must use a headset, ensure it is fully charged and within range. ## Disable Conflicting Features Like Voice Control You cannot use Voice Control and Dictation at the same time. If you have Voice Control enabled for accessibility, it will override your standard Dictation settings. Check your settings by going to System Settings > Accessibility > Voice Control. If the switch is on, turn it off. Restart your Mac to ensure all background processes have cleared. Many users find that Voice Control interferes with their workflow because it is designed for system-wide navigation rather than simple text entry. ## Test in a Neutral Environment If you are still having trouble, test your microphone in a neutral environment. Open a new document in TextEdit and press your chosen dictation shortcut. If you see the microphone icon appear but it does not react to your voice, check your hardware. Ensure the microphone holes on the side of your MacBook Air are not blocked by a case or dust. You can also check your input levels in System Settings > Sound > Input. Speak normally and watch the input level meter. If the bars do not move when you speak, your microphone might be having a hardware issue. It stops listening after 30 seconds of silence. If you are pausing to think, you will need to reactivate the dictation shortcut. Also, keep in mind that background noise affects performance. The built-in array is excellent, but it cannot filter out loud environments. If you are working in a busy cafe, your accuracy will drop. Try to stay within two feet of your laptop for the best results. Using a keyboard shortcut is a great way to manage this. If the default shortcut is not working, try customizing it to something easier to reach. You can change this in System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation. Pick a combination that does not conflict with your other apps, such as ⌘ ⌥ ⇧ 1. This ensures that you can always trigger the feature when you need it.
