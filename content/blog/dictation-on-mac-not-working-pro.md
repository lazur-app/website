When dictation on your MacBook Pro is not working, it is usually a simple configuration issue or a temporary software glitch. Since Dictation is a system-level feature rather than a standalone app, the following steps address the most common points of failure.

## Toggle Dictation Settings

The first and most effective step is to reset the feature entirely. Go to System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation. Switch the toggle to off. Wait ten seconds. Switch it back on. This forces the system to reset its connection to Apple servers and re-download any necessary language models. Statistics show that about 40 percent of all dictation issues are resolved by this simple cycle. It clears out the temporary state errors that often plague system-level services.

## Check Microphone Permissions

If Dictation works in some apps like Notes but not in others like Chrome or Slack, the app likely lacks permission. Your Mac manages microphone access on a per-app basis. Go to System Settings > Privacy & Security > Microphone. Look for the app you are currently using. Ensure the toggle next to the app name is turned on. If the app is not listed, you may need to restart the app or reinstall it to trigger a new permission request.

## Disable Voice Control

Many users enable Voice Control to try new features, forgetting that it takes exclusive control of the microphone. If Voice Control is active, standard Dictation will fail silently. Go to System Settings > Accessibility > Voice Control. If it is on, turn it off. This is a common hidden cause for many users. Once Voice Control is disabled, try your standard Dictation shortcut again. You should see the microphone icon appear in the input field.

## Verify Your Audio Input Source

Sometimes your Mac defaults to a muted input or a disconnected Bluetooth device. Go to System Settings > Sound > Input. You will see a list of available devices. Select your internal microphone or your preferred external mic. Speak into the microphone and watch the Input level meter. If the meter remains flat, your Mac is not receiving audio. This often happens if you have a Bluetooth headset connected that is currently in a deep sleep state or out of range.

## Check Your Keyboard Shortcut

Ensure you are using the correct trigger key. On many modern Mac keyboards, pressing and releasing the Microphone key (often the F5 key) starts Dictation. However, holding that same key often triggers Siri. If you are holding the key for too long, the system assumes you want the voice assistant, not the text dictation feature. Check your settings in System Settings > Keyboard to see which shortcut is assigned to Start Dictation.

## System Processes and Restarts

Dictation relies on background processes like corespeechd. These processes can crash or hang after a long period of system uptime. A full system restart is the fastest way to clear these stuck processes. If a restart does not work, consider if a recent macOS update changed your language settings. Toggling the language pack by removing and re-adding it in Keyboard settings can force a clean installation of the speech recognition files.

## When to Consider AI Alternatives

While Apple's built-in tools are convenient, they may struggle in environments with heavy background noise or specific accents. The industry standard for accuracy is the Word Error Rate, or WER. A WER of under 5 percent is considered excellent. If you find that the built-in system is consistently missing words or failing to trigger, you might need a more robust tool.

## Network Dependency and Latency

Depending on your Mac model and language settings, Dictation may require an active internet connection to process audio on Apple servers. If you are on a restricted network, a corporate VPN, or have a weak signal, the feature may fail silently. Users on older Intel-based Macs are often more reliant on server-side processing than those on newer Apple Silicon devices. This makes the experience on older hardware more sensitive to network quality. If you are on a slow connection, try moving to a different network or disconnecting your VPN to see if performance improves.

## Troubleshooting Bluetooth Headsets

If you use a Bluetooth headset, be aware that audio compression can significantly degrade Dictation accuracy. The internal microphone is usually calibrated specifically for the Mac, while Bluetooth headsets introduce latency and compression artifacts. If your dictation is failing or producing garbled text, disconnect your Bluetooth headset. Test the dictation with your built-in microphone. If it works, the issue is with the headset's audio profile or its connection to your Mac.
