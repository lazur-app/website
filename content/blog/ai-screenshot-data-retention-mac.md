Where your AI screenshots go depends on the tool that takes them. Some tools keep the image on your Mac and never upload it. Others send it to a company's servers, and that company sets how long it stays.

This guide walks through each path on a Mac. It also covers what the published policies say as of November 2026, and where those policies disagree.

## How long do AI tools keep screenshots on a Mac?

A screenshot can take one of three paths. It can stay on your Mac as a file. It can be uploaded, processed, and sent back to you. Or it can be uploaded and kept for a set period.

Each path carries different risk. The wording matters too. A company can say it does not store an image after processing, and still keep records for other reasons. You will see an example of that below.

## Where does macOS save screenshots on a Mac?

Before any AI tool can read a screenshot, macOS saves it somewhere. By default, that place is the Desktop. Apple's support page says screenshots save to the desktop by default. Anything on your Desktop is easy to drag into a chat, attach to an email, or share by accident.

You can change the location in a few clicks. Press ⇧⌘5 to open the Screenshot app. Click Options, then choose where screenshots are saved. Pick a folder you check often, so you always know what is inside it.

If you do not want a file at all, use the clipboard. Control-click the thumbnail that appears in the corner of the screen, then choose the Clipboard option. You can also add Control to the key combination, such as ⌃⇧⌘3. The image goes to the clipboard instead of the desktop.

One caution about menus. Apple's screenshot page carries a September 14, 2026 date and mentions macOS 27 features. Menu wording can change between releases. If the names on your screen do not match these steps, look for the same options under a similar name.

Most people never open that Options menu. Their screenshots pile up for months. Set a regular time to clear the Desktop, then empty the Trash.

## What does Apple keep from Private Cloud Compute?

Apple Intelligence can send some requests to Private Cloud Compute, which is Apple's server system for processing. Apple's Mac privacy support page says the data processed there is not stored or made accessible to Apple. It also says results are not retained by Private Cloud Compute after they return to your device.

Apple's June 10, 2024 newsroom release says Private Cloud Compute uses your data only to fulfill your request and never stores it. The release also says the data is made accessible to no one, including Apple.

You can also check what your Mac has sent. Apple's page explains how to generate a report of requests sent to Private Cloud Compute. The default window covers 15 minutes. You can pick a 7-day window instead, or turn the report off.

The page does not say whether request data is kept anywhere else. That gap is the question to put to Apple directly if it matters to you.

## How long does Codex for Mac keep screen captures?

OpenAI's Codex for Mac includes a feature called Chronicle. Chronicle watches your screen to build context for the assistant. The Next Web reported on it on April 20, 2026.

According to that report, Chronicle captures the display periodically. Raw captures sit in a system temp directory, and they are deleted after six hours. The screenshots also go to OpenAI's servers for OCR and visual analysis. OpenAI states the screenshots are not stored on its servers after processing, according to the same article.

Chronicle is a research preview. The article says it is not available in the EU, UK, or Switzerland.

Here is the caveat. The six-hour figure comes from one news article. I could not confirm it on OpenAI's own pages. OpenAI's macOS help page returned a 403 error when I tried to open it, so I also cannot tell you how the regular ChatGPT app handles screenshots. Check OpenAI's current documentation before you rely on either claim.

## What does Anthropic say about computer use screenshots?

Anthropic publishes two statements about computer use, and they do not line up. Start with the developer docs. The Claude Platform docs describe computer use as a client-side tool. Screenshots and files are captured and stored in the developer's environment, not by Anthropic. The docs also list the tool as eligible for zero data retention, with covered models excluded.

Now the privacy article. Anthropic's March 16, 2026 help article says that by default Anthropic deletes all screenshots from its backend within 30 days. That applies unless you and Anthropic agree on different terms. The same article says Anthropic keeps data to help enforce its Usage Policy. It calls computer use a beta feature. It does not say how long enforcement retention lasts.

So the answer depends on which page you read, and on your contract. If you build on the API, ask Anthropic which terms apply to your account. Get the answer in writing. Do not decide from one page.

## Side by side: AI screenshot retention by tool

Here is the comparison in one place. Where a claim comes from a single report, the table says so.

| Apple Intelligence (Private Cloud Compute) | Apple's server system for processing | Not stored or made accessible to Apple, results not retained after return | Apple Support |
| Codex for Mac (Chronicle) | OpenAI servers for OCR and visual analysis | Raw local captures deleted after six hours, per one news report. OpenAI says not stored after processing | The Next Web |
| Claude computer use (API) | Developer's environment, per Anthropic docs | Anthropic deletes screenshots from its backend within 30 days by default, per its privacy article | Anthropic docs and privacy article |

Microsoft Recall runs only on Windows, so treat its row as a yardstick. Its support page says snapshots stay on the device and are always encrypted. Sensitive information filtering is on by default. It keeps passwords, national ID numbers, and credit card numbers out of Recall. You can delete snapshots at Settings > Privacy & security > Recall & snapshots.

## Worked example: one hold of the shortcut on a bank statement

Say you are reading a bank statement in your browser. You hold the shortcut and ask what a fee means. The answer might take a few seconds. The data path matters more than the speed.

If the tool works locally, the capture stays on your Mac. Check whether the tool writes the capture to disk, and where.

If the tool sends the capture to a cloud model, the image leaves your Mac. The vendor's policy decides what happens next. Anthropic's stated default for computer use applies to developers building on the API. Under that default, screenshots are deleted from its backend within 30 days, unless you agree to other terms. Under Apple's Private Cloud Compute description, results are not kept after they return to your device.

Your statement shows account numbers and a balance. The sources above do not say whether a tool masks those numbers before it sends the image. Ask the vendor whether it filters or blurs sensitive fields.

## Mistakes that leak more screen data than you expect

- **Treating local as private.** A local file is still a file. It stays on disk until you delete it, and anyone with access to your Mac can open it. Email threads count too. If you draft replies by voice, the [Gmail page](/apps/gmail) shows that workflow.
- **Leaving sensitive windows open.** A screen tool sees what is visible. That includes bank balances, payroll pages, password managers, and medical portals. Close them, or hide them, before you hold the key.
- **Reading "not stored" as "kept nowhere."** Apple says Private Cloud Compute results are not retained after they return. Anthropic keeps some data to enforce its Usage Policy. Those are different promises.
- **Trusting one article.** The six-hour Chronicle figure comes from one report. Confirm it with the vendor before you rely on it.
- **Skipping the filter question.** Microsoft's Recall filters card numbers and passwords by default. Ask whether your tool does the same. Test it with a fake card number before you trust it.

## Edge cases that change the answer

Region matters. The Next Web says Chronicle is not offered in the EU, UK, or Switzerland. If you live in one of those places, check what your tools offer before you assume a feature exists or does not.

Preview and beta labels matter too. Chronicle is a research preview. Anthropic calls computer use a beta feature. Terms can change once a feature leaves that stage.

Contracts override defaults. Anthropic's 30-day default applies unless you and Anthropic agree on different terms. Teams with their own agreement should check that paper, not the public help article.

Operating system versions matter as well. Apple's screenshot page references macOS 27. Menu names and file locations may move between releases.

## What to do if you already used these tools

Start with the files. Look in your screenshot folder and on the Desktop for captures you no longer need. Delete them, then empty the Trash.

Next, check the request report. Apple's Mac privacy page explains how to generate the report. It shows requests your Mac sent to Private Cloud Compute in the default 15-minute window, or in the 7-day window if you choose it.

For anything a cloud vendor stored, ask the vendor how to delete your data, and ask for written confirmation. The public pages above do not list a deletion process for every product. The vendor's support team is the right place to start.

## Why people are wary of AI watching their screens

Pew Research Center's April 2025 report found 59% of U.S. adults have not too much or no confidence in companies to develop and use AI responsibly. That adult survey had 5,410 respondents, fielded August 12 to 18, 2024. It measures confidence in companies, not data privacy directly. Treat it as context, not proof about screenshots.

Pew's March 2026 short read adds a wider mood check. In a June 2025 survey, 50% of U.S. adults said AI's growing role in daily life leaves them more concerned than excited. Another 10% said more excited, and 38% said equally both.

## Questions to ask before an AI tool watches your screen

1. Does the screenshot leave my Mac, or does it stay here?
2. If it leaves, is it kept after the answer comes back, and for how long?
3. Does the company keep copies for safety or policy enforcement?
4. Is my content used to train models?
5. Does the tool filter passwords, card numbers, and national ID numbers?

Some privacy pages answer only the first two questions. Look for the other three in the terms or the help center, not the marketing page.
