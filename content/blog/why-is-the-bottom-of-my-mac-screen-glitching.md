If the bottom of your Mac screen is glitching, it is often a sign of either a software conflict or a hardware failure. Because these symptoms can be identical for both, troubleshooting systematically is the best way to determine if you need a simple software fix or a professional repair.

## Why is the bottom of my Mac screen glitching

When you see horizontal lines, flickering, or distorted images at the bottom of your display, your first reaction might be to panic. It is common to assume the worst. However, many display issues start as minor software bugs that are easy to resolve. You should first determine if the issue is persistent. Does it happen only in certain apps? Does it show up when you are on the login screen? If the glitching happens before you even log in, or while you are running Apple Diagnostics, the cause is almost certainly hardware. If it only happens when you are using a specific browser or a heavy application, you are likely looking at a software conflict.

## Initial troubleshooting steps

Before you take your machine to a repair shop, perform these basic steps. They solve a large percentage of display issues.

1. Restart your Mac. This clears temporary system processes and minor software glitches that might be causing the display to behave erratically.
2. Check for updates. Navigate to System Settings > General > Software Update. Ensure you are running the latest version of macOS. Apple frequently releases patches that address display bugs.
3. Disconnect external accessories. Unplug all monitors, USB hubs, and cables. If the issue disappears, the problem is likely related to the external hardware or a faulty cable.

## Booting into Safe Mode

Safe Mode is a powerful tool for isolating third-party software. It prevents certain apps and extensions from loading during startup. If the glitching stops in Safe Mode, you know a third-party app or extension is the culprit.

For Apple Silicon Macs, shut down your Mac. Press and hold the power button until you see Loading startup options. Select your volume, hold the Shift key, and click Continue in Safe Mode. For Intel Macs, restart your Mac and immediately press and hold the Shift key until the login window appears.

## Running Apple Diagnostics

If you still see the glitching, you should run Apple Diagnostics. This built-in tool can help identify hardware-level failures. Shut down your Mac. Turn it on and hold the power button if you have an Apple Silicon Mac. If you use an Intel Mac, hold the D key while turning it on. The system will run a check and report any hardware errors it finds.

## Common hardware and software causes

Hardware issues are often related to loose or damaged display flex cables. A failing LCD panel or a faulty backlight driver can also cause these symptoms. If the issue persists across different screens or during startup, it is a strong signal that the display hardware is failing. On the software side, corrupted NVRAM on Intel Macs can cause intermittent flickering. Resetting the NVRAM is a free, 20 second process that resolves these issues in about 30 percent of cases. Conflicting graphics settings, such as Automatic Graphics Switching, can also trigger glitches.

## OLED and PWM sensitivity

If you own a newer model with an OLED screen, you might be experiencing something different. Some users report discomfort or perceived flickering due to Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) at lower brightness settings. Independent testing shows that some new OLED displays exhibit PWM flickering at 240Hz. This is 62 times lower than the 14,880Hz measured on older Mini-LED models. This difference can cause eye strain or perceived glitching for sensitive users.

## When to seek professional repair

If you have exhausted all software fixes and the screen still glitches, you may need a repair. Screen repairs can range from 300 to 800 dollars depending on the model. Always check if your device is covered under AppleCare+ or a limited warranty before paying for third-party repairs. Remember that nearly 1 in 3 Mac repairs in some regions involves a damaged or cracked screen. MacBook Air screen issues are reported 25 to 30 percent more frequently than on Pro models, often due to the thinner, more fragile design. If you need to document your screen issues for a repair technician, you can use built-in tools to capture the screen, but note that screenshots often do not capture hardware-based glitches. If the glitch does not appear in a screenshot, it is a hardware issue.
