An AI chief of staff is an assistant that takes the small, repeatable work off a founder's plate: replies, scheduling, reminders, and quick research. On a Mac, you can set one up today with a voice assistant that works across the apps you already use, or with Claude Desktop and a folder you choose.

This guide covers what an AI chief of staff can and can't do, how much time it might give back, three setups you can run on a Mac, and the mistakes people make when they start. Read the numbers as directional. Most of the research on founder admin time comes from survey publishers, not academic studies.

## What an AI chief of staff does, and what it doesn't

A human chief of staff does two jobs. They run logistics, and they carry context for the person they support. They make small calls on that person's behalf and flag what needs attention.

An AI version can cover most of the logistics. It can draft a reply, put a call on the calendar, set a reminder, summarize a long thread, or run a search while you work on something else. These tasks are easy to describe and easy to check.

It can't make the judgment calls. It doesn't know which investor you want to keep warm, whether a candidate is the right hire, or whether to cut a price. Keep those decisions with yourself. Let the assistant prepare the material.

A useful way to sort requests is into three groups:

- **Answers.** Questions about what's on screen, like "what does this clause mean?" Nothing changes when you ask.
- **Drafts.** Text you read before you send. A dictated reply is a draft until you press send.
- **Actions.** Anything that changes your data, like adding a calendar event or a reminder.

Start with answers and drafts. Move to actions after you've checked the output a few times. Most early mistakes happen when people skip that step.

## How much admin work founders really lose each week

Many articles say founders spend over a third of their week on admin. The most-cited version of that number comes from a 2023 survey, not 2025. Get the date right before you quote it.

In September 2023, Time etc with Censuswide surveyed 251 US-based entrepreneurs. Fieldwork ran from 22 to 28 September. Respondents were founders or owners in growth mode. The survey excluded freelancers and sole traders, and every business had traded for at least two years.

The survey found that entrepreneurs at growing businesses spend an average of 36% of their work week on administrative tasks. The average work week in the same survey was 45.5 hours. Also, 29% of respondents worked more than 50 hours a week.

A more recent figure comes from Prialto's 2025 Executive Productivity Report. It surveyed 508 business leaders, founders, and high-performing executives. It puts admin work at 20 to 30% of their time. It also says over half of executives spend 30% or more of their day on busywork, and it estimates 8 to 12 hours a week on non-specialized work.

The Prialto report has gaps. It doesn't describe how it recruited people, how it worded its questions, or what its margin of error is. Its own text says data gathering was planned for late 2024 and moved to spring 2025. It also gives the admin share two ways, 20 to 30% in one place and nearly one-third in another. Read it as a signal, not a measurement.

| Source | Who was surveyed | Admin share | Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time etc with Censuswide | 251 US entrepreneurs in growth mode | 36% of the work week | Fielded 22 to 28 September 2023 |
| Prialto Executive Productivity Report | 508 business leaders, founders, and executives | 20 to 30% of time | 2025 report; data gathered in spring 2025 |

What does that mean for you? At 45.5 hours a week, 36% is more than a third of your time. Your own week may run shorter. Track it for a few days anyway. You'll know your number, and that's more useful than an industry average.

## Set up Claude Desktop with a folder you choose

If you want an assistant that reads your documents, the official filesystem server for Claude Desktop is the simplest start. The setup is short. It has a few places where people get stuck, so go slowly.

1. **Make one work folder.** Name it something clear, like Founder Admin. Put in the files you want Claude to read, and nothing else.
2. **Open the config file.** Go to Claude > Settings > Developer > Edit Config. This edits claude_desktop_config.json in ~/Library/Application Support/Claude/.
3. **Add the filesystem server.** List only the folder from step 1 in the server's arguments. The official server only accesses the directories you list there.
4. **Quit completely.** Save the file, then quit Claude with ⌘ Q and reopen it. Closing the window doesn't reload the config.
5. **Check that the tools loaded.** Click Add files, connectors, and more at the bottom left of the input box.
6. **Run a harmless test.** Ask Claude to list the files in Founder Admin. Claude asks for approval before each file operation. Read each prompt before you approve it.
7. **Read the logs if tools don't appear.** Open ~/Library/Logs/Claude and look for an mcp-server-SERVERNAME.log file. Its stderr output usually names the problem.

Menu names can change with app updates. The MCP docs page is versioned, and I did not confirm that it matches the newest Claude Desktop screens. If a menu name differs, look for the Developer section in the Settings window.

Local access has limits too. Projects tied to a local folder support Cowork sessions in the desktop app only. A cloud session can reach local folders only while the desktop app is open on that computer. If you close your laptop, the folder goes out of reach.

## A worked example: one founder's workday

Here is a plausible day. The times are examples, not data from a study. Each line shows one request and one check.

- **9:00.** A long investor thread needs an answer. Ask for a summary first. Then dictate your reply with fn: "Thanks, Thursday works for me. I'll send the deck before the call." Read the draft, then send.
- **10:30.** Ask for background research: "Compare how five competitors price annual plans." Keep working. Open the report when it's ready, and check the sources it cites.
- **12:00.** Say "Lunch with Sam on Thursday at one." Open Calendar and confirm the time zone before you move on.
- **14:00.** You're stuck in a billing dashboard. Ask where to issue a refund. The assistant points at the button, and you click it.
- **17:00.** "Remind me to follow up with the investors Monday morning." Check that the reminder landed in Reminders at the right time.

Notice what didn't happen. The assistant didn't send the investor reply. It didn't decide whether to offer a discount. It didn't click the refund button for you. Each of those stayed with you.

Dictation errors show up in drafts, so read every draft. Look hardest at names, dates, and amounts. Those are the words you'll miss when you're in a hurry. If you're going to be away, set your auto reply before you leave. [How to set up out of office in Gmail on Mac](/how-to/set-up-an-out-of-office-reply-in-gmail) covers the steps. If you send from Outlook, you can also hold a message before it leaves. [How to delay delivery of an email in Outlook on Mac](/how-to/delay-delivery-of-an-email-in-outlook) shows how.

## Common mistakes when you hand admin to an AI

- **Sending without reading.** A fast draft is still a draft. Check names, dates, and figures every time.
- **Trusting a summary you haven't checked.** Open the original thread for anything that matters, like terms, pricing, or a commitment.
- **Asking for a decision.** "Should I take this meeting?" gets you tradeoffs, not an answer. Ask for the options, then decide.
- **Giving access to too much.** One work folder is enough for most founder admin. A whole home folder is too much.
- **Waiting to start.** The fastest way to learn what helps is one real week of use, not a month of reading setup guides.

## Privacy checks before your data goes anywhere

Founders handle sensitive material: term sheets, customer lists, and cap tables. Before you give any tool access, check three things.

First, know when the tool listens or looks. Lazur listens and looks at the screen only while you hold its shortcut. Audio and the screenshot go to AI providers so they can answer you, and they aren't stored. Lazur says its content is never used to train AI models. Confirm that against the current privacy text before you rely on it.

Second, know which folders can be read. The official filesystem server reads only the directories in its arguments. Claude asks before each file operation. Local folder access works only while the desktop app is open.

Third, read what happens to recordings. Thout turns recorded conversations into notes, decisions, and next steps. Before you record an investor call, check its privacy information on the App Store listing and on the developer's own site. Ask where the recording goes and how long it stays there.

## Start with one week

Pick one week of real work. Track every admin task you do on one day, then sort each one into an answer, a draft, or an action. Use your assistant for answers and drafts first. Add actions once you trust the output.

1. Write down the admin tasks from a normal morning.
2. Sort each task into an answer, a draft, or an action.
3. Use your assistant for answers and drafts for a full week.
4. Read every draft before you send it.
5. Add reminders and calendar events after a few days of clean results.

Keep the judgment calls on your own list. Your assistant can prepare the material. You still decide what to send, and to whom.
