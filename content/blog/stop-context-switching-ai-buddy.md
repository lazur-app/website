The real cost of a quick question isn't the question. It's everything around it: leave the window, find the tab, explain the situation, copy the answer back, find your place. Do that thirty times a day and the deep work you meant to do quietly disappears.

An AI buddy fixes this by answering **where you already are**.

## The hidden tax of "I'll just ask ChatGPT"

Picture a normal afternoon:

- An error appears in your terminal. You copy it, open a tab, paste, read, come back.
- A colleague asks if Thursday works. You check your calendar in another app, then reply.
- You need a setting in an app you rarely use. You search, open a help article, skim, go back, hunt for the menu.
- You remember to send an invoice later. You open Reminders, type it, close it.

Each one feels like ten seconds. Each one also costs the thread you were holding. Researchers who study interruptions consistently find that resuming work after a switch takes much longer than the switch itself.

## Answering in place

With an AI buddy like Lazur, the same afternoon looks different:

- **The error:** hold ⌥ ⌘, ask "what does this mean?" Lazur sees the error on screen and explains it out loud. Your eyes never leave the editor.
- **The reply:** hold fn, say "Thursday works for me, let's do three." Clean text lands in the reply box.
- **The setting:** ask "where do I turn off notifications for this?" Lazur circles the exact menu item, then the next one.
- **The invoice:** "remind me to send the invoice at five." Done, in Apple Reminders.

You stay in the same window the whole time. That's the point.

## It's not about speed, it's about focus

Talking is faster than typing, but the bigger win is that **small things stop being interruptions**. When asking costs nothing, you stop batching questions and stop putting off tiny tasks, and the background noise of "I should look that up" goes away.

## Three habits that compound

1. **Ask instead of searching.** If it's on your screen, ask about it where it is.
2. **Say small tasks out loud as they come.** Reminders, timers and calendar events take one sentence.
3. **Hand off the long stuff.** Research and reading can run as background jobs while you keep going.
