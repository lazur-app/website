The best MacBook notch apps turn the camera cutout into an interactive hub for your daily tasks. These tools help you reclaim space and add functional widgets to your menu bar area. ## Understanding the MacBook Notch Hardware The notch on your MacBook Pro is not a software element. It is a physical cutout that houses the FaceTime camera and ambient light sensor. Apple designed this area as bonus screen space above the standard 16:10 aspect ratio. You are not losing any desktop area compared to older models without the notch. On a 16-inch MacBook Pro, the notch measures 220 by 38 points. The 14-inch model features a slightly smaller notch at 185 by 32 points. Both versions occupy roughly 12 percent of your screen width. Because there is no official Apple API for the notch, third-party developers use creative workarounds. They draw custom windows that expand when you hover or click near the top center. This provides a user experience similar to the Dynamic Island on iPhone. ## Top Notch Apps for 2026 If you want to customize your menu bar, several apps stand out. Boring Notch is a free, open-source project. It offers music controls, a calendar, and a file shelf. It is a great starting point if you want basic functionality without a subscription. NotchNook is another popular choice. It is highly polished and includes a file tray and an AirDrop zone. If you need a more professional feel, Alcove offers the closest experience to Apple native animations. It includes support for Live Activities, which makes it feel like part of the operating system. For power users, Crest combines multiple tools. It handles passwords with Touch ID and includes 2FA code auto-fill. It even supports coding agents like Claude for quick snippets. If you simply want the notch to disappear, TopNotch is your best bet. It darkens the menu bar area to blend the cutout into the background. ## How to Manage Menu Bar Clutter Before installing third-party apps, try cleaning up your existing menu bar. Apple allows you to rearrange system icons by holding the Command key. Click and drag the Wi-Fi, battery, or volume icons to move them away from the center. This prevents them from being hidden by the notch. If you have too many icons, use a manager app. Bartender 5 is the industry standard for hiding and collapsing menu items. For a free alternative, look at Ice. It is open-source and handles icon hiding with a clean, simple interface. These tools ensure your critical information stays visible while your less important icons stay tucked away. ## Handling App Conflicts with the Notch Sometimes, an older application might have its menu items obscured by the notch. You can fix this with a built-in macOS feature. First, quit the application completely. Open Finder and navigate to your Applications folder. Right-click the app icon and select Get Info. Check the box labeled Scale to fit below built-in camera. When you relaunch the app, macOS will shrink the display area to sit entirely below the notch. This is a life-saver for legacy software that was not updated for the current hardware. ## Faster with Lazur If you find yourself constantly reaching for the mouse to adjust settings or find files, try a different approach. They remain idle until you trigger them with a hover or a click. This keeps battery and memory usage low. However, you should always verify the permissions of any app you install. If an app requests Accessibility or Screen Recording access, make sure you trust the developer. Always check the official website for pricing and feature lists before you install. Some free, open-source tools might not be notarized by Apple. You may need to manually allow them in System Settings > Privacy & Security. Stick to reputable sources to ensure your data stays private. If you are worried about clipboard managers or password fillers, keep your data local to your machine. Always audit your permission list every few months to see which apps are still active. ## Choosing the Right Tool for Your Needs The best MacBook notch apps depend on how you work. If you need a utility hub, look at Crest or NotchNook. If you prefer a minimalist look, TopNotch is perfect. Do not feel pressured to install every app you see. Start with one and see if it actually saves you time. If you find yourself ignoring the widget after a week, it is probably not the right tool for you. Focus on apps that solve a specific problem, like managing files or controlling your music. Your goal is to make your Mac work for you, not to clutter your screen with more icons. Keep your menu bar clean, your shortcuts handy, and your workspace focused.
