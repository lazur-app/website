Wispr Flow and Aqua Voice are dictation apps that turn your speech into typed text on your Mac. Lazur is a Mac voice assistant that also dictates, so pick it when you want help beyond the typing.

This guide compares the three on price, free limits, platforms, speed, accuracy and privacy. It ends with a way to test them on your own work. All prices are as checked on 2026-11-07. Vendor pages change, so check them on the day you buy.

## What each app is built to do

Wispr Flow's pricing page lists two products. The first is dictation, which the page says runs on Mac, Windows, iOS and Android. The second is Notetaker, which runs on Mac and Windows only. Mobile for Notetaker is described as coming later. If you only need dictation, read the plan that includes it, not the headline.

Aqua Voice's pricing page has four tiers. Free gives 1,000 words with no credit card. Pro gives unlimited words, custom instructions and a personal dictionary. Max adds Realtime Mode, voice commands and early access. Business is for teams and starts at two seats.

Lazur works from the Mac notch. Hold ⌥ ⌘ to ask about anything on screen, and it answers out loud. Ask where something is and it draws a circle and a label around it. Ask how to do something and it guides you one click at a time, waiting for each click. Hold fn to dictate clean text at the cursor. It also handles timers, Apple Reminders, Calendar events, notes, music, opening apps and Shortcuts. Research jobs run in its own Chrome tabs and come back with a summary.

That difference matters. A dictation app's job ends when the words appear. An assistant keeps going. You can ask it what a button does, or have it draft a reply you then check. Lazur does not click buttons or operate other apps for you.

## Prices in 2026: what each plan costs per year

Wispr Flow Pro costs $15 per user per month on monthly billing. On annual billing it costs $12 per user per month. The pricing page labels annual billing a 20% discount. On annual billing, that adds up to $144 a year. Its Growth tier is $23 or $33 per user per month monthly, or $18 or $26 on annual billing.

Aqua Voice Pro costs $12 per month monthly, or $10 per month annual. On annual billing, that is $120 a year. Max is $24 monthly or $19 annual. Business is $24 per seat monthly or $19 per seat annual, with a two-seat minimum. Aqua also offers a 70% student discount on Pro and Max with a school email. Pro then costs $3.60 monthly or $3 annual, and Max costs $7.20 monthly or $5.70 annual.

Lazur Pro costs $18 per month, or $15 per month billed annually, which is $180 a year. It includes 150 AI actions and unlimited dictation. Power costs $120 per month, or $96 per month billed annually. It includes 1,000 AI actions and unlimited dictation.

Here is a simple check. Take the entry paid tier of each on annual billing for one year. The totals are $144 for Wispr Flow Pro, $120 for Aqua Voice Pro and $180 for Lazur Pro. Lazur's price also covers the assistant side. If you only dictate, the extra $60 a year over Aqua is hard to justify. If you also ask questions and get tasks done, it may not be.

## Free plans and the point where each one stops you

Free plans sound alike. They stop you in different ways.

Wispr Flow's free plan has a weekly word cap. Its pricing page says "limited words per week." Its help article says non-paying desktop users are blocked after they exceed the cap. Neither page gives a number. The same help article says desktop sessions warn at 19 minutes and stop at 20 minutes. Android sessions have a 5-minute limit.

So the 20-minute stop often matters more than the weekly cap. If you dictate a long draft in one sitting, you will hit the warning first. Break long drafts into sections and dictate each one separately.

Aqua Voice's free plan gives 1,000 words one time, with no credit card required. "One time" means the allowance does not refill each week. When it runs out, you need Pro to keep going.

Lazur gives a 3-day trial of Pro, with no card. The trial starts the first time you open the app, not when you install it. After the trial, you choose a paid plan. Use the trial on a real work week, not a quiet weekend. A slow Saturday tells you almost nothing about Monday.

## Platforms: which one runs where you work

If you work only on a Mac, all three can run for you. The list changes fast if you use a Windows PC or an iPhone.

Wispr Flow lists dictation on Mac, Windows, iOS and Android. Aqua Voice lists Mac, Windows, iOS and Android via Google Play. Lazur runs on macOS, on both Apple silicon and Intel Macs. Windows is planned, not available.

So if your team mixes Macs and Windows PCs this year, Lazur is not an option. Choose Wispr Flow or Aqua Voice. If you work on a Mac, Lazur is worth a trial. If you type on an iPhone and want the same tool there, Lazur does not offer that.

## How much faster is speaking than typing?

The best public measure here comes from a Stanford study by Ruan, Wobbrock, Liou, Ng and Landay. It compared English speech and keyboard entry for short messages on touchscreen phones. Speech averaged 153 words per minute. The touchscreen keyboard averaged 52 words per minute. That makes speech 2.93 times faster.

That study matters, but it has limits. It tested phones and short messages. It did not test Wispr Flow, Aqua Voice or Lazur. Treat it as a useful signal, not a verdict on your Mac.

Here is what the study rates mean for a 300-word email. At 153 words per minute, speech takes about 2 minutes. At 52 words per minute, typing takes about 6 minutes. Those figures are our arithmetic from the study rates. Your speed will differ. Run your own numbers with the [speaking vs typing test](/tools/speaking-vs-typing-test).

## Accuracy: what is left after the paste

Speed is only half the story. The same study measured errors. During entry, speech had a corrected error rate of 5.30%. The keyboard had 11.22%. Speech fixed more of its own mistakes while people spoke.

The final text tells a different story. Speech left 1.30% uncorrected errors, against 0.79% for typing. Speech was slightly more likely to leave a wrong word in what you sent.

Read a dictated message before you send it. Watch for names, numbers, product names and anything with a currency sign. You can test how your own voice and mic perform with the [dictation accuracy checker](/tools/dictation-accuracy-checker).

A personal dictionary helps with product names. Aqua Voice's Pro plan includes one. Lazur includes a personal dictionary and snippets, plus clipboard slots for text you repeat. The Wispr Flow pages in this comparison do not describe a dictionary feature.

Also remember the source. These error rates come from the Stanford study, not from these three apps. Do not read them as a grade for any product.

## Privacy: what to check before you speak

Dictation sends your voice somewhere. Before you choose, know where.

Lazur says it listens and looks at the screen only while you hold the shortcut. When you ask a question, the audio and the screenshot go to AI providers to answer them. Lazur says they are not stored. It also says content is never used to train AI models. Its memory of what you asked and did stays on your Mac.

The Wispr Flow and Aqua Voice sources in this comparison cover price and platforms. They do not settle data handling. Read each vendor's privacy policy. Find out whether your audio is processed on your Mac or sent to a server. Do not assume either answer. If you handle client or patient data, check this before you speak a single sentence.

## Which app fits which job

Pick by the job you do every day, not by the feature list.

- You want fast dictation on a Mac at the lowest annual price. Aqua Voice Pro is the lowest of the three entry prices at $120 a year on annual billing. Test its 1,000-word free plan first.
- You work on Windows, or you type on an iPhone too. Choose Wispr Flow or Aqua Voice. Lazur does not run on those yet.
- You dictate, and you also ask what a button does or want a reminder set. Lazur covers both in one tool.
- You dictate long drafts in one sitting. Check the 20-minute desktop stop on Wispr Flow's free plan before you decide.

## A one-week test you can run on your Mac

Do not pick from a checklist. Run the same work through each app.

1. Choose five messages you send every week. Include a Slack reply, a Gmail follow-up, a prompt for a coding tool, a note full of jargon and a reply to a customer.
2. Start a timer when you begin speaking. Stop it when the text is ready to send, including every fix you make.
3. Write two numbers for each message: the time and the number of fixes.
4. Run the same five on each app's free plan or trial. Use the same mic and the same room.
5. Note every limit you hit. Wispr Flow warns at 19 minutes on desktop. Aqua's 1,000 words run out. Lazur's trial ends after 3 days.
6. Compare the fix counts first, then the times. A fast draft that needs ten fixes costs you more than a slower one with none.

Keep the app that needs the fewest fixes at a reasonable time. If two apps tie, the cheaper annual price wins.

## Mistakes people make when comparing these apps

- Comparing only the free tier. A free plan shows you the limit, not the value.
- Trusting a blog's price table without checking it. Several third-party sites disagree with the vendor pages. Check the vendor page on the day you buy.
- Judging a dictation app and an assistant on the same scale. A dictation app is judged on text. An assistant is judged on tasks. Lazur is not a like-for-like rival of Wispr Flow.
- Forgetting the platform. A Windows teammate or an iPhone habit can settle the question before you test anything.
- Skipping the read-through. The Stanford study found speech leaves slightly more uncorrected errors in the final text.
- Expecting any of these to work inside other apps for you. Lazur does not click buttons or operate other apps.
