When dictation on Mac is not working, the primary cause is often a conflict with Voice Control or a lack of microphone permissions for the specific application. You can resolve most issues by checking your input source in System Settings and ensuring the dictation feature is enabled for your current language. 

## Why Dictation Fails on macOS

Many users assume that if the microphone works in a video call, it must work for dictation. This is not always the case. macOS treats system-wide Dictation and app-specific microphone access as distinct features. If you are struggling with dictation on Mac not working, start by looking at your accessibility settings. 

Voice Control is the most common culprit. It is a powerful accessibility tool that allows you to control your entire computer with your voice. Because it needs total control over your audio input, it disables the standard Dictation service. If you have Voice Control turned on, your Mac will ignore your attempts to use the standard dictation shortcut. 

## Check Your Microphone Permissions

Privacy settings are another frequent point of failure. Even if your microphone is active, macOS might prevent an app from receiving text input from the speech engine. Go to System Settings > Privacy & Security > Microphone. Look through the list of apps. If you are trying to dictate into a specific app like Slack or a browser, ensure the toggle next to that app is turned on. 

If the app is not in the list, it might not have requested access yet. Sometimes, restarting the application forces it to trigger a new permission request. If you still see the dictation icon but nothing appears on screen, the permission might be blocked at the system level. 

## Verify Your Input Source

Sometimes the system defaults to the wrong microphone. If you have a webcam with a built-in mic or an external audio interface, your Mac might be trying to listen to a device that is not currently active or is muted. 

1. Open System Settings. 
2. Navigate to Sound. 
3. Select the Input tab. 
4. Ensure your preferred microphone is highlighted. 

Speak into the microphone. You should see the input level meter jump. If the meter remains flat, your Mac is not receiving any audio signal. This confirms the issue is hardware or driver related rather than a software bug. 

## The Role of Shortcuts and Key Conflicts

Dictation on Mac relies on specific keyboard shortcuts. By default, this is often set to pressing the Fn key twice or holding the Microphone key. If you use utilities like Raycast, BetterTouchTool, or other automation software, they might be intercepting these keystrokes before they reach the system. 

Check your shortcut settings in System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation. Try changing the shortcut to something unique, like ⌥ ⌘ D. If the feature works with a new shortcut, you have a conflict with another app. 

## Restarting the Speech Daemon

If you have verified your settings and the feature still fails, the background process responsible for speech recognition may have hung. macOS uses a process called corespeechd to handle voice-to-text conversion. You can force this process to restart without rebooting your entire computer. 

Open the Terminal app. Type the following command and press Enter: `killall corespeechd`. This command stops the current instance of the speech engine. The system will automatically launch a fresh version of the process. This fix often resolves "ghost" issues where the dictation icon appears but the system refuses to process your audio.

## Understanding the 30-Second Limit

Apple designed the native dictation feature for short bursts of text. It has an architectural limit of about 30 seconds of silence. If you pause to think, the system will often stop listening entirely. This is why it feels like the feature is broken when you are trying to compose a long email or a report. 

If you find yourself constantly restarting the dictation session, you are hitting this design limit. It is not a bug, but it is a major friction point for power users. This is where dedicated tools become helpful for maintaining your flow. 

## Environment and Background Noise

Background noise is the silent enemy of voice recognition. Even with modern noise cancellation, loud environments can reduce recognition accuracy by up to 40 percent. If your Mac is struggling to turn your words into text, try moving to a quieter space or using a headset with a directional microphone. 

If you must work in a noisy office, consider using a directional mic that focuses on your voice. This provides the speech engine with a much cleaner signal, which reduces errors and prevents the system from giving up on your input. 

## When to Create a New User Account

If none of the above steps work, your user profile might have corrupted preference files. Create a new user account in System Settings > Users & Groups. Log in to this new account and test dictation there. If it works perfectly, the problem is isolated to your main user account. This means you likely have a conflicting background utility or a corrupted configuration file that needs to be reset. 

## Final Steps for Persistent Issues

If you have tried everything and dictation on Mac is still not working, consider checking for macOS updates. Sometimes, system-level bugs are patched in minor updates. You can also try booting your Mac into Safe Mode to see if a third-party app is causing the conflict. If it works in Safe Mode, you know for sure that a background utility is the problem. 

Remember to check your language settings as well. If you have multiple languages installed, the system might be trying to interpret your speech using the wrong model. Ensure the primary language in your Dictation settings matches the language you are speaking.
