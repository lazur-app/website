Type to Speak is the text box inside Apple's Live Speech feature. Turn on Live Speech in System Settings, type your reply in the box, and press Return, and your Mac says it out loud.

This guide covers the exact menu path, what your Mac needs before the feature shows up, how to pick a voice, how to save phrases you repeat, and what to check when the box does not appear. If you already know what the feature does, skip to the steps.

## What Type to Speak does on a Mac

Live Speech is an accessibility feature from Apple. Apple describes it as a way to type text and have it spoken during phone and FaceTime calls and in-person conversations. Those are the situations it was built for. The Type to Speak field is the part where you do the typing.

Here is the detail that trips people up. Type to Speak does not put your words into the app you are using. If you type in the Live Speech window while Messages is open, the Messages conversation stays empty. Your Mac reads the phrase through its speech system and nothing else happens to the text.

That makes the feature useful in a few cases:

- A phone or FaceTime call where you cannot talk easily and still need to answer.
- A face-to-face conversation, such as a counter, a clinic, or a room where talking is hard.
- A phrase you want the Mac to say on demand, as long as you can type it.

If you need words to appear inside a document or a message, Type to Speak is the wrong tool. Dictation or ordinary typing fits that job. The mistakes section below covers this again, because it is the most common confusion.

## Check what your Mac needs first

Live Speech is not on every Mac. Apple's Live Speech page says that on a Mac, the feature needs Apple silicon. Intel Macs are not on that list, so you will not find the setting on them.

Apple's requirements also name macOS Sonoma or later. Apple's Mac help pages list macOS 27 Golden Gate, Tahoe 26, Sequoia 15, and Sonoma 14 in their version selector. To check your own chip and macOS version, click the Apple menu and choose About This Mac. The chip line tells you whether you have Apple silicon.

Apple's help pages do not say which macOS release their steps match. If your menus look different from the steps below, open System Settings and search for Live Speech. Search finds the setting even when the sidebar layout is different.

The last requirement is language. Apple says Live Speech is not available in every language. If your system language is not supported, you may not be able to use the feature, so check the language setting in Live Speech settings before you spend time on it.

## How to turn on Type to Speak on Mac

Apple's path is Apple menu > System Settings > Accessibility > Speech > Live Speech. Scroll down if you need to find Live Speech in the list.

Here are the steps in order:

1. Click the Apple menu in the top-left corner and choose System Settings.
2. Click Accessibility in the sidebar, then click Speech.
3. Scroll down if needed and click Live Speech.
4. Switch on the Live Speech toggle.
5. The Live Speech window opens. Click the Type to Speak field.

Once the window is open, you can leave it on screen or hide it. The menu bar icon controls that, and we cover it later in this guide.

## Type a reply and have your Mac speak it

Type your words into the Type to Speak field. Then press Return, or click Speak. Your Mac says the phrase in the voice you picked.

Here is a worked example. A colleague asks a question on a FaceTime call, and your voice is not cooperating that morning. You click the Type to Speak field, type "Yes, I can hear you, go ahead," and press Return. The Mac speaks it. Nobody on the call sees your text, so they hear the voice only.

Three habits make this work better:

- Read the phrase before you press Return. Once it is spoken, you cannot take it back.
- Keep replies short. Short lines are easier to follow when they are spoken aloud, and they are quicker to type under pressure.
- Use the Recent list for phrases you already said. AbilityNet's guide for macOS 15 Sequoia says the Recent category stores typed phrases, and the same idea applies on current releases.

If pressing Return does nothing, click inside the Type to Speak field first. The cursor needs to sit in that box before the shortcut works.

## Choose a voice for Type to Speak

By default, Live Speech uses the system voice from your Read & Speak settings. You can change that. Click the Voice pop-up menu in the Live Speech window, or open Live Speech settings and pick a voice in the Voice option.

| Voice option | What you get | How to choose it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| System voice from Read & Speak | The voice you already use for Read & Speak | Default, nothing to change |
| Another system voice | A different built-in voice | Click the Voice pop-up menu |
| Personal Voice | A synthetic version of your own voice, after you record it | Create it first, then pick it from the Voice pop-up menu |

Personal Voice takes some effort. Apple's 2023 announcement says you create it by reading randomized text prompts until you have recorded 15 minutes of audio. Apple said you can create it on iPhone, iPad, or a Mac with Apple silicon. At that launch, Personal Voice was English only.

Apple has not said in the pages we checked whether that language limit has changed, so check the current options before you record. Record in a quiet room. Background noise makes the result sound worse, and you will have to start over if the recording is poor.

## Save phrases you use every day

Saved phrases are for lines you say often. Good examples include "Hi, thanks for calling" for a greeting, "I need a receipt" for a shop, and "Give me a moment, please" for a call where you need time.

To use a saved phrase, click the Saved Phrases icon in the Live Speech window, select the phrase, then click Speak or press Return. AbilityNet describes the same split: Recent holds phrases you typed, and Saved holds phrases you wrote in advance.

To add phrases, open Live Speech settings and use the Add button. To group them, use Add Category. Apple's settings list recent and saved phrase categories, so you can keep greetings, shopping, and call lines apart.

Keep each saved phrase to one idea. A paragraph saved as one entry is hard to scan when you need a single line quickly, and you will end up searching the list during the conversation.

## Show or hide the Live Speech window

Click the Live Speech icon in the menu bar to show or hide the window. Hiding the window does not turn Live Speech off. You turn the feature on and off in Live Speech settings.

Live Speech settings also include a font size control for the Live Speech window. Raise the font size if you need to read your typed text from across a counter or a table. The larger text is easier to check before you press Return.

## Why text to speech matters for so many people

Voice, speech, and language problems are common. The NIDCD's quick statistics say that 7.6% of U.S. adults, or 17.9 million people, report having had a problem with their voice. About 2 million Americans currently have aphasia, which affects how people use language. In 2012 survey data, about 1 in 14 children ages 3 to 17 had a disorder related to voice, speech, or language.

Those numbers do not mean Type to Speak is only for people with a condition. Anyone who has lost their voice for a day, or who cannot talk in a certain room, can use it. The feature is useful any time speaking is hard and typing is easy.

Apple's Personal Voice gives you a second option. It is useful if you want replies to sound like your own voice rather than a built-in one. Remember the 15 minutes of recording only applies if you choose to create it.

## Fix Type to Speak when it does not work

Most problems come down to one of a few causes. Check this table before you start over.

| Symptom | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| Live Speech is missing from Accessibility > Speech | Confirm you have an Apple silicon Mac and macOS Sonoma or later, then search System Settings for Live Speech |
| The Live Speech window does not appear | Click the Live Speech icon in the menu bar to show it |
| The voice sounds wrong | Open the Voice pop-up menu and pick another voice |
| Personal Voice is not in the list | Create it first, and check the language options, since Apple's 2023 launch was English only |
| A phrase you need is gone | Typed phrases go to Recent. Save the ones you reuse under Saved Phrases |
| Nothing is spoken | Check the volume and the output device in System Settings > Sound |
| Live Speech is not available for your language | Check the System speech language setting in Live Speech settings |

If none of these fixes the problem, open Live Speech settings and go through each option once. If the window still does not appear, turn Live Speech off and on again in System Settings, then check the menu bar icon.

## Mistakes people make with Type to Speak

The most common mistake is expecting your words to land in an app. They do not. Type to Speak only speaks. If you need text in a message, dictate it or type it normally.

The second mistake is trying the feature for the first time during a call. Set it up when you are calm. Speak one test phrase, check the volume, and save the phrases you know you will need.

The third mistake is saving everything in one long list. Use categories from the start. A simple split into greetings, shopping, and call lines saves you from scrolling when the other person is waiting.

The fourth mistake is assuming Personal Voice works in every language. Check the current list before you record, because Apple's 2023 announcement limited it to English.

The last mistake is trusting the default volume. A quiet Mac in a loud room is hard to follow. Turn the volume up before you use it in person.

## Type to Speak and Speak Selection are different tools

Type to Speak reads the words you write. Speak Selection reads words that already exist on your screen. You use Type to Speak when you need to say something new. You use Speak Selection when you want the Mac to read a paragraph, a message, or a web page you already have open.

If you want the steps for reading selected text, see [How to make Mac read text out loud on macOS: Speak selection and Option-Esc](/blog/mac-read-text-aloud-speak-selection). Use that guide for text on screen and this one for text you type.

## Test it once, then keep it ready

Set up Type to Speak before you need it. Turn on Live Speech, speak a test phrase, and check the voice. Then save two or three phrases you use often under Saved Phrases.

If you want to compare how fast you speak with how fast you type, try the [speaking vs typing test](/tools/speaking-vs-typing-test). It gives you a rough sense of which method suits the replies you write most.
