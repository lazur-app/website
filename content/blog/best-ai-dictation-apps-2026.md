Start with Apple Dictation if you write mostly in Notes, Mail, and Messages. It is free, it ships with macOS, and it has no length limit. If you write all day in Slack, Gmail, Notion, or a code editor, try a paid AI dictation app on its free plan or trial, because it cleans up text in those apps as you speak.

No app wins every case. The rest of this page shows what each tool does well, what it costs, and how to test them yourself.

## What an AI dictation app does that Mac dictation does not

Plain dictation turns speech into words. An AI dictation app tries to turn speech into text you can send. That usually means fixing punctuation, capital letters, names, and filler words. Some apps also rewrite a rambling sentence into a clean one, or change the tone for email, chat, or docs.

You will see three kinds of tool in search results.

- **Built-in dictation.** Apple Dictation on Mac and Windows voice typing come with the operating system. They are free and simple.
- **Transcription-first apps.** Wispr Flow is built to get words on screen fast, on Mac, Windows, and phones.
- **Whisper-based apps.** Superwhisper gives you access to Whisper models and runs on Mac, Windows, iOS, and Android.

The labels blur. With macOS 27 and Apple Intelligence, Apple says dictation uses an on-device model to improve spelling and punctuation. So read what each app says about processing, then test it yourself.

## Is dictation faster than typing? The numbers

Speech is faster. A study by Ruan and colleagues, posted on arXiv, measured English entry at 153 words per minute by speech and 52 by keyboard. That is 2.93 times faster.

The same abstract shows the catch. Speech produced fewer errors while people were entering text. But more errors survived into the final text, at 1.30% against 0.79% for the keyboard.

Two limits matter. The study used short messages on touchscreen phones, not long desktop documents. And corrections eat into the speed gain.

Here is a worked example. Suppose you need to send a 500-word email. At the study’s speech rate, the first pass takes about 3.3 minutes. At the keyboard rate, it takes about 9.6 minutes. Those figures are rough, because real drafts include pauses, rewrites, and fixes for names. Still, you can see why people switch.

Time the parts yourself. Start a timer when you begin dictating. Stop it when the text is clean, and note how long the fixes took. That total tells you whether an app saves time. A words-per-minute number does not.

## Apple Dictation: the free starting point on Mac

Turn it on in System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation. Then start it in one of three ways. Press the Microphone key, press the dictation shortcut you chose, or go to Edit > Start Dictation in the app you are using.

Apple’s guide says dictation has no time limit on length. That makes it useful for long notes. It also has one trap. Dictation stops on its own when it hears no speech for 30 seconds. If you pause to think in the middle of a paragraph, the session can end before you finish. Keep talking, or press the shortcut again.

With macOS 27 and Apple Intelligence, the on-device model works in English on Macs with an M3 chip or later and at least 12GB of unified memory. To check your memory, choose Apple menu > About This Mac.

Privacy is worth a look too. In Keyboard settings, you can see whether dictation is processed on your device and not sent to Siri servers. Audio is stored only if you choose Share Audio Recordings, and Apple may review a sample of stored audio. Check that setting before you dictate sensitive text.

Apple Dictation is a sensible default. Test it in the apps you use most, such as Slack or your code editor, before you rely on it. ## Wispr Flow: fast transcription with a free plan

Wispr Flow runs on Mac, Windows, iOS, and Android. Its free plan on Mac and Windows gives you a weekly allowance of 2,000 words, with a 5,000-word maximum. The allowance resets Sunday at 12 a.m. Pacific Time, according to its help center.

At 2,000 words a week, you get about 400 words a day across a five-day workweek. A heavy morning of Slack replies and email can use that up early in the week. Plan your dictation around the limit, or pay for Pro.

Pro costs $15 per user per month, or $144 per user per year. The help center puts the annual plan at $12 a month. Trials last 14 days by default, and referral trials run 30 days. After a trial ends, unpaid users get a three-day grace period. Check the pricing page before you buy, because plans change.

Wispr Flow’s release log also shows recent changes. On 9 October 2026, Flow began warning you right away when the microphone picks up no sound at the start of dictation. That saves time when a headset is muted. On 19 October 2026, it added a dark mode for desktop.

Wispr Flow is a reasonable pick if you want fast words on screen across several devices. Expect to do some light editing afterward.

## Superwhisper: a free plan with Whisper models

Superwhisper’s homepage lists a free plan at $0. It includes voice-to-text in any app, unlimited use of Whisper models, support for more than 100 languages, and meetings on Mac only. It runs on Mac, Windows, iOS, and Android.

The paid plans are harder to read. The official page shows a “$849” figure that looks like a display error. It also does not clearly list yearly or lifetime prices. Do not budget from a number you saw in a roundup. Check superwhisper.com for current pricing.

Superwhisper suits people who want to test Whisper models for free before they pay. Read the app’s privacy details before you dictate anything sensitive.

## Dragon Professional and Windows voice typing

Dragon Professional is a long-running dictation product, but it is built for Windows. A third-party price listing shows Dragon Professional v16 for Windows at $699.99 as a one-time purchase. Nuance’s own site showed only a contact button when we last checked it, so treat that figure as a reseller price. If you run Windows at work, Dragon may be worth a look. On a Mac, skip it.

Windows 11 has its own built-in option. Press Windows key + H to turn on voice typing. Microsoft’s help page lists three requirements: an internet connection, a working microphone, and the cursor in a text box. Voice typing uses online speech recognition powered by Azure Speech. Fluid dictation, the on-device option, works only on Copilot+ PCs and in English locales. To stop, say “Stop listening” or press the microphone button.

## Privacy checks before you dictate work text

Some dictation setups send your voice to a server. Check before you dictate client, legal, or medical text.

- **Apple Dictation.** Check Keyboard settings for on-device processing. Leave Share Audio Recordings off unless you want Apple to store audio.
- **Windows voice typing.** Uses online Azure Speech recognition, so your speech leaves the PC.
- **Wispr Flow and Superwhisper.** Read each privacy page for the current data path, storage, and training terms. Do not assume.

Privacy terms change, so check them on the day you sign up, not the day you read a roundup.

## Common mistakes when choosing a dictation app

1. **Choosing from a demo.** Demos use clean sentences. Test with your own speech, your team’s names, and the apps you use every day.
2. **Ignoring fix time.** A fast app that leaves a dozen errors can cost you the time it saved. Time the fixes, not just the speaking.
3. **Forgetting the silence timeout.** Apple Dictation stops after 30 seconds of silence. A long pause can end the session.
4. **Hitting a free cap mid-week.** Wispr Flow’s free plan resets weekly, so a heavy week runs out fast. Check your usage before a deadline.
5. **Buying lifetime access from an old price.** Confirm the current price on the vendor’s own site first.
6. **Skipping microphone permission.** If dictation does nothing, open System Settings > Privacy & Security > Microphone and turn on the app.

## A 20-minute test to pick your app

Use this test before you pay for anything.

1. Pick two candidate apps. Use each one’s free plan or trial.
2. Choose one real message you must write this week. Pick a draft of about 300 words with names and a link in it.
3. Dictate it in the app where you will send it. Use the shortcut you would use every day.
4. Time the dictation and the cleanup separately. Write both numbers down.
5. Repeat the test the next day with a different message. Check whether the second try is faster.

Here is how the numbers can look. App A took 3 minutes to speak and 4 minutes to fix, so 7 minutes in total. App B took 4 minutes to speak and 1 minute to fix, so 5 minutes in total. App B wins, even though it was slower to speak.
