An **AI buddy** is an assistant that lives on your computer, sees your screen when you ask, and answers out loud. It can point at the thing you need, and some versions also do small jobs inside the apps you already have open.

"AI buddy" is marketing shorthand, not a standard product category. Two apps use the phrase for themselves, and Microsoft sells the same basic idea as Copilot Vision. This guide covers what the term means, how an AI buddy differs from a chatbot, and what to check before you give one access to your screen.

## What an AI buddy actually is

The word "buddy" describes how the tool feels. It sits beside your work, the way a colleague would, instead of waiting in a separate browser tab.

Most AI buddies share four traits:

- They run on your computer, as a small app or a floating panel.
- You start them with a key or a click, so they are not listening all day.
- They look at your screen when you ask, not before.
- They answer with voice, text, or both. Some also draw a circle or label on the screen.

The two apps that use the name say much the same thing. heyclicky calls itself "an ai buddy that lives on your mac or windows pc." Its FAQ says it answers spoken questions, points at things on screen, and can run agents. Clippi says its assistant "appears next to your cursor." It speaks its replies and points at the relevant part of the interface.

Neither name is a standard term. Compare features, not labels, when you shop.

## How an AI buddy works, step by step

Here is a common case. You are in a spreadsheet, and one cell shows #DIV/0!. You want to know which cell causes it, and why.

1. Leave the spreadsheet as it is. Don't take a screenshot or open a new tab.
2. Hold the assistant's shortcut. Clippi, for example, uses push-to-talk with ctrl+option.
3. Ask out loud: "Why is this showing a divide by zero error?"
4. The assistant looks at the screen for that question.
5. It answers out loud. Ask "Which cell is empty?" and a good one points at it, rather than describing the grid in words.
6. Release the key and go back to work.

Notice what you skipped. You did not copy the formula, paste it into a chat window, or describe the sheet. The assistant saw the sheet for you. That is the whole idea in one example.

The answer itself matters too. A good answer is short, specific and tied to the screen. "The cell in row 4 divides by B4, and B4 is empty" is useful. A general lecture on spreadsheet errors is not.

## AI buddy vs. chatbot: what actually changes

The table below sets a chatbot in a browser tab against an AI buddy. The chatbot column describes a general pattern, not one product.

| | Chatbot in a browser tab | AI buddy |
|---|---|---|
| Where it lives | Its own window or tab | Over your apps, on your computer |
| How it sees your screen | You upload a screenshot or paste text | It looks when you ask |
| How you ask | Type, or speak into a box | Hold a key and speak |
| How it answers | Usually text | Voice, sometimes with a circle or label on screen |
| Points at buttons | Usually not | Some do, including heyclicky and Clippi |
| Does small tasks | Depends on the tool | Some do, such as reminders or timers |

The biggest gap is the copying. A chatbot can answer the same question. It just needs more of your time first. Each upload, paste and explanation pulls you out of the app you were using.

An AI buddy keeps the answer in your flow. You can keep typing in the app you were in, and the answer arrives where you are working.

## Where the AI buddy idea came from

The idea is older than the phrase. XDA describes Clippy, the Office helper from the late 1990s, as an "overly enthusiastic Office '97 helper." The modern version differs in one respect. It waits for you to ask.

Microsoft's current version is Copilot Vision on Windows. Microsoft says it "sees your screen and responds in real time" and gives guidance by voice. The company also says Vision "will not take actions on your PC for you." It is available in "over 170 markets worldwide" through the Copilot on Windows app.

ASUS OMNI takes a different route. XDA's hands-on review from March 2026 describes a desktop mascot that wanders the screen when idle. It can find settings, open apps, summarize documents, and transcribe voice recordings. It runs locally, but it works only on certain Asus devices. XDA says it needs AMD Ryzen AI 300 series or newer, or Intel processors with a capable NPU, and notes that "Not every Asus device will work."

If a search result shows a desk pet or a desk robot, that is a different category. This page does not cover it. The tools above share one idea, and they differ on how much they do. Copilot Vision explains and points. Some AI buddies also run everyday tasks. Pick based on what you want the assistant to touch.

## Can an AI buddy click buttons for you?

Usually it doesn't, and in most products that is by design. Microsoft says Vision "will not take actions on your PC for you." In practice, an AI buddy points at a button and waits for you to press it.

Some products go further. heyclicky says its assistant can run agents, and its free plan includes agent messages. Ask any vendor exactly what its assistant does with your mouse and keyboard, and where that work runs. The answer tells you how much to trust it with a live account, such as your email or your bank.

## AI buddy price and message limits

Most AI buddies have free tiers, and free tiers cap usage. Check the pricing pages before you buy, because prices change.

On its pricing page when we checked on November 7, 2026, heyclicky listed a free plan at $0. It includes 25 talk messages and 25 agent messages a month. Its Pro plan was $20 a month, with unlimited talk and dictation and 150 agent messages. Its Max plan was $100 a month, with 1,000 agent messages.

Clippi listed a free plan at $0 with 20 queries a month. Its Personal plan was $4.99 a month with 200 queries. Its Professional plan was $9.99 a month with 500 queries.

Do the math before you commit. Suppose you ask 10 questions on each workday. A cap of 25 messages a month lasts about two and a half workdays. That is not a plan for a heavy week.

Platform limits matter as much as price. heyclicky's Mac version needs macOS Sonoma 14.2 or higher. Its Windows page says it runs on Windows 10 and Windows 11, but its download wording differs, so confirm before you install. Clippi's desktop app is macOS only, with Windows listed as "Coming Soon." Microsoft's Copilot Vision is a Windows feature. Microsoft says a personal Microsoft account adds more features, including Copilot Vision, and that sign-in is not required to start.

## AI buddy privacy: the question most people skip

Privacy is the first worry for most people, and it should be. Pew Research Center surveyed 5,119 U.S. adults from February 17 to 23, 2026. Roughly seven in ten, or 71%, expect AI to make their personal information less secure. Only 3% expect more security. Another 10% expect not much difference, and 16% are not sure. About half of U.S. adults, or 49%, now report using AI chatbots.

Pew asked about AI in general, not screen assistants. Treat those numbers as the mood of the country, not a verdict on any one app.

Check four things before you turn an AI buddy on:

1. **When does it look?** heyclicky's Windows page says "we never look at your screen unless you press the hotkey." Clippi's page does not explain how it handles screenshots, so ask before you rely on it.
2. **What does it keep?** heyclicky says "screenshots are never stored," but that "basic text summaries" are kept for context. Its FAQ says you can delete your account and data in settings.
3. **Where do requests go?** An assistant that answers with a cloud model sends your question, and often a screenshot, to that model. Find out what the provider keeps.
4. **Does it run locally?** ASUS OMNI runs locally, which keeps more on the device. The trade-off is that it works only on certain Asus hardware.

A common mistake is reading "private" as "nothing leaves my computer." Those are different promises. Read the policy, and look for plain words about screenshots, audio and retention.

## Mistakes people make with an AI buddy

- **Asking vague questions.** "Help with this" gets a vague answer. Name the app and the goal: "How do I shrink this PDF in Preview?"
- **Expecting it to do the work.** Pointing and explaining is often the whole job. Let it walk you through, then press the button yourself.
- **Forgetting the monthly cap.** Check your plan's limit before a heavy week, not on Thursday afternoon.
- **Trusting one answer on money, health or legal questions.** It sees one screen. It may miss the context in your inbox or your contract. Check the source.
- **Skipping the privacy page.** Five minutes now costs less than a surprise later.

## Who gets the most out of an AI buddy

- **People learning new software.** A guide that points at each control often beats a long video.
- **Students reading dense material.** Ask about the paragraph on screen, not the whole chapter. See the [AI study buddy for students](/for/students) page.
- **Developers.** Ask about an error without copying a long log into a chat window.
- **People who type all day.** Hold a key, talk, and let the text land at your cursor.
- **Anyone with many small requests.** Reminders, calendar entries and quick lookups add up across a day.

An AI buddy is not for everyone. If you rarely use AI, a chatbot tab may be enough. If you dislike speaking near your desk, a text tool may suit you better.

## A one-week test before you pay

Run a simple trial before you commit money.

1. Pick one task that repeats three or more times a week.
2. Write the question you would ask before you open the assistant.
3. Use the assistant for that task, and only that task, for one full workweek.
4. Note which answers were right on the first try, and which needed a follow-up.
5. Check how much of your monthly limit you used. Then decide whether the plan fits.

## How to choose an AI buddy in 2026

Use this checklist:

- **Platform.** Mac, Windows, or both. Check the minimum macOS version.
- **Trigger.** Hold-to-talk is quick for short questions. A click may suit you better in a shared room.
- **Pointing.** Does it circle or label things on screen, or only describe them?
- **Limits.** Monthly caps for talk, agent use and queries.
- **Privacy.** When it looks, what it keeps, and who processes the request.
- **Price.** Check the pricing page on the day you buy.
