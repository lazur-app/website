Pick Aqua Voice if you want fast, accurate dictation across a Mac, a Windows PC, and a phone. Pick Lazur if you want a Mac voice assistant that sees your screen and turns a rough spoken message into a reply you can send.

Both apps turn up on lists of Wispr Flow alternatives. They do different jobs, though. Aqua is a dictation app. Lazur is an assistant that lives in the notch at the top of your Mac. If you only compare words per minute, you miss most of the difference. This guide covers platforms, prices, accuracy claims, privacy, and the daily work each app handles best. For a three-way view, read [Wispr Flow vs Aqua Voice vs Lazur](/blog/wispr-flow-vs-aqua-voice-vs-lazur). For more Aqua options, see [Aqua Voice alternatives for Mac](/alternatives/aqua-voice).

## Aqua Voice vs Lazur at a glance

The table shows where the two products start to differ. Read it as a list of hard limits first and tradeoffs second.

| | Aqua Voice | Lazur |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | Dictation app with AI polish | Voice assistant that lives in the notch |
| Platforms | Mac, Windows, iPhone, Android | macOS on Apple silicon and Intel. Windows is planned, not available |
| Sees your screen | Deep Context is listed on the free plan | Yes, while you hold ⌥ ⌘ |
| Free option | Free plan, 1,000 words, no credit card | 3-day Pro trial, no credit card |
| Entry paid plan | Pro, $12/month or $10/month billed annually | Pro, $18/month or $15/month billed annually |
| Usage limit | Unlimited words on Pro | Dictation unlimited on fair usage. 250 AI actions a month on Pro |

Need Windows this month? Lazur is out. Need the tool to point at a button? Aqua is out. Everything else is a tradeoff.

## Speed and accuracy: what the numbers show

Aqua leads with accuracy. On its comparison page, Aqua says its Avalon model got a key term right in 97.4% of clips. Whisper Large v3 got it right in 65.1%. That test is Aqua's own benchmark, called AISpeak-10. Treat it as a vendor claim until you run your own clips.

The same page quotes a 9to5Mac test that counted one error for Aqua and 17 for Apple Dictation. The page gives no date for that test. It also does not compare Aqua with Wispr Flow head to head. I did not find an independent comparison of Aqua and Lazur.

Lazur's pages do not publish a matching accuracy number, so there is no percentage to set beside Aqua's. Lazur is built around what happens after the words come out. It rewrites, replies, and reformats. Aqua's pages focus on the transcript itself.

Speed is the other half. A Stanford study first posted in 2016 (arXiv 1608.07323) found speech about three times faster than a keyboard for short English phone messages. Speech ran at 161.20 words a minute. The keyboard ran at 53.46. I read those figures through a secondary summary, because the primary PDF would not load. The study used short messages, so a day of Slack, email, and documents will look different.

Try this on your own Mac. Pick a real email you would send today. Dictate it in Aqua. Then dictate the same request in Lazur by holding fn. Start the timer at the first word. Stop it when the text is ready to paste. Then count what you changed. Most people time only the speaking. The fixing time is where the gap shows up.

## Pricing: what you pay each month

Both apps have a free start. The numbers below come from each company's own pricing page, checked in November 2026.

**Aqua Voice**

- Free: $0. 1,000 words. No credit card.
- Pro: $12/month, or $10/month billed annually. Unlimited words. Adds Custom Instructions and a Personal Dictionary.
- Max: $24/month, or $19/month billed annually. Adds Realtime Mode, Voice Commands, and early access.
- Business: $24 per seat a month, or $19 billed annually. Minimum two seats.
- Students with a school email get 70% off Pro and Max.

Aqua's own comparison page lists Pro at $8/month billed annually. Its pricing page says $10/month. Go with the pricing page, and check it again on the day you buy. For Lazur's side of the numbers, see the [Lazur pricing page](/pricing).

**Lazur**

- Trial: 3 days of full Pro. No card. The clock starts when you first open the app.
- Pro: $18/month, or $15/month billed annually. 250 AI actions a month.
- Power: $120/month, or $96/month billed annually. 1,000 AI actions a month.

Dictation is unlimited on both Lazur plans, with fair-usage limits set per plan. An AI action is one thing you ask Lazur to do. Dictation does not count toward it.

Put the annual prices side by side. Aqua Pro comes to $120 a year. Lazur Pro comes to $180. Aqua Max comes to $228. Lazur Power comes to $1,152. Pro against Pro, Aqua costs less. Those plans are also different products, so the gap buys you different things.

For a baseline, Wispr Flow's pricing page lists Pro at $15/user/month, or $12/user/month billed annually. Its free plan has limited words per week, and the page gives no number.

Two mistakes skew price comparisons. The first is putting a monthly price next to an annual one. The second is assuming both free options limit the same thing. Aqua's free plan caps words. Lazur Pro caps AI actions, and dictation sits outside that cap.

## Privacy: where your voice and screen go

Lazur's Command Mode post describes local speech-to-text plus Smart Rewrite. The app listens and looks at your screen only while you hold the shortcut. When you ask a question, the audio and the screenshot go to AI providers so Lazur can answer. Lazur says they are not stored, and that content is never used to train AI models.

I did not confirm where Aqua processes audio in this review. Read Aqua's privacy policy before you use it on client work. Check what it says about audio, transcripts, and screenshots. Ignore the tagline.

If you handle client data, write the answer down. List which app sends what, and where it goes. Then choose. A written list is easier to defend than a feeling.

## Which one fits your daily work

- **Replies in Slack and email.** Lazur has the stronger case here. It calls this Intent Mode. Lazur's blog says Intent Mode is what most dictation apps call command mode. Aqua's Voice Commands sit on the Max plan.
- **Long drafts and notes.** Aqua's Max plan adds Realtime Mode. Check Aqua's docs to see how it behaves in the apps you use. If most of your day is long-form dictation, test Aqua's Max plan first.
- **Windows or a phone in the same week.** Aqua. Lazur runs on macOS only.
- **Finding a setting or a button.** Lazur. Hold ⌥ ⌘ and ask where something is. Lazur draws a circle and a label around it. Ask how to do something, and it guides you one click at a time, waiting for each click. Lazur does not click for you. Aqua's pages describe dictation and text. They do not describe guidance on your screen.
- **Names, acronyms, and jargon.** Both apps have a personal dictionary. Aqua puts it on Pro. Lazur bundles it with snippets and clipboard slots.

## A worked example: a Slack reply, start to finish

Say a teammate asks whether Thursday works for a call. You want to answer, but you are not sure about the time yet. You speak it messily: “uh, reply to this and say Thursday works but I'm not sure about the time yet, keep it casual.”

A dictation app turns those words into text. Whether “keep it casual” stays in the reply as words, or gets treated as an instruction, depends on the tool. Test it on a real message before you trust it.

Lazur reads the thread on your screen and treats “reply to this” as a task. It writes a casual reply that says Thursday works and leaves the time open. You still read it before you send.

Before you send any AI-written text, check three things. Look at names, dates, and numbers. Check the tone against the person you are talking to. Confirm that the reply answers the message on screen, not an older one. Those checks take seconds, and they catch most of the errors.

## Edge cases that break a voice test

Most comparisons skip the hard cases. Those are the ones that will decide your choice.

**Background noise.** Test in the room where you actually work. A quiet desk tells you little about an open office or a shared kitchen. Run the same sentence twice, once with a fan or a call running nearby, and compare the output.

**Mixed languages.** Aqua's comparison page says it detects 49 languages automatically. Wispr Flow's pricing page says its speech-to-text supports 100+ languages on every plan. If you switch languages mid-sentence, test that exact habit. Do not trust a language count alone.

**Product names and acronyms.** Dictate the names of your own tools, clients, and teammates. Check how each app spells them. Then add the stubborn ones to the personal dictionary. Aqua puts that feature on Pro. Lazur includes a personal dictionary with snippets and clipboard slots.

**Requests with no message on screen.** Say “reply to this” with an empty window in front of you. Lazur looks at your screen, so a blank page gives it nothing to work from. Expect a clarifying answer, not a made-up reply. Check that behavior in your own setup.

**Long pauses.** Stop mid-thought for five seconds, then keep going. Some tools cut the recording at the pause and some keep it. Find out which one you have before a client call.

## Who should skip which

Skip Aqua if you want an assistant that sees your screen and points at things. Skip Lazur if you need Windows or an iPhone this quarter. Also skip Lazur if all you want is dictation. You pay for the assistant, not only for the typing.

## Mistakes that skew the comparison

- **Comparing a dictation app with an assistant.** Lazur is not a like-for-like dictation competitor. Decide what job you are hiring the app for first.
- **Trusting one benchmark.** A vendor test on vendor clips shows what the vendor chose. Run your own clips, with your names and jargon in them.
- **Judging on the free start.** Aqua's 1,000 words can run out fast in one busy day. Lazur's trial lasts three days. Test with real work, not a demo sentence.
- **Ignoring the action cap.** 250 AI actions a month is a fixed budget. Ask for help a few times an hour and it runs out.
- **Trusting one price.** Aqua publishes two different Pro prices. Check the pricing page on the day you buy.

## How to run a three-day test on your Mac

The Lazur trial runs three days, so use that window for a fair test. Keep the same three tasks in both apps. Otherwise you compare your best day against your worst one.

Start with microphone access. Open System Settings > Privacy & Security > Microphone and turn on both apps. Then run the three tasks in each app, in the same order, on the same day if you can.

Three numbers tell you most of what you need. Time to paste shows speed. Edits after paste show whether the text was finished. App switches show whether the tool fits how you work. Average the three across the test. Do not pick the best run.
