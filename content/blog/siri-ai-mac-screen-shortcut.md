On a Mac running macOS 27, press Shift-Command-6 (⇧⌘6) and drag a box around part of your screen. Siri AI then offers three choices: Ask Siri, Search Image, or a suggested action such as Look Up Nutrition.

That is the short version. The rest covers what the shortcut does in daily use, what your Mac needs, where Apple's pages leave gaps, and the limits Apple has not published.

## What Shift-Command-6 does with Siri AI on Mac

Shift-Command-6 is not a Siri-only key. In Apple's Mac Help guide for Siri AI, it is the selection step. It picks the part of the screen that Siri AI works from. Apple's wording is short. Press Command-Shift-6 to select a portion of the screen, then choose Ask Siri, Search Image, or an action such as Look Up Nutrition.

Once the box is drawn, you have three paths.

- **Ask Siri.** Ask a question about what is inside the box.
- **Search Image.** Search for the picture you selected.
- **A suggested action.** Siri AI offers actions that fit the content. Apple's example is Look Up Nutrition.

Apple's September 2026 announcement describes the wider idea. Siri AI can answer questions or take actions related to the content on your screen. On a Mac, Control-click an image, file, or piece of text to open system-wide options to ask Siri. Apple also describes a dedicated keyboard shortcut for Visual Intelligence. It lets you select part of the display and type a question. The announcement does not name that key. For Shift-Command-6, use Apple's Mac Help page as the source.

### Three examples you can try

Say you are reading a recipe page with a nutrition panel. Press ⇧⌘6, drag over the panel, and choose Look Up Nutrition. You get the suggested action without typing a word.

Now say an error dialog appears while you install an app. Press ⇧⌘6, drag over the message, and choose Ask Siri. Ask what the message means and what to do next. Pick the smallest area that holds the answer. A tight box keeps the question focused.

Last, say you see a product photo on a shopping page and want to search for it. Press ⇧⌘6, drag over the photo, and choose Search Image.

Use this table to pick the right option.

| What you want | Choose | Example selection |
|---|---|---|
| Numbers or facts on a page | Look Up Nutrition, when Siri AI offers it | A nutrition panel |
| An explanation of text on screen | Ask Siri | An error dialog |
| A search based on a picture | Search Image | A product photo |

## Shift-Command-6 next to the other screenshot shortcuts

Several Mac shortcuts select parts of the screen, so people mix them up. This table separates them.

| Shortcut | What it selects | What it does on macOS 27 |
|---|---|---|
| ⇧⌘6 | Part of the screen, by dragging | Gives you the Siri AI choices: Ask Siri, Search Image, or a suggested action |
| ⇧⌘Space | A whole window | Selects a window for the same Siri AI choices |
| ⇧⌘5 | Opens the Screenshot tools | Takes screenshots or recordings, not a Siri request |

Shift-Command-5 is the one most people already know. It opens the Screenshot tools for captures and recordings. Shift-Command-6 belongs to Siri AI on macOS 27. Use Shift-Command-Space when you want a whole window instead of a region.

The difference matters in practice. If you press ⇧⌘5 expecting a Siri answer, you get screenshot controls instead. If you press ⇧⌘6 expecting a full-window capture, you get a region you drew yourself. Read the label on each step before you act.

## What Shift-Command-6 did on Touch Bar Macs

Older MacBook Pro models with a Touch Bar used Shift-Command-6 for something else. It captured the Touch Bar. Apple's screenshot page still lists it that way. Apple's Siri AI page uses the same key to select part of the screen, so the two uses overlap on those machines.

MacRumors reported in October 2023 that Apple discontinued the 13-inch MacBook Pro that week. It was described as the final model with a Touch Bar. Apple's pages do not say which use wins on a Touch Bar Mac running macOS 27.

Test it on a plain web page before you need it. If the key captures the Touch Bar instead, use the Control-click route described above. It works on images, files, and text.

## What your Mac needs before the shortcut works

Apple's Siri AI guide lists three requirements. Your Mac must run macOS 27 or later. It must be a MacBook Neo with the A18 Pro chip or a Mac with an M1 chip or later. Your device language and your Siri language must both be supported.

The language rule catches people. A supported Mac language is not enough if Siri uses another one. Both settings need to match the supported list.

| Setup | Siri AI (beta) with Shift-Command-6 |
|---|---|
| MacBook Neo with A18 Pro on macOS 27 | Eligible, if both languages are supported |
| Mac with M1 or later on macOS 27 | Eligible, if both languages are supported |
| Intel Mac | Not eligible, because the chip requirement is M1 or later |
| Any Mac on macOS 26 or earlier | Not eligible, because the feature needs macOS 27 |

Apple lists six languages to start: English, French, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, and Spanish. Apple's September newsroom post said the non-English languages would follow the next month. Check Apple's current list before you assume your language works.

Region and age also matter. Siri AI is not available if your Apple Account region is mainland China. It is also not available to users under 13. Apple says it is not yet available in the EU on iOS, iPadOS, or watchOS. On macOS 27, EU Apple Accounts can use it if the language settings match.

## How to turn on Siri AI and try Shift-Command-6

Follow these steps once.

1. **Check your macOS version.** Choose Apple menu > About This Mac. You need macOS 27 or later.
2. **Check both languages.** Confirm your device language and your Siri language are on the supported list.
3. **Join the beta.** Go to Settings > Siri > Try Siri AI (Beta).
4. **Wait if you land on a waitlist.** Apple says a waitlist can apply during high demand, and wait times vary.
5. **Open the content.** Load the page, photo, or document, and keep it visible on screen.
6. **Press ⇧⌘6 and drag.** Drag across the part you want. Keep the box tight.
7. **Pick an option.** Choose Ask Siri, Search Image, or a suggested action.
8. **Check the answer.** Compare numbers, names, and steps with the screen before you act on them.

Start on a harmless page. A public article or a sample document makes a safe first test. Once the steps work there, move to your real work.

## A checklist before you rely on the shortcut

Run through this list once. It takes two minutes and saves you from guessing later.

- Confirm macOS 27 under Apple menu > About This Mac.
- Confirm the chip is an M1 or later, or that the Mac is a MacBook Neo.
- Confirm both the device language and the Siri language are supported.
- Confirm Try Siri AI (Beta) appears in Settings > Siri, and that you are not stuck on a waitlist.
- Run one test with ⇧⌘6 on a harmless page.
- If you have an older Touch Bar Mac, confirm which behavior the key triggers on your machine.

Once all six pass, the shortcut is ready for your routine. Keep the Control-click route in mind as a backup for images, files, and text.

## When Siri AI gives a weak answer

A vague answer usually comes from a vague selection or a vague question. Drag a box around the exact text or object you care about. Then ask a specific question. "What does this error mean?" works better when the box holds the full error message and the question names the step you are stuck on.

If the answer still misses, widen the box by a little and try again. Two small tries often beat one large selection. A large box mixes unrelated content into the question, and the answer drifts with it.

Pick the option that matches your goal. Search Image fits a picture you want to learn more about. Ask Siri fits a question about the text in the box. Look Up Nutrition fits a panel of numbers when Siri AI offers it.

Keep an eye on what the answer depends on. If it cites a number, check that number against the source on the page. Treat the answer as a starting point, not a verdict.

## Shift-Command-6 or asking Siri by voice

You can also ask Siri a question without using the shortcut. A spoken question on its own does not say which part of the screen you mean. The shortcut adds that target. You select the exact area first, then ask.

Use the shortcut for anything with small, specific detail. Good examples include one number in a table, one line of an error message, or one panel of a settings screen. Use a plain question for general topics that do not depend on what is on screen.

## Limits, waitlists, and what Apple has not published

Apple confirmed a daily usage limit for Siri AI, as MacRumors reported on September 9, 2026. Expanded access may cost a fee in the future. Apple's fine print says limits can vary by feature, request complexity, and system demand. When you reach a limit, the feature returns after a time-out period. Apple does not say how long that period lasts.

| Question | What Apple has published |
|---|---|
| Is there a daily limit? | Yes, Apple confirmed one |
| What is the numeric cap? | Not published |
| How long is the time-out? | Not published |
| What is the fee for expanded access? | Not published, only that a fee may apply in the future |
| When do limits start? | With the 2027 software releases, according to the fine print |

The launch date and the fine print point in different directions. Apple began the English beta on September 14, 2026. The fine print ties the limits to the 2027 software releases. Plan around a limit even if you do not hit one today.

Batch your questions when you can. Apple says limits can vary by request complexity, so a few clear questions in one sitting beat a long chain of small, vague ones. Do not build a daily routine that stops working the moment a time-out starts. Keep a manual method ready for the most important tasks.

## Mistakes people make with this shortcut

1. **Expecting a full-screen grab.** The key selects a portion of the screen. Use Shift-Command-Space when you want a whole window.
2. **Trusting the key on a Touch Bar Mac without a test.** It may capture the Touch Bar instead.
3. **Assuming an Intel Mac will get the feature.** Apple's rule is an M1 chip or later.
4. **Setting only the device language.** Siri's language must also be supported.
5. **Treating the answer as final.** Siri AI answers from what it sees. Check numbers and steps against the screen.
6. **Selecting sensitive content by habit.** A box can catch a name, an account number, or a password field. Draw the box around the one thing you need, and nothing else.

## Privacy checks before you use it on work screens

Siri AI processes the part of the screen you select. Before you use it on client files, HR records, or unreleased product screens, read Apple's privacy material for Siri AI. Then check your company's rules. The Apple pages this guide relies on do not say how long a selected area is stored, so this page cannot answer that question for you.
