The best Wispr Flow alternative depends on why you are leaving. Aqua Voice is the closest like-for-like app, Superwhisper suits offline work, and Apple Dictation is free and built in.

Wispr Flow is a capable dictation app. It types what you say into the app under your cursor, and it runs on more platforms than most of its rivals. People still go looking for a replacement, usually for one of four reasons: the free weekly cap, the cloud-only design, the price, or a job Wispr does not do. This guide sorts the main options by those reasons. Pick the two that fit yours, then test them side by side.

## What Wispr Flow does well, and why people switch

Wispr Flow runs on Mac, Windows, iPhone and Android. Its help center lists macOS 12 or later on Apple silicon or Intel, Windows 10 or 11 on x64, iOS 18.3 or later, and Android 13 or newer. iPad, Linux, Chromebooks and virtual machines are not supported.

The reasons for leaving tend to come down to four things.

The first is the cap. The free plan on Mac and Windows allows 2,000 words a week, with a 5,000-word weekly maximum. Flow slows down when you reach the allowance. At the maximum, dictation stops until the weekly reset or an upgrade.

The second is the cloud. Wispr says transcription always happens in the cloud, so there is no offline mode. If your audio cannot leave your Mac, Wispr is the wrong tool.

The third is the price. Pro costs $15 per user per month, or $144 per user per year. Annual billing saves 20%. Team plans cost more.

The fourth is change. Wispr moved all users to Canto, its first in-house speech model, on September 17, 2026. It also renamed Flow Notes to Scratchpad, and Smart Formatting became Auto Cleanup on the Style tab with four levels. If you learned the old menus, expect to relearn them.

None of these reasons means Wispr is a bad app. They do tell you what to test for.

## Is there a free Wispr Flow alternative, and what does each one cost?

Yes, but every free plan has a limit. The table shows the free and paid terms from each vendor's pricing page.

Monthly and yearly prices are easy to mix up. Wispr Pro at $144 a year works out to $12 a month. Willow Pro billed yearly at $12 per user per month also comes to $144 a year. Aqua Voice Pro at $10 a month billed annually comes to $120 a year. Typeless lists $12 billed yearly, so confirm whether that is a monthly figure before you compare.

Free limits are easy to hit. Say you dictate 500 words a day on five workdays. That is 2,500 words in a week. You pass Wispr's 2,000-word allowance before the week ends. Aqua's 1,000 words would last about two days at that pace.

Free plans are for testing. If dictation is part of your daily work, plan for a paid plan from the first week. Teams should also look at Wispr's Growth plan, which is dictation only at $23 per user per month or $216 per year.

## Aqua Voice: the closest like-for-like option

Aqua Voice is the closest like-for-like swap for Wispr Flow. Its pricing page leads with plans, not with a feature tour, so test it on your own messages before you commit.

Aqua's pricing page lists a SOC 2 Type II report, ISO 27001 certification, and GDPR and HIPAA compliance claims. It lists Zero Data Retention only under the Business plan. Business costs $24 or $19 per seat, and it needs a minimum of two seats. A solo user cannot pick that plan alone.

The page makes no offline claim. If offline use is a requirement, Aqua does not answer that question on its pricing page. For the fuller side-by-side, see [Aqua Voice alternatives for Mac in 2026](/alternatives/aqua-voice).

## Superwhisper: the best offline Wispr Flow alternative

Superwhisper's free tier includes voice-to-text in any app, support for more than 100 languages, and unlimited use of Whisper models. Its homepage says "Superwhisper works offline." The same page says offline models run well only on Apple silicon Macs. Intel Macs work best with cloud models.

Check your chip before you rely on offline mode. Open the Apple menu > About This Mac. If the chip line shows an Apple M-series chip, you have Apple silicon. If it shows an Intel processor, expect cloud models to work better.

Local models give you control, but you manage more settings. Superwhisper's Meetings feature is Mac only, according to its homepage, and the product offers a 30-day refund. For the wider picture, see [Superwhisper alternatives for Mac in 2026](/alternatives/superwhisper) or [Superwhisper vs Wispr Flow in 2026](/compare/superwhisper-vs-wispr-flow).

## Apple Dictation: the free option already on your Mac

Apple Dictation costs nothing and it is already on your Mac. Turn it on in System Settings > Keyboard. Start it with the Microphone key, the Dictation shortcut, or Edit > Start Dictation in most apps.

Apple's help page says some processing stays on the device. It does not say that dictation works offline. It also says dictation is not available in every language or region. Check your language before you rely on it.

Apple Dictation types what you say. It does not rewrite your sentences. For a quick reply or a note to yourself, that is often enough. For longer writing, expect to edit more. The full comparison is at [Apple Dictation alternatives for Mac in 2026](/alternatives/apple-dictation).

## Willow Voice and Typeless: simpler subscriptions

Willow Voice and Typeless both sell subscription voice typing with short pricing pages. Both are worth a one-week test.

Typeless lists macOS, Windows, Linux, iOS and Android. That matters if you move between a Mac and a phone. Its page gives no numeric free-tier word limit, so check the signup flow before you assume one.

Willow's free plan counts Scribe uses, not words. Count your uses for a full week before you judge the plan. For the Willow detail, see [Willow Voice alternatives for Mac in 2026](/alternatives/willow-voice).

## Wispr Flow alternatives for Windows and iPhone users

On Windows, Wispr needs Windows 10 or 11 on x64. Windows on ARM and Snapdragon are not supported. Check your PC before you buy.

Built-in Windows voice typing is not an offline fallback. Microsoft says Windows voice typing (Win+H) needs an internet connection, because it uses online speech recognition powered by Azure Speech services.

On iPhone, Wispr's free weekly allowance is 550 words, with a 750-word maximum. On Android it is 2,500 words, with a 3,000-word maximum. Those limits are much smaller than the Mac allowance on an iPhone, so heavy phone users should plan for a paid plan.

## Wispr Flow alternatives for privacy and offline use

Privacy questions and offline questions are different. Wispr can have a strict training setting and still send your audio to a cloud server.

Here is what Wispr says. Audio is sent to its servers over TLS 1.2 or later. Audio is deleted once it is transcribed, unless you turn on Dictation cloud storage. The setting "Improve the model for everyone" is on by default for individuals. Turn it off, and Wispr says nothing you say is used to train its models. On Growth and Enterprise plans, model training is off and locked for members, as of the October 2, 2026 change log.

I could not confirm how long Wispr keeps dictation cloud storage. Its data controls page was not part of this check. Ask Wispr before you rely on that detail.

Superwhisper's offline models stay on your Mac when you use them, but only on Macs that run them well. Apple says some processing stays on the device. Aqua Voice lists compliance claims but no offline use.

Ask each app two questions. Where is the speech processed? How long is it kept? Write the answers down next to your test results.

## Is speech really faster than typing?

A 2016 Stanford study by Ruan and colleagues measured speech entry on phones. It had 32 participants, 16 for English and 16 for Mandarin. For English, speech averaged 161.20 words per minute, against 53.46 for the built-in keyboard. That is 3.01 times faster. Speech also had a 20.4% lower total error rate for English.

The more useful finding is about correction. In that study, 86.5% of correction time went to the keyboard, not to speech. If an app gets more of the sentence right, you spend less time fixing it by hand.

The study compared speech with an on-screen keyboard on phones. It did not test desktop dictation apps on a Mac, so your numbers will differ. Run the [speaking vs typing test](/tools/speaking-vs-typing-test) and the [typing speed test](/tools/typing-speed-test) to get your own baseline.

## How to test a Wispr Flow alternative before you switch

Do not switch on a hunch. Run a one-week test with your own writing.

1. Write down five real jobs. Good picks are a Slack reply, a short email, a note to yourself, a ticket and a prompt for an AI tool.
2. Install each app and allow microphone access in System Settings > Privacy & Security > Microphone.
3. Run the same five jobs through each app for a week. Use the same microphone and a quiet room.
4. Time each job from the first word to the sent message. Count every fix you make with the keyboard.
5. Note how many words or uses you spent each week. Compare that with the plan limit before you pay.

The keyboard count matters as much as the timer. A fast app that needs six fixes per message can lose to a slower app that needs none.

## Mistakes to avoid when you switch dictation apps

- Judging on one sentence. Dictate ten real messages, not one test line.
- Forgetting the weekly reset. Wispr's free allowance resets on Sunday at 12 a.m. Pacific time, so a heavy week can end early.
- Skipping microphone permission. A new app that cannot hear you looks broken. Check System Settings > Privacy & Security > Microphone.
- Assuming every language works. Apple says Dictation is not available in every language or region.
- Assuming offline means every feature works offline. Superwhisper's offline models run best on Apple silicon, and its Meetings feature is Mac only.
- Comparing speed alone. Time to sent message, including edits, is the number to track.
