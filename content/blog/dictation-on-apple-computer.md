Dictation on an Apple computer allows you to turn your speech into text in almost any application. This built-in feature is a powerful way to speed up your writing and reduce strain on your hands.

## How to enable dictation on your Mac

To start using this feature, you must enable it in your system settings. Apple has consolidated these controls in recent macOS versions. Open the Apple menu and select System Settings. Navigate to the Keyboard section in the sidebar. You may need to scroll down to find the Dictation heading. Toggle the switch to On and click Enable when prompted. This activates the background processing required to turn your voice into written words.

## Configuring your dictation settings

Once enabled, you should verify your configuration. Within the same Dictation section, select your primary language. If you are multilingual, ensure the correct language is selected before you start. Choose your microphone source. Using the Automatic setting is usually sufficient, but if you have a high-quality external headset, selecting it specifically can improve accuracy. You can also define your shortcut key. By default, pressing the Fn key twice is the standard trigger, but you can change this to a different combination if you prefer.

## How to start and stop dictating

Using the feature is straightforward. Click into any text field where you want to input content. Press your designated shortcut key. You will see a microphone icon appear on the screen, signaling that your Mac is listening. Begin speaking clearly. When you are finished, press the shortcut key again or press the Escape key. If you stop speaking for about 30 seconds, the system will automatically stop listening. This pause allows you to gather your thoughts without the computer recording silence.

## Using punctuation and commands

Dictation is not just about words. You can add punctuation by speaking the names of the marks. Say period, comma, or question mark to insert them. You can also use structural commands. Say new line or new paragraph to format your text as you speak. While these commands are helpful, they require practice to get the timing right. If you find the built-in punctuation support lacking, you may want to look into dedicated tools or check out [Apple Dictation vs AI dictation on Mac in 2026](/blog/apple-dictation-vs-ai-dictation) for a comparison of how different engines handle your sentences.

## Troubleshooting common dictation issues

If dictation fails to trigger, the most common cause is a conflict with other accessibility settings. Standard macOS dictation is not available if Voice Control is turned on. You must disable Voice Control in your Accessibility settings to use the standard dictation feature. Another frequent issue is microphone permissions. Go to System Settings, then Privacy and Security, and finally Microphone. Ensure the application you are currently using has permission to access your microphone. If the app is not on the list, the system cannot process your voice input.

## Why speech is faster than typing

Most people speak at about 150 words per minute. In contrast, the average typing speed ranges from 40 to 60 words per minute. This difference provides a significant speed advantage for those who get comfortable with dictation. It is especially useful for long-form content, such as emails or drafts. ## Limitations of built-in dictation

While convenient, Apple Dictation has limits. It is excellent for prose but struggles with technical content or code. The system might misinterpret specialized terminology or complex formatting requirements. Additionally, accuracy can vary based on your accent and the environment. If you work in a noisy room, the background sounds may interfere with the software's ability to pick up your words. Always review your text after dictating to catch any transcription errors.

## Tips for better accuracy

To get the best results, speak at a steady pace. Do not rush your words. If you are in a quiet room, your Mac will capture your speech much more effectively. If you are using a built-in laptop microphone, try to sit at a consistent distance from the device. If you are constantly struggling with accuracy, consider using an external microphone. This can make a significant difference in how well the system understands you. For more advice on voice tools, read [Apple Dictation alternatives for Mac in 2026](/alternatives/apple-dictation).

## Privacy and on-device processing

One of the benefits of using an Apple computer is the focus on privacy. On Apple Silicon Macs, the speech processing for many supported languages happens directly on your device. This means your audio does not necessarily need to be sent to a cloud server to be converted into text. This is a significant advantage for users who handle sensitive information. Always check your settings to ensure you are comfortable with how your data is handled.
