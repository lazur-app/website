## How to Start Using Dictation Mac Commands

Dictation Mac commands allow you to insert punctuation, formatting, and symbols using your voice while the microphone is active. You must enable this feature in System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation to start using voice input in any text field.

Once enabled, you can activate the microphone by pressing the F5 key or your custom shortcut. You will see a microphone icon appear on your screen, indicating that your Mac is listening. Speak clearly and at a natural pace. If you speak too quickly or mumble, the accuracy will decrease. You do not need to pause between words, but you should speak your punctuation marks as you go. For example, say "The project is finished period" instead of just "The project is finished." The Mac will interpret the word "period" as a punctuation mark rather than text.

## Essential Punctuation and Formatting Commands

Using voice commands correctly prevents the need for manual cleanup after you finish speaking. When you dictate, your Mac listens for specific keywords that trigger formatting changes. If you want to start a new paragraph, simply say "new paragraph" and the cursor will move down two lines. For a soft line break, use "new line."

Using these commands consistently will save you from jumping back to the keyboard every few seconds. If you find yourself frequently fixing errors, check your microphone quality or background noise levels. Accuracy drops in noisy environments, so try to use a quiet space or a dedicated headset if you work in an open office.

## Why Dictation is Faster Than Typing

Most people type at 40 to 60 words per minute. By contrast, the average person speaks at 130 to 150 words per minute. This speed gap is the primary reason for using dictation. Research suggests that 62 percent of professionals save over four hours per week using automated transcription tools. That adds up to more than a month of extra work time every year. 

Even with the time required to correct minor errors, dictation remains significantly faster than thumb typing or standard keyboard input. Modern AI has reduced the correction tax that used to make voice input feel sluggish. You can now achieve 95 to 99 percent accuracy with standard Apple Dictation. This makes it a reliable tool for drafting emails, long-form documents, or meeting notes.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

One common mistake is treating dictation like a conversation with a human. Apple Dictation is a tool for inputting text, not a conversational assistant. If you say "change that to blue" mid-sentence, the Mac will likely type the words "change that to blue" into your document. It will not actually perform the edit. You must use Voice Control if you want to manipulate the interface or perform editing tasks.

Another mistake is pausing too long. Dictation will stop listening after 30 seconds of silence. If you need to stop and think about your next sentence, you will have to reactivate the microphone. This can disrupt your flow if you are not careful. Try to keep your thoughts organized before you start speaking to minimize the need to stop and restart the process.

## Technical Jargon and Accuracy

If you work in a field with specialized terminology, you may notice that standard dictation struggles with certain words. Apple Dictation is trained on general English, so it may misinterpret industry-specific jargon or proper nouns. To mitigate this, speak clearly and try to use full terms rather than abbreviations. If you find that the Mac consistently mishears a specific word, you may need to manually edit that word after you finish dictating. For highly technical workflows, you might consider third-party tools that allow for custom vocabulary training.
