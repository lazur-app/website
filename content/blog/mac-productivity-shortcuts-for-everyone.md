Mastering keyboard shortcuts is the most effective way to reduce stop and go friction in your workflow. By keeping your hands on the keyboard, you maintain focus and avoid the time consuming process of reaching for the trackpad. ## The Core Navigation Shortcuts The most critical tool on your Mac is Spotlight. Use Command ⌘ + Space to launch it. It acts as your command center for opening apps, finding files, or doing quick math. When you have many apps open, use Command ⌘ + Tab to cycle through them. Hold Command ⌘ and tap Tab to move forward. Add Shift to move backward. If an app stops responding, do not click around blindly. Press Option + Command ⌘ + Esc to open the Force Quit menu. Select the frozen app and click Force Quit. To lock your screen quickly when you walk away, press Control + Command ⌘ + Q. This keeps your data private without needing to shut down or sleep the machine. ## Managing Files and Folders in Finder Efficient file management relies on knowing how to move and preview items without clicking through folders. Use Command ⌘ + I to open the Get Info window for any selected file. This shows you the file size, creation date, and permissions. For a quick look at content, highlight any file and press the Spacebar. This is the Quick Look feature. It lets you see images, documents, and videos instantly. To create a new folder in your current directory, use Shift + Command ⌘ + N. If you want to move a file from one place to another, copy it with Command ⌘ + C. Instead of pasting with Command ⌘ + V, use Option + Command ⌘ + V. This performs a move action instead of a copy action. ## Capturing Your Screen Like a Pro The screenshot tools in macOS are robust and require no extra software. Press Shift + Command ⌘ + 4 to bring up a crosshair. Drag it to capture a specific area of your screen. If you want to capture a specific window, press Shift + Command ⌘ + 4 and then hit the Spacebar. Your cursor will turn into a camera icon. Click the window you want to capture. For more control, press Shift + Command ⌘ + 5. This opens the full screenshot toolbar. From here, you can record your screen or adjust timer settings. ## Essential App and Editing Shortcuts Every app on your Mac follows standard conventions for menus and editing. To access the settings or preferences for any active app, press Command ⌘ + , (comma). This is faster than navigating to the top menu bar. If you need to hide an app to clear your view, use Command ⌘ + H. To close the front window, use Command ⌘ + W. If you make a mistake, Command ⌘ + Z is your best friend. It undoes the last action. Use Shift + Command ⌘ + Z to redo if you go too far. ## Customizing Your Shortcuts If you find yourself clicking the same menu item repeatedly, create a custom shortcut. Open System Settings > Keyboard > Keyboard Shortcuts. Select App Shortcuts from the list on the left. Click the plus button to add a new one. You must type the menu command exactly as it appears in the app. Assign your preferred key combination. This is a great way to save time in apps like Excel or Figma where you perform repetitive actions. ## Faster with Lazur Sometimes the best way to handle a task is to ask for help without typing. You might not need apps like Rectangle or Magnet. Stick to the basics until you hit a wall. Only then should you look for specialized utilities. This keeps your system lean and saves you money on monthly subscriptions. Focus on learning the shortcuts you use every hour. The goal is muscle memory.
