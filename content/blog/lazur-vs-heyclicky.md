Lazur and Clicky, now sold as HeyClicky, both see your Mac screen and talk back. Pick Lazur if you want a Mac-only buddy that circles things and handles everyday tasks, or HeyClicky if you also need Windows or agent runs.

We make Lazur, so read our side with care. Everything about HeyClicky comes from its own pages and the open-source Clicky README, checked on 7 November 2026. Lazur facts come from lazur.app and its pricing page. We found no independent hands-on tests, so this compares published features, prices and privacy statements. It is not a lab test.

## Mac only or Mac and Windows?

Platform decides a lot, so start here. HeyClicky runs on both Mac and Windows, and one account works on each. Its Mac page asks for macOS Sonoma 14.2 or higher, on Apple silicon or Intel. Its Windows page says Windows 10 or higher.

Lazur is a Mac app. Its site says "Built for macOS" and offers a Mac download. It does not list a minimum macOS version, so read the download page before you install. Windows is planned, not available.

Check your version first. Open Apple menu > About This Mac. If you run anything older than Sonoma 14.2, HeyClicky's Mac app doesn't support your machine, and that settles the question.

### The open-source Clicky is a separate route

The Clicky repo on GitHub is open source under the MIT license. It is a Swift menu bar app that needs macOS 14.2 or later. The README says it uses Claude for AI, AssemblyAI for speech-to-text and ElevenLabs for text-to-speech, through a Cloudflare Worker proxy.

The same README says new work is private. Windows apps built from the repo are "community ports, not made by us." If you want the official app and its account, pick HeyClicky. If you want to read or change the code, start with the repo. Don't mix the two up when you read forum posts, because they are not the same product.

## Pointing and guiding: which one shows you where to click

Both products can tell you where something is. The difference is how they show it.

Lazur draws a circle and a label around the exact element on screen. It does this for icons without text too. Ask where the screen recording setting lives, and Lazur circles it so you can find it fast. You still do the clicking.

For a multi-step task, Lazur works one click at a time. It circles the first control, waits while you click, looks again, then circles the next one. Lazur does not click buttons for you, and it does not operate other apps.

HeyClicky says it can point at things on screen and walk you through a task by voice. Its pages also describe agents that run to complete tasks. Read its pages to see exactly what an agent can touch on your Mac before you hand it a job.

Here is a real case to test. Say you want to let a new app record your screen. Apple's path is Apple menu > System Settings > Privacy & Security > Screen & System Audio Recording. Both products need this permission to see your screen. If either buddy seems blind, check this setting first.

## Everyday Mac tasks: timers, reminders and shortcuts

Lazur handles a set of small jobs by voice. It sets timers, adds reminders to Apple Reminders, creates Calendar events and works with notes. It plays music, changes volume, opens apps and links you name, runs web searches and runs your Shortcuts.

Try a simple one. Say "remind me to send the invoice at four." The reminder lands in Apple Reminders. You don't open the app or type the task.

HeyClicky's pages we checked don't list an equivalent set of everyday Mac actions. Its public material centers on guidance and agents. If timers and reminders are your main use, test both before you decide.

Shortcuts only work when the shortcut works. Build and test a Shortcut in the Shortcuts app first. A broken Shortcut fails the same way for any buddy that runs it.

## Background agents and usage limits explained

Background work is the hardest part to compare, because the two products count different things.

Lazur runs research in its own Chrome tabs, through the Lazur for Chrome extension. When the job ends, you get a short spoken summary and a full report. The homepage says Lazur asks before the big stuff.

Lazur's pricing page defines an AI action as one thing you ask Lazur to do. That covers a question, a reminder, a pointed-at button or a background job. Dictation doesn't count.

HeyClicky counts talk messages and agent messages. You start agent mode by saying "heyclicky agent." When the monthly agent cap runs out, agent messages pause until the next cycle, and talk keeps working.

Here are HeyClicky's plan limits, from its Mac page:

| Plan | Price | Talk per month | Agent messages per month |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | 25 | 25 |
| Pro | $20/month | Unlimited | 150 |
| Max | $100/month | Unlimited | 1,000 |

Do the math for your week. 150 agent messages in a 30-day month works out to five a day. Max's 1,000 covers about 33 a day. If you run fewer than five agent jobs a day, Pro's cap covers you on paper.

Lazur counts every question as an action too, so a heavy day of questions uses up its allowance faster. HeyClicky's talk is unlimited on Pro, so questions don't touch its agent cap. Check your own mix of questions and background jobs before you compare the two limits.

## Dictation: included, and unlimited?

Lazur started as a dictation app. Hold fn, speak, and you get clean, formatted text at the cursor in any app. Snippets, a personal dictionary and clipboard slots come with it. Dictation is unlimited on both Lazur plans and doesn't count against AI actions.

HeyClicky's free tier includes "plenty of dictation." Pro and Max include unlimited talk and dictation. Its pages don't say what "plenty" means, so test your own volume on the free tier before you rely on it.

Is dictation worth the switch? A 2016 study by Ruan and colleagues found English speech input was 3.0 times faster than typing on a miniature smartphone keyboard. The English error rate was 20.4% lower with speech. That test ran on phones, not Macs, and it is a decade old. Treat it as a direction, not a Mac benchmark.

Run your own test. Write the same two-paragraph reply once by typing and once by holding fn. Time both, then count the fixes you make. That number matters more for your week than any study.

## Privacy: what each app sends and stores

Lazur listens and looks only while you hold its shortcut. Hold ⌥ ⌘ to ask, or fn to dictate. The audio and one screenshot go to AI providers so Lazur can answer. They are not stored, and your content is never used to train AI models. Lazur keeps a memory of what you asked and did, kept on the Mac. Its homepage makes no on-device processing claim, so treat answers as cloud-processed.

HeyClicky says it only sees your screen when you press the hotkey, and that screenshots are never stored. It does keep basic text summaries so it has context. You can delete your account and all its data in settings.

Both are vendor statements. We found no independent audit of either product. If the difference matters to you, read both privacy pages and check the one thing you care about, such as whether text summaries are acceptable for your work.

Both products ask for Screen Recording. HeyClicky's setup also asks for Microphone and Accessibility. Apple's path for screen recording is Apple menu > System Settings > Privacy & Security > Screen & System Audio Recording. You can allow an app to record the screen, audio, or both. Apple's page doesn't say whether you need to restart the app after you allow it, so test once after you change the setting.

## Lazur vs Clicky pricing in 2026

Lazur gives you a 3-day Pro trial. The clock starts the first time you open the app, and no credit card is needed. After that, Pro is $18 a month, or $15 a month billed annually, with 150 AI actions. Power is $120 a month, or $96 a month billed annually, with 1,000 AI actions. Dictation is unlimited on both. Check [Lazur's pricing page](/pricing) for the current figures.

HeyClicky's plans are month to month and cancelable. Free is $0, Pro is $20 a month and Max is $100 a month. Its yearly toggle shows a 20% saving, but it doesn't list yearly prices, so check the page before you assume a discount.

Compare the units, not the sticker. Lazur Pro at $18 and HeyClicky Pro at $20 look close. Lazur Pro counts questions and tasks as actions. HeyClicky Pro gives unlimited talk plus 150 agent messages. Work out which limit your week fills first.

## Which AI buddy should you pick?

Gallup's May 2026 indicator says 15% of U.S. employees use AI daily in their jobs. Most people are still building that habit. Start with one task, not a full setup.

- Pick Lazur if you work on one Mac and want circles, step-by-step guidance, timers, reminders and dictation in one app.
- Pick HeyClicky if you also use a Windows PC, want one account across both, or want agent runs under a monthly cap.
- Pick the open-source Clicky if you want to read or change the code, and you run macOS 14.2 or later.
- Pick neither if you mostly need a chat window. [ChatGPT on Mac](/apps/chatgpt) fits that job differently.

## A three-day test you can run on your Mac

1. Check your macOS version with Apple menu > About This Mac.
2. Grant Screen Recording in Apple menu > System Settings > Privacy & Security > Screen & System Audio Recording, for whichever app you test first.
3. Pick one real task from this week, such as finding a setting or drafting a reply.
4. Try the task in HeyClicky's free tier. Note whether it guided you, pointed at something or ran an agent.
5. Try the same task in Lazur, holding ⌥ ⌘ to ask. Note whether it circled, answered or walked you through.
6. Time one dictated reply in each app, then check your usage counters. HeyClicky counts talk and agent messages. Lazur counts AI actions and not dictation.

## Three mistakes that skew this comparison

- Testing on a demo. A pointing buddy looks great on a clean screen. Test on your real apps, with your real windows and your messy desktop.
- Starting the trial too early. The Lazur trial clock starts when you first open the app. Open it on the day you can really try it.
- Treating HeyClicky and the open-source Clicky as one product. The repo is the open-source version, and HeyClicky is the current official app.
