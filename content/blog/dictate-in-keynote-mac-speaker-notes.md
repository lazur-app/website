To dictate in Keynote on Mac, click where the text should go, then choose Edit > Start Dictation from the menu bar. Turn on Dictation first in System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation, and click Done under the microphone when you finish.

That answer covers most cases. The rest of this guide covers the details that trip people up: setup, start and stop options, punctuation, speaker notes, time limits, and cost. Apple's Keynote help page does not name a minimum version, so check your version under Keynote > About Keynote if something acts differently from what you read here.

## Turn on Dictation on your Mac first

Keynote uses the same Dictation system as the rest of macOS. If you have never used it, switch it on before you open a deck. Otherwise Start Dictation may not respond the way you expect.

1. Choose Apple menu > System Settings.
2. Click Keyboard in the sidebar. Scroll down if you need to.
3. Go to Dictation and turn it on. Click Enable if your Mac asks.

Your Mac also asks whether to share audio recordings to improve Siri and Dictation. The choice is yours. Apple's page does not say it changes the steps below.

The Dictation pane is also where you manage languages. Click Edit next to Languages to add one. Apple says Dictation is not available in every language or region. Check the list before you rely on it for a talk in another language.

## How to dictate in Keynote on Mac, step by step

These steps take a minute the first time, because Keynote may walk you through a short setup. After that, the same steps take a few seconds.

1. Open the presentation and go to the slide you want to edit.
2. Click inside a text box or placeholder until a cursor blinks. If you want to replace words, select them first.
3. Choose Edit > Start Dictation from the menu bar.
4. When the microphone appears, speak your text in normal sentences.
5. Say the name of any punctuation mark you need, such as comma or apostrophe.
6. Click Done under the microphone, or press Escape.
7. Read the text back and fix errors before you move to the next slide.

Here is a worked example. You are writing slide 4 of a sales deck. The body box is empty. You click inside it, choose Edit > Start Dictation, and say "Three reasons customers stay comma and one reason they leave." Then you click Done. The sentence lands in the box with the comma in place. Check it anyway. Dictation is fast, but it still makes mistakes with names, numbers, and product terms.

## Ways to start and stop dictation in Keynote

Keynote gives you three ways to start dictation. The menu is the most reliable one, especially the first time you use it.

| Method | How to start | How to stop |
|---|---|---|
| Menu bar | Edit > Start Dictation | Click Done under the microphone, or press Escape |
| Microphone key | Press the Microphone key, if your keyboard has one | Press the Microphone key again, or press Escape |
| Dictation shortcut | Press the shortcut set in System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation | Press the shortcut again, or press Escape |

Apple's current help page does not name a default shortcut. You set it in Keyboard > Dictation through the Shortcut pop-up menu, or by choosing Customize and pressing the keys you want. Some guides online say a double tap of fn or the Globe key. Apple's page does not confirm that, so read what your own Shortcut menu shows.

To switch languages during a session, click the language indicator next to the cursor, or press the Globe key. Those are the two options Apple lists.

## How to say punctuation and new marks out loud

Keynote does not guess every mark. Apple's examples are "comma" and "apostrophe." When you say the name of a mark, Dictation inserts the mark. Say "comma" after "stay" and you get "stay,". Say "apostrophe" inside a word and you get the mark you need.

Test the marks you use most before a live talk. Say two or three of them, read the result, and fix anything odd. Expect trouble if you need the literal word "comma" on a slide. In that case, type the word or paste it in.

Apple's pages do not describe spoken commands for bullets, line breaks, or formatting. Plan to type those yourself. Dictate the sentences, then format them by hand.

The same Edit > Start Dictation path works in Pages. If you write handouts or scripts in both apps, read [how to dictate in Pages on Mac](/blog/dictate-in-pages-mac-start-dictation-edit-menu) for the matching steps.

## How to add and dictate speaker notes in Keynote on Mac

Speaker notes are the part of this topic that confuses people most. Keynote keeps them in their own area, separate from the slide.

To show them, click the View menu button in the toolbar and choose Show Presenter Notes. The notes area sits beneath the slide in the slide navigator. Click there and type. To hide them again, choose Hide Presenter Notes from the same menu.

Apple says you can edit notes in the presenter display during rehearsal, or when you present on a separate display. That means you can tidy notes while you practice, not only before you start.

Apple's presenter notes page does not say you can dictate into the notes area. Test it on your own Mac. Click the notes area, choose Edit > Start Dictation, and say one sentence. If the words appear, keep going. If nothing appears, use a workaround. Dictate into a text box on a spare slide or into the Notes app. Select the text, copy it with ⌘C, click the notes area, and paste with ⌘V.

Keynote also has a toolbar button called Generate Presenter Notes. Apple says it writes notes for a slide automatically. It needs an Apple Creator Studio subscription and macOS 26 or later. MacRumors describes the presenter-notes tools as beta, so expect changes. Read every generated note before you rely on it on stage.

For speaker notes, write short cues, not full scripts. A cue like "pricing, two tiers, ask about the trial" is easy to dictate and easy to read at a glance.

## Where Keynote dictation stops and how long it lasts

Dictation has two stopping rules you should know.

The first is silence. Apple's Mac Help page says dictation stops automatically when it hears no speech for 30 seconds. The Keynote page does not state a timeout, so Keynote's behavior is an inference from Apple's general Mac rule. Until Apple says otherwise, plan for it. If you stop to think for half a minute, the microphone may close, and you will need to restart it.

The second is length. Apple says you can dictate text of any length without a timeout. The limit applies to silence, not to how long you talk. Dictate one slide at a time, and you will rarely hit the silence rule by accident.

If the microphone does not respond at all, check the input device. Open System Settings > Sound > Input, and confirm the Mac is listening to the microphone you expect. A headset that is connected but not selected is a common cause.

## Common mistakes and quick fixes

Most problems come from a few habits. Check these first.

**The cursor is not in a text box.** Click inside the box until the cursor blinks. If you click the empty slide canvas, the text may not land where you want.

**You forgot to click Done.** Click Done under the microphone or press Escape to end the session. A clear end point helps you see what Keynote heard.

**You paused too long.** Remember the 30-second silence rule. Restart dictation after a long think, and dictate in short passes.

**Dictation is off.** If Start Dictation does nothing, reopen System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation and confirm it is on.

**You expected formatting.** Apple's pages do not list spoken formatting commands for bullets or line breaks. Type those yourself after the words are in.

For the full troubleshooting list, read [how to dictate on a Mac](/blog/how-to-dictate-on-mac) and the general setup notes there.

## Is dictation worth it for slide text?

Speed is the main reason people dictate. A lab study by Ruan and colleagues measured English speech input at 153 words per minute and keyboard entry at 52 words per minute on touchscreen phones. That makes speech 2.93 times faster in that test. The study had 48 students copy short phrases in a quiet room. The 153 figure is after participants fixed their errors. Before correction, speech measured 179 words per minute, according to a Laxis summary of the study.

Keynote is not a phone, and slide text is not a copied phrase. Use the study as direction, not a promise. Do the math on your own talk instead. Say a slide needs 100 words. At 153 words per minute, that takes about 39 seconds. At 52 words per minute, it takes almost two minutes. Your own pace will land somewhere in between, and it will change with your voice, your mic, and the room.

Dictation fits first drafts well. It fits speaker cues and rough bullet points well too. It fits final, polished copy less well, because you will edit most of it anyway.

## Keynote versions, Creator Studio, and what each costs

Apple launched the Apple Creator Studio bundle on January 28, 2026. MacRumors reports US pricing at $12.99 per month or $129 per year. The subscription adds extra features and premium content in Keynote, Numbers, Pages, and Pixelmator Pro.

The version split matters. On Mac, the existing Keynote app was updated to version 14.5, and Apple said it will no longer receive new features of any kind. New features, including free ones, require the separate Creator Studio edition, version 15.1.

| Item | Keynote 14.5 | Keynote 15.1 in Creator Studio |
|---|---|---|
| New features | None after this update | Yes, including beta tools |
| Generate Presenter Notes | Not in this version | Yes, with macOS 26 or later |
| Price (US) | Not a subscription | $12.99 per month or $129 per year |

Apple's dictation and Keynote pages do not list a subscription for dictation itself. Check Apple's pricing for your country, because prices differ by region.
