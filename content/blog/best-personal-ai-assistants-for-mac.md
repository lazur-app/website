There is no single best personal AI assistant for every Mac. The right pick depends on the job, whether that is open chat, a built-in Apple feature, or help inside the app already on your screen.\n\nMost people do best with two tools. Use one general chat assistant for research and long questions. Use one tool close to your work for email, notes, and the “how do I do this” moments. This guide compares the main options as of November 2026, using vendor help pages, pricing pages, a Pew Research survey, and one third-party review.\n\n## How this comparison was built\n\nWe did not run our own side-by-side tests for this guide. We read the help and pricing pages from Apple, Anthropic, and Raycast. We checked a Pew Research survey from June 2026, and we used one third-party review for scores. Where a vendor page was unclear, we say so below.\n\nPrices and versions change often. Before you buy, open the vendor's pricing page and check the date on it. A comparison from a few months ago can already be wrong about plan limits.\n\n## What a personal AI assistant on a Mac should do\n\nStart with the job, not the brand. A useful assistant does four things well:\n\n- It understands what you are looking at, so you do not paste every email or error message into a prompt.\n- It works in more than one app, not only inside its own window.\n- It returns the result where you need it, such as a draft at the cursor.\n- It tells you what leaves your Mac, and when.\n\nThen check the practical limits. Some features need a newer chip or more memory. Others need a paid plan, a specific macOS version, or a matching language setting. Ten minutes of checking now saves an afternoon of confusion later.\n\n## Apple Intelligence and Siri AI: the built-in option\n\nApple's option costs nothing extra and comes with macOS. Apple describes Siri AI as “an entirely new version of Siri” that is in beta for macOS 27 Golden Gate. Beta means the feature can change between releases. Do not build a daily routine around it yet.\n\nApple's support page lists Siri AI for MacBook Neo (A18 Pro) and for Macs with M1 or later, running macOS 27 Golden Gate. A supported Mac is only the first gate. Three more can block it:\n\n- Language. The device language and the Siri language must match. Apple lists English, French, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, and Spanish.\n- Region. Siri AI will not work if the Apple Account region is mainland China. In the EU, it works on macOS 27 only if the Apple Account region is in the EU and the language settings match.\n- Chip and memory for the best features. Apple's page says Macs with M3 or later and at least 12 GB of unified memory need up to 14 GB of storage. Other supported Macs need up to 8 GB.\n\nMacworld reports that on-device model features, including expressive voices and higher-accuracy dictation, need an M3 chip or faster and 12 GB of RAM. An M2 MacBook Air can run macOS 27, Apple Intelligence, and Siri AI, but it misses those on-device features. That matters if dictation accuracy is your main reason to switch.\n\nSome third-party guides cite older macOS versions for Apple Intelligence. Apple's page is the one to trust. Before you plan around any version, check Apple menu > System Settings > General > Software Update.\n\nUse Apple's option when your Mac, language, and region all qualify, and you want no extra app. Skip it if you need a stable tool for work this month.\n\n## ChatGPT: the general chat choice\n\nChatGPT is the assistant most people already know. In Pew Research's 2026 survey, 44% of U.S. adults said they have ever used ChatGPT. That is a useful sign of reach, but it is not a clean trend, as the mistakes section explains.\n\nArahi, a third-party reviewer, scored the ChatGPT Mac app 9 out of 10. Its tests ran from April 28 to May 12, 2026, on an M3 Mac mini. Arahi says the scores cannot be independently verified, because it does not publish raw results.\n\nWe did not check ChatGPT's current plans against OpenAI's own pages for this guide. For prices and limits, check their pricing page directly. We are not quoting ChatGPT prices here.\n\nChatGPT suits long research, brainstorming, and drafts that start from a blank page. Its weak spot for Mac work is the copy and paste. If an answer depends on an email in Gmail, you bring that email into the chat yourself. A tool that reads your current selection saves that step.\n\n## Claude desktop: chat with computer use in beta\n\nThe Claude desktop app is the second general chat option. It also has the most hands-on feature in this list. Anthropic's help center says computer use is in beta for Pro, Max, and Ultra plans. It works in Cowork and Claude Code inside the Claude Desktop app for macOS and Windows. Team and Enterprise plans do not have access yet.\n\nTo turn it on, update Claude Desktop, then open Settings > Capabilities. If no Computer use section appears, open Settings > System under “This computer.” Turn on “Enable computer use.”\n\nAnthropic lists limits that matter in daily use:\n\n- The computer needs to be awake, and Claude Desktop needs to be open.\n- Complex, multi-step tasks may need a second attempt.\n- Screen interaction is slower than connectors.\n- Background operation needs macOS 15 or later.\n\nHere is a worked example. You ask Claude to work through a long list of files, then walk away to lunch. The task stops once the Mac sleeps. Keep the Mac awake and the app open, or split the list into smaller batches and run them one after another.\n\nArahi scored Claude Desktop 7 out of 10 in the same test.\n\n## Raycast AI: for people who stay on the keyboard\n\nRaycast is a keyboard launcher with AI added on top. If you already open apps by typing their names, it may match your habits better than a voice tool.\n\nIts pricing page lists four tiers:\n\n- Free: $0, and it has no AI.\n- Pro: $10 per month, or $96 per year, with 500 monthly AI credits.\n- Plus: $20 per month, or $192 per year, with 3,000 credits and top-tier models.\n- Max: $50 per month, or $480 per year, with 7,500 credits.\n\nRaycast says unused monthly credits roll over for one extra billing cycle. Top-up credits expire 12 months after purchase, and they need an active paid subscription. Arahi scored Raycast AI 8 out of 10.\n\nHere is a common trap. You install Raycast for the launcher, then expect the AI to work on day one. It will not. The free plan has no AI, so you need Pro at minimum.\n\n## Scores from one third-party review\n\nArahi's review gives a quick ranking. Use it as a starting point, not a verdict.\n\n| Assistant | Arahi score (out of 10) |\n| --- | --- |\n| ChatGPT for macOS | 9 |\n| Apple Intelligence | 8 |\n| Raycast AI | 8 |\n| Claude Desktop | 7 |\n| Microsoft Copilot | 6 |\n| Gemini (web) | 6 |\n\nArahi tested from April 28 to May 12, 2026, and updated the review on May 18, 2026. It describes Apple Intelligence as system-level writing features, such as Writing Tools and Smart Reply, rather than a standalone assistant. That distinction matters. If you want a chat window, Apple's option will feel thin. If you want writing help everywhere, it may be enough.\n\nArahi also runs a directory that sells listings. Read its ranking with that in mind.\n\n## Three setups that work for real people\n\nA setup is easier to judge than a list of features, so here are three.\n\nSay you write client email all day. Use a general chat app to draft new messages from a blank page. Use a voice tool for replies inside [Gmail](/apps/gmail), where the thread is already on screen. Two tools, each doing one job, beat one tool stretched across both.\n\nSay you write code in an editor. Keep the coding assistant in the editor, where it can read your files. The [Cursor guide](/apps/cursor) covers that workflow. Use Claude or ChatGPT for design questions and longer explanations that do not need your repository open.\n\nSay you are not comfortable with System Settings, and you often cannot find a menu. A voice assistant that points at what is on screen is the better match. Apple's built-in option can help too, but only if your Mac, language, and region qualify. Check those first.\n\n## Which assistant fits which job\n\nMatch the tool to the task, not to the reviews:\n\n| What you need most | Start with | Why |\n| --- | --- | --- |\n| No extra app, supported Mac | Siri AI | Built into macOS, still in beta |\n| Long research and open chat | ChatGPT or Claude | Built for conversation |\n| Claude to use your screen | Claude desktop on Pro, Max, or Ultra | Beta, needs app open and Mac awake |\n| Keyboard-first launching with AI | Raycast Pro or higher | Free plan has no AI |\n| Voice help for the app in front of you | A context-aware voice tool (see below) | Hold a key and talk |\n\nMost readers pick two. A general chat app handles the big questions. A tool close to your work handles the small, constant ones, like a reply or a menu you cannot find.\n\n## How to protect your privacy with a Mac AI assistant\n\nPrivacy depends on the product and the task, so check three things.\n\nFirst, read the privacy policy, not the marketing page. The policy says what the company keeps, for how long, and whether it uses your content to train models.\n\nSecond, know what the assistant can see. An assistant that reads your screen needs macOS permission to do it. Review those permissions in Privacy & Security settings, and turn off the ones you do not need.\n\nThird, keep sensitive work in mind. Medical, legal, and client material deserves a closer look at where audio and text go. If the answer is unclear, do not paste that material into any tool until it is.\n\n## Mistakes people make when choosing\n\n1. Reading the Pew jump as a trend. Pew found 44% of U.S. adults used ChatGPT in 2026, up from 34% in 2025. But Pew changed the 2026 question to cover other chatbots too. The two figures do not compare cleanly.\n2. Treating a review score as a lab result. Arahi says its scores cannot be independently verified.\n3. Expecting AI on Raycast's free plan. The free plan has no AI.\n4. Buying a Team seat for Claude computer use. Team and Enterprise plans do not have access yet.\n5. Assuming Siri AI works in your language. Check the language and the Apple Account region first.\n6. Comparing credits with other units. Raycast counts credits, and other tools use their own units. Test the work you actually do before you compare numbers.\n\n## Where Lazur fits\n\nLazur is a voice assistant for the Mac that lives in the notch. Hold ⌥⌘ to ask about anything on screen, and it answers out loud. Ask where a button is, and it draws a circle and a label around it. Ask how to do something, and it guides you one click at a time, waiting for each click. It does not click buttons or operate apps for you.\n\nHold fn to dictate clean, formatted text at the cursor in any app. Dictation is unlimited on both paid plans.\n\nIt listens and looks at the screen only while you hold the shortcut. Audio and the screenshot go to AI providers to answer, and they are not stored. The company says content is never used to train AI models.\n\nIt fits best if your day runs through apps like [Safari](/apps/safari) or Gmail, and you want help without switching windows. It fits less well for long research. Use ChatGPT or Claude for that.\n\nIt runs on macOS, on Apple silicon and Intel Macs. A Windows version is planned, not available. A free Pro trial needs no credit card. Plan details are on the [pricing page](/pricing), and you can [download it](/download) from your Mac.\n\n## Next steps\n\n1. Check your macOS version and Apple's Siri AI requirements on Apple's support page.\n2. Pick one general chat app and one tool for work inside your apps.\n3. Run both on real tasks for one week. Try an email reply, a menu you cannot find, and one research question.\n4. Read the privacy policy of each tool before you use it with work accounts.\n"
