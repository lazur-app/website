Claude Desktop Quick Entry on Mac is a small prompt window that opens over any app when you double-tap Option. It attaches screenshots, takes spoken questions through Caps Lock dictation, and needs macOS 12 or later, with dictation needing macOS 14 or later.

This guide covers setup, the screenshot and voice steps, the Windows question, and the fixes Anthropic shipped in recent weeks. The details come from Anthropic’s Help Center, its changelog, and Claude Academy, as of 2026-11-07. Anthropic updates these pages often. If your screen looks different, trust the newest Help Center article over this page.

## What Claude Desktop Quick Entry does

Quick Entry is a second way into Claude. The main Claude Desktop window is the full app. Quick Entry is the small floating box you open while you stay in your work.

That changes how you use it. You skip the app switch, the copy and paste, and the hunt for the right window. You point at the thing on screen, ask, and send. Three common cases:

- A build fails in Terminal. Double-tap Option, drag a box around the error, and ask what it means.
- A client email mentions a deadline you need to check. Capture the message, then ask for a short reply.
- A PDF has a table you cannot read. Select the table and ask for a plain-English summary.

Claude Desktop has to be running. It can sit in the background, so the main window does not need to be open. Every plan includes Quick Entry, including Free, Pro, Max, Ultra, Team, and Enterprise. You do not need to upgrade to try it.

## Requirements for Quick Entry: macOS versions and permissions

Start by checking your macOS version. Choose Apple menu > About This Mac. Then compare what you see with this table.

| Requirement | What it covers |
|---|---|
| macOS 12 or later | Quick Entry |
| macOS 14 or later | Voice dictation with Caps Lock |
| Claude Desktop running | Quick Entry, including when the app sits in the background |
| Screen Recording permission | Screenshots and window sharing |
| Accessibility permission | Opening Quick Entry |
| Speech Recognition permission | Voice dictation (macOS 14 or later) |

On macOS 13, you can use Quick Entry but not Caps Lock dictation. That gap matters if your Mac runs an older release.

Grant the permissions in System Settings > Privacy & Security. Each permission has its own entry there. When Quick Entry seems broken, check these three before you reinstall anything. Without Screen Recording, the screenshot step cannot work.

## How to open Quick Entry and change the shortcut

The default is simple. Double-tap Option in any app, and the prompt box appears. You can change that trigger if the double tap gets in your way.

1. Open Claude Desktop and go to Settings > Desktop app. On the older layout, go to Settings > System and look under “This computer.”
2. Find the Quick access shortcut setting.
3. Pick double-tap Option, Option + Space, or a custom shortcut.
4. Open Notes, press your shortcut, and confirm the prompt box appears.

| Shortcut setting | How you trigger it | Good fit when |
|---|---|---|
| Double-tap Option | Press Option twice quickly | You want the default and a short, memorable trigger |
| Option + Space | Hold Option, then press Space | You tap Option often while typing shortcuts |
| Custom shortcut | The key combination you set | Another app already uses the keys you would pick |

If a shortcut does nothing, another app may already use it. Pick a different combination and test again in Notes.

## How to take a screenshot with Quick Entry on Mac

Screenshots set Quick Entry apart from a plain chat box. You can capture one region or one whole window. Here is the sequence Anthropic describes.

1. Leave the content on screen. Bring the window, document, or error to the front.
2. Double-tap Option.
3. Wait for the “Drag to take a screenshot” prompt near your cursor.
4. Click and drag around the area you want.
5. Check that the screenshot is attached, type your question, and send.

To capture a window instead, click the window rather than dragging. Claude attaches that window’s contents. Dragging works better when you need one error message, one cell, or one paragraph. Only the area you select, or the window you click, goes into the message.

Say a spreadsheet shows a DIV/0 error in one cell. Drag a box around that cell and the row labels beside it. Then ask what is breaking the formula. A tight box keeps the rest of your sheet out of the message.

Some work Macs have screenshots turned off. Changelog entries from 20 and 22 October 2026 added admin settings for macOS, quickEntryScreenshotsEnabled and quickEntryFileSharingEnabled. When an admin sets one to false, that feature stops. If the screenshot option is missing on a work Mac, ask your IT team before you troubleshoot.

## How to dictate with Caps Lock in Quick Entry

Voice dictation uses Caps Lock, and it is off by default. Dictation takes over that key, so it stays off until you choose it. Turn it on before you expect Caps Lock to record.

1. Go to Settings > Desktop app and open the voice shortcut dropdown. Choose Caps Lock, a custom shortcut, or off.
2. Open Quick Entry with your shortcut.
3. Press Caps Lock to start. Watch the words appear as you speak.
4. Press Caps Lock again to stop.
5. Read the text, fix names and numbers, then click the arrow to send.

Step five matters most. Speech recognition is fast, but it still mishears proper names, file paths, and numbers. A wrong digit in a reply can cost more time than the dictation saved.

Expect Caps Lock to behave differently while dictation owns it. If you need normal capitals in another app, change the voice shortcut first. If you type in all caps often, pick a custom shortcut now. Then Caps Lock stays a plain key in every app, and you avoid a surprise mid-sentence.

## Recent Quick Entry fixes and what they mean for you

Anthropic’s changelog lists Quick Entry fixes from late September to early November 2026. The version numbers do not follow one pattern, so match entries by date.

| Date | Entry | What it fixed |
|---|---|---|
| 29 September 2026 | v2.16120.0 | Prompts sent while the app was loading, file names in existing chats, and a crash after an update |
| 1 October 2026 | v2.19675.0 | A message discarded when an attached file had moved, been deleted, or could not be read. It now sends without that file and shows a notice. |
| 20 and 22 October 2026 | Admin settings and a session fix | Added quickEntryDefaultShortcut, quickEntryScreenshotsEnabled, and quickEntryFileSharingEnabled on macOS. Fixed a prompt that did not start a session. |
| 3 November 2026 | v4.16336.0 | Dictation on Mac closing without adding what you said, and words lost when a message was sent from another box on the same page |

Two fixes matter most for daily use. The 1 October fix stops a whole message from disappearing when an attached file moved or cannot be read. The 3 November fix targets dictation that closed without adding your words.

If words vanished for you during dictation, update Claude Desktop. Then run a one-sentence test in Notes before you rely on it. Quick Entry changes often. Check the changelog before you spend an hour on a problem a newer build may have fixed.

## Is Claude Desktop Quick Entry available on Windows?

No, not according to Anthropic’s Help Center. Its answer is short: “No. Quick entry is currently only available for macOS users.” Windows users can still open Claude Desktop, just without the Quick Entry features.

Not every source agrees. A Claude Academy tutorial describes a Windows shortcut and a system tray icon. That conflicts with the Help Center. For now, trust the Help Center and treat the tutorial’s Windows section as unconfirmed.

The Help Center article shows only “Updated this week,” with no exact date. That makes the Windows answer harder to pin down. Check the changelog every few weeks for a Windows change.

If you work on Windows, use the main Claude window for questions that need context. Paste the text or describe what you see. Use your system’s own tools for dictation and screenshots.

## Does voice beat typing for Quick Entry questions?

Voice wins on speed for long questions, based on research. None of these numbers measure Quick Entry itself, so treat them as a guide, not a promise.

A 2016 study of short English messages on an iPhone 6 Plus found speech entry at 153 WPM. Keyboard entry reached 52 WPM in the same setup. The authors describe speech as 2.93 times faster. That was a lab test on a phone, not a desktop chat window.

Speech costs some accuracy. In the same study, speech left slightly more uncorrected errors in the final text: 1.30% against 0.79% for the keyboard. Corrected error figures differ between the paper and a secondary summary, so this page does not rely on them.

For desktop typing, a Laxis summary gives an average of 51.56 WPM across 168,000 volunteers, from a CHI 2018 study by Dhakal et al. A 2025 preprint of more than 1,000 clinicians reports 21.4 WPM for keyboard and 93 WPM for dictation. The preprint’s funder employs its first author, so treat it as a lead, not a verdict.

Run your own test. Take three questions you normally type, speak them, and time each one. The [speaking vs typing test](/tools/speaking-vs-typing-test) gives you a quick baseline.

Voice suits long questions and rough thoughts. Typing suits exact file names, URLs, and code. Quick Entry lets you do either in the same box.

## Mistakes that waste time in Quick Entry

Capturing too much. A full-screen screenshot hides the one line that matters. Drag a tight box, then add a sentence that says what you want Claude to check.

Sending dictated text unread. Read the draft before you send. The review step is where names, numbers, and code get fixed.

Using Quick Entry for a long thread. Quick Entry suits one question with its context attached. For a back-and-forth session, open the main Claude window. It is the better place for threads you plan to return to.

Ignoring the changelog. An old build can keep a bug that a newer release already fixed.
