To copy text from an image on Mac, open the photo in Preview, hover over the words, drag to select them, then Control-click the selection and choose Copy Text. In the Photos app, hover over the text, select it, and press Command-C or use the same Copy Text menu item.

That is the short version. This guide covers the setting you need to check first, the steps for Preview, Photos, and screenshots, what to do when selection fails, and how to clean up the text after you paste it. Most failures come from one of three things: a turned-off setting, a blurry image, or a menu item you did not expect to see.

## What Live Text does with photos and screenshots

Live Text finds text inside an image and makes it selectable. The image can be a photo of a receipt, a scan of a page, a sign you snapped on a walk, or a screenshot of a chat. Once Live Text finds the words, you can select them, copy them, and paste them like normal text.

Apple added Live Text with macOS Monterey. At launch, 9to5Mac reported that it supported seven languages: English, Chinese, French, Italian, German, Portuguese, and Spanish. That article is from December 2021. Apple's current feature availability page lists more language and region entries than that, so check the page before you assume your language is missing.

The same 2021 coverage says Live Text works in Photos, Safari, Quick Look, and the Screenshot interface. Preview is the most direct route for image files, so this guide starts there. For exact steps, Apple's own Preview and Photos guides are the most reliable sources.

## Check your Mac and the Live Text setting first

Start with a quick check. It takes about a minute, and it rules out the most common cause of a missing menu.

Step one: confirm your macOS version. Choose Apple menu > About This Mac. The current release is macOS 27 Golden Gate, which reached the public on September 14, 2026, according to MacRumors. The latest point release on that MacRumors roundup, when I checked, was 27.2. If you run an older version, the steps below still apply, but some menu labels may differ.

Step two: confirm your Mac supports the feature. Apple first said Live Text needed an M1 Mac. Intel Macs got it in the fourth Monterey beta on July 27, 2021, according to MacRumors. So age alone will not stop you. If you run a current macOS and still see nothing, move on to the setting.

Step three: check the setting. Choose Apple menu > System Settings, then General > Language & Region. Look for the row that says 'Select text in images to copy or take action'. You may need to scroll to find it. Turn it on.

Older guides use different names. The 2022 MacRumors walkthrough, written for Monterey, calls the app System Preferences and shows a checkbox named Live Text: Select Text in Images. If your screen does not match the labels in this guide, search for the phrase in the System Settings search box.

Turning Live Text off also hides the text recognition symbol and removes the Live Text contextual menu, according to MacRumors. If Copy Text vanished from your menu one day, this setting is the first thing to check.

## Copy text from a photo in Preview

Preview is the most direct route for image files like JPEG and PNG. Use these steps.

1. Open the image. Double-click it in Finder. If another app opens it, right-click the file and choose Open With > Preview.
2. Hover your pointer over the text. You do not need to click first.
3. Drag across the words you want. The selection highlights as you move.
4. Control-click the selection and choose Copy Text.
5. Switch to the app where you want the words and press Command-V (⌘V).

Apple's Preview guide does not list a keyboard shortcut for the copy step. Use the menu. You can test Command-C on your version, but treat it as unconfirmed until it works for you.

Two details matter here. First, Copy Text copies only what you selected. If you want the whole block, drag across all of it, from the first word to the last. Second, long passages are easier to handle in parts. Select one paragraph, copy it, paste it, then repeat. You get cleaner output, and you can catch a bad read before it reaches your document.

Preview also gives you zoom and crop tools. If the text is small in the frame, zoom in first. Selection works better on large text than on a tiny corner of a photo.

## Copy text from a photo in the Photos app

Photos is faster when the picture already lives in your library. Apple's Photos guide describes two ways to copy.

1. Open the Photos app and open the photo.
2. Hover over the text.
3. Select the words you want.
4. Control-click the selection and choose Copy Text, or press Command-C (⌘C).
5. Paste into your destination app with ⌘V.

The Photos guide is the Apple page I checked that lists Command-C for copying Live Text. That makes Photos the quickest path when you want a fast copy and do not need to crop or mark up the image first. If you need those tools, open the photo in Preview instead.

Photos also has its own selection behavior, so the steps can feel slightly different from Preview. If the highlight does not appear, try a bigger zoom level before you give up.

## Copy text from a screenshot

Screenshots are the right route for text you can see but cannot save as a file. Think of web pages you cannot copy, chat windows, and frames from a video.

1. Press ⇧⌘4 and drag over the area that holds the text.
2. Find the saved screenshot. Open it in Preview.
3. Follow the Preview steps above.

The 2021 coverage says Live Text works in the Screenshot interface, the small preview that appears in the corner after you capture. You can try it there first. If the words do not select, open the saved file in Preview. That is the path Apple documents.

Zoom in before you capture. Small text in a shrunken window gives Live Text less to work with. Capture the window at a size where the text is large and sharp. A tiny text line from a full-screen capture often reads worse than the same line captured at full size.

## What to do when you can't select the text

When selection fails, check the usual suspects in this order. Most of them have a fix that takes under a minute.

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No Copy Text option appears | Live Text is off, or the language is unsupported | Turn on the Language & Region setting, then check Apple's feature availability page |
| Hover does nothing in Preview | The Text Selection tool is off | Choose Tools > Text Selection |
| Words highlight but read wrong | Blurry, small, or skewed image | Crop, zoom, or retake the photo flat and sharp |
| Handwriting comes out as junk | Live Text reads printed text more reliably | Retype the handwriting by hand |

The Tools > Text Selection menu item trips up many people. Apple's Preview guide says that if you can't select text, you should choose this item to turn on the Text Selection tool. Check it first, before you assume the setting is broken.

Live Text is picky about the image itself. A straight, well-lit photo works best. A phone photo of a menu taken at an angle, or a page photographed in dim light, gives you more misreads. Retake the photo if you can.

## Clean up the text after you paste it

Pasted text often carries extra line breaks, stray spaces, or odd symbols. Fix those before you send anything.

In most Mac apps, Edit > Paste and Match Style (⌥⇧⌘V) removes the source formatting and keeps your document's style. Use it when the pasted text looks like it came from a different document.

Then check the characters that OCR tends to confuse. The usual suspects are the number zero and the capital letter O, the digit one and the lowercase l, and the letter S and the number 5. Check every number, email address, and link against the original photo. If a line looks wrong, go back to the image and fix it by hand.

Here is a worked example. You photograph a restaurant receipt and need the total for an expense report. Copy only the total line, not the whole receipt. Paste it, then compare the digits to the photo. If the photo reads 42.50 and your paste reads 42.5O, the letter O slipped in. Fix it before the report goes out.

If the text lands in a spreadsheet, you are in a different job. In Numbers, use Find and Replace to fix the same error across a whole column. The steps are in [How to find and replace in Numbers on Mac](/how-to/find-and-replace-text-in-numbers). If you are placing the text into a page layout, [How to add a text box in Numbers on Mac](/how-to/add-a-text-box-in-numbers) shows the Text button route first.

## Live Text compared with other ways to get text from an image

You have more than one option. Each one fits a different job.

| Method | Works on | Best for | Main drawback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Preview, Copy Text | Image files you open in Preview | Pulling a paragraph out of a photo | Needs Live Text turned on, and no shortcut is documented |
| Photos, Command-C | Images in your Photos library | Quick copies from your library | Starts from Photos, not from a file in Finder |
| Screenshot, then Preview | Anything you can see on screen | Chats, web pages, video frames | Extra step, and quality depends on the capture |
| Retype by hand | Anything | Short text and handwriting | Slow, and typos creep in |
| Ask Lazur | Anything on screen | Hearing what a sign or label says | Lazur answers out loud, so you still copy the words yourself |

Retyping still wins for handwriting and for a few words. Live Text wins for a page of printed text, because it gets you the exact characters in seconds. Asking an assistant fits when you need the meaning of a label, not a file of the words.

## Mistakes people make with Live Text

These are the errors I see most often when people try this for the first time.

- **Turning off Live Text and forgetting.** Check the Language & Region setting whenever the menu disappears.
- **Trusting the output without a check.** Numbers and emails need a look against the photo.
- **Expecting Command-C in Preview.** Apple's Preview guide does not document it. Use the Control-click menu there.
- **Copying from a tiny screenshot.** Zoom in before you capture.
- **Assuming your language works.** Check Apple's feature availability page before you troubleshoot for an hour.
- **Pasting with the original formatting.** Use Paste and Match Style so the text matches your document.

## Privacy: what to check before you copy sensitive text

Apple's Live Text help pages I checked do not say where text recognition happens, or whether the image leaves your Mac. If you copy from contracts, medical forms, or ID cards, check Apple's privacy documentation before you rely on the feature.

Until you know, keep the source small. Crop the image to the lines you need. Delete screenshots you no longer need, and empty the Trash after you do. Those steps cost you nothing and shrink the amount of sensitive text sitting on your Mac.
