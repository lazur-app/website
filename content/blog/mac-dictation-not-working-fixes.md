If Mac dictation is not working, open System Settings > Keyboard, confirm Dictation is turned on, and click Enable if macOS asks. Most failures after that come from four things: the wrong language, a shortcut that never fires, a microphone the Mac does not hear, or Voice Control, which switches standard Dictation off.

This guide follows Apple's own troubleshooting order, then adds the fixes people use when the basics pass. It covers macOS 27 Golden Gate and the point releases around it. Each step tells you what to look for, so you can stop as soon as dictation works.

## Confirm Dictation is on and set to the right language

Start in System Settings > Keyboard. Find Dictation and turn it on. If macOS shows an Enable prompt, click it. Apple's Dictation help page describes this exact sequence. Skipping it is one of the most common reasons the shortcut does nothing.

Next, check the language. Dictation only transcribes the language you chose. If you speak English but the setting is on another language, you get strange output or nothing useful. Pick the language and region you actually speak. Apple's troubleshooting page lists the language and region as a core check for this reason.

Run a quick test. Open TextEdit, create a new document, click into the empty page, and press your Dictation shortcut. Say one short sentence. If words appear, the feature works and the problem sits in the app you were using. If nothing appears, keep going.

## Fix the shortcut so Dictation starts

Dictation usually starts from a key, not from a menu. Apple's Dictation page says that choosing a shortcut can change the "Press fn key to" or "Press Globe key to" setting, depending on your Mac model. So a shortcut you picked months ago may not match the key you press today.

Open System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation and read the Shortcut setting. Then read the line that describes it. If the setting says Press Fn Key Twice, you start Dictation by pressing fn twice. Apple notes that choosing Press Fn Key Twice switches the setting to Start Dictation (Press Fn Twice). If the setting says Press Fn Key or Press Globe Key, the key you press must match the label on your keyboard.

Three mistakes show up again and again:

- You press fn once, but the shortcut is set to double-press.
- Your keyboard has a Globe key, and you press a different key by habit.
- Another tool claims the same key. Two dictation tools, or a launcher, can both want fn. Turn one of them off, then test again.

Dictate into a text field, not a window with no cursor. Dictation types at the insertion point. If no cursor blinks in the field, the words have nowhere to go. That looks exactly like a failure, but the fix is to click first.

## Fix the microphone and input volume

If the shortcut works but nothing appears, the Mac may not hear you. Apple's troubleshooting page says to select the microphone in Sound settings and increase its input volume. Go to System Settings > Sound > Input.

Then check the Microphone source menu in Dictation settings. Apple explains that with Automatic, the Mac listens to the device you are most likely to use for Dictation. That guess goes wrong often. A Bluetooth headset that connects in the middle of a session can take over the input. A closed laptop can pick the wrong mic.

Pick the device by name. For a MacBook, that is usually the built-in microphone. For a headset, pick the headset. Speak at a normal volume and watch the level bar in Sound > Input. If the bar barely moves, raise the input volume slider. If it sits at the top the whole time, lower it, because clipped audio produces bad text.

Check permissions too. Open System Settings > Privacy & Security > Microphone and confirm the app you dictate into is allowed to use the microphone. An app without permission can receive silence, and the result looks like broken Dictation.

## Turn off Voice Control, which blocks standard Dictation

This one catches people who did nothing wrong. Apple's Voice Control help page says that when Voice Control is on, you use Voice Control to dictate text, and standard macOS Dictation is not available. If you turned on Voice Control for accessibility, or it switched on by accident, standard Dictation will not respond.

To check, open System Settings > Accessibility > Voice Control. If the switch is on, turn it off and test Dictation again. If you still want Voice Control, choose one tool for each job. Use Voice Control for commands and its own text entry, or use standard Dictation for writing. You cannot run standard Dictation while Voice Control is on.

## Check the network and the macOS 27 on-device model

Apple's troubleshooting page says Dictation may need an internet connection, and it points you to Network in System Settings. If your Mac is offline, or sits on a Wi-Fi network you have not signed into, Dictation can fail without a clear error. Open System Settings > Network and confirm the connection is active. Then load a page in Safari. If the page does not load, fix the network first.

macOS 27 adds one more condition. Apple says Dictation uses an on-device model to refine spelling, punctuation, and capitalization. That refinement is available in English on Macs with an M3 chip or later and at least 12GB of unified memory. MacRumors describes the same requirement and says the on-device model improves accuracy for systemwide dictation.

What this means in practice:

- On an older Mac, or a Mac with less memory, you do not get the refinement. Dictation can still work, but expect more cleanup in the text.
- If you dictate in a language other than English, the refinement does not apply, according to Apple's page.
- Check your chip and memory under Apple menu > About This Mac. Compare them with the requirement before you assume a bug.

Check your version too. MacRumors reports that macOS 27.2 was released on October 26, 2026. It also reports that the 27.3 public beta arrived on November 5, 2026. If you run a beta, Dictation may behave differently from the release build. Unless you run the beta on purpose, update from System Settings > General > Software Update.

## When Dictation stops by itself

Apple's page says Dictation has no length limit of its own. It stops automatically if no speech is detected for 30 seconds. You can also turn it off yourself. This matters when you pause to read a note or think through a sentence. The fix is to keep talking, or to press the shortcut again to start a fresh session.

Some vendor blogs describe shorter silence timeouts. The sources I could open do not confirm those, so use Apple's 30-second figure as the documented behavior. If Dictation cuts out after a few seconds, check the microphone level first. A weak signal can make the Mac hear silence, and then it stops sooner than you expect.

## Last-resort fixes: toggle, restart, clear the cache

When the settings look right and Dictation still fails, try these in order. Apple does not document these steps. They come from third-party guides, so treat them as common practice, not official repairs.

1. Turn Dictation off in System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation, wait about ten seconds, and turn it back on. MacPaw's guide suggests this step.
2. Restart the Mac. A restart can clear speech services that got stuck. It costs a minute, and it is worth trying before anything else on this list.
3. Clear the speech cache. MacPaw's guide suggests removing the folder at ~/Library/Caches/com.apple.SpeechRecognitionCore. Quit the apps you dictate into first. Open the folder with Go > Go to Folder (⇧⌘G), then move it to the Trash instead of deleting it. If the change does not help, you can put it back.

Some guides also suggest Terminal commands that restart speech services. Skip them. They are not on Apple's pages, and a command that stops system processes can cause more trouble than the original bug.

## Quick reference: symptom, likely cause, fix

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shortcut does nothing | Dictation is off, or the shortcut does not match the fn or Globe setting | Turn on Dictation in Keyboard settings, then match the shortcut to your key |
| Text appears in the wrong language | Language or region is wrong | Pick the language and region you speak |
| Nothing appears, no error shown | Wrong microphone source or input volume | Choose the mic by name, then watch the level bar in Sound > Input |
| Dictation never responds | Voice Control is on | Turn off Voice Control in System Settings > Accessibility |
| Dictation fails only sometimes | Network is down or a Wi-Fi portal is unsigned | Check Network, then load a web page |
| Dictation stops mid-thought | 30 seconds with no speech | Keep talking, or press the shortcut again |
| Still broken after all steps | Stuck speech service or cache | Toggle Dictation, restart, then move the cache to the Trash |

## Mistakes that make Dictation look broken

Most people fix the wrong thing first. A few habits cause most of the false alarms.

- Dictating into a window with no text field. Click in the document first.
- Forgetting that a headset or a dock changed the input device during the session.
- Testing in a loud room, or with the Mac fan running hard. Test in a quiet room.
- Changing five settings at once. Change one setting, then test. Otherwise you cannot tell which change worked.
- Assuming Dictation is broken when Voice Control is on. The Mac is doing what you told it to do.

The last habit wastes the most time. Keep a short note of each change. If the fix works, you know which setting did it.

## Is the fix worth the time?

When dictation works, it is fast. In a study of short messages on touchscreen phones, English speech entry ran 2.93 times faster than keyboard entry, at 153 WPM against 52 WPM. The authors ran these tests in laboratory conditions, and the speech text had slightly more uncorrected errors in the final output. Typing speed is a separate baseline. A sentence-copying test with about 168,000 online volunteers averaged 51.56 WPM on a computer keyboard, according to Laxis's summary of the research.

Use these numbers as direction, not as a promise. They come from phones and lab tests, not from a Mac at your desk. Your own speed depends on the text, your accent, and how much you edit afterward. If you want the comparison for your own work, time yourself on one email with each method.

## If Apple Dictation still fails

If every step above passes and Dictation still refuses to work, another tool may suit you better. Apple's Dictation page says that sharing audio with Apple is optional, and that Apple may review a sample of stored audio when you do share.
