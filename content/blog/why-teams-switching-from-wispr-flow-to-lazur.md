To switch from Wispr Flow to Lazur on Mac, install Lazur, settle which app owns the fn key, and run your real messages through both apps for one week before you cancel. Wispr Flow is a dictation product on Mac, Windows, iOS, and Android. Lazur is a Mac voice assistant with dictation included, so the switch makes most sense if you also want help with what is on your screen.

This guide covers what each product does, what it costs at list price, how to time your own edit work, and the steps for moving over. Every price and version number comes from the vendor pages and help articles linked below. Check them again on the day you buy, because plans change.

## Is Lazur a like-for-like replacement for Wispr Flow?

No. Wispr Flow is a dictation product, and its pricing page lists dictation on Mac, Windows, iOS, and Android. Its Free plan covers all four platforms, with a weekly word limit. [Check Wispr Flow's pricing page](https://wisprflow.ai/pricing) for current plan names before you decide.

Lazur is a voice assistant for the Mac, and dictation is one part of it. Hold fn to dictate clean text at your cursor in any app. Hold ⌥ ⌘ to ask about anything on your screen, and Lazur answers out loud.

The second key is the real difference. Lazur’s own Command Mode post puts it this way: “Dictation types what you said. Intent Mode does what you meant.” That is the vendor’s framing, not independent proof. Test it on your own writing before you rely on it. The [Command Mode post](https://www.lazur.app/blog/command-mode-voice-writing) has the examples.

Check the platform first. Lazur runs on macOS only, on Apple silicon and Intel Macs. Windows is planned, not available. If you dictate on a Windows PC or an iPhone, Lazur will not cover that part of your day, so you will keep Wispr Flow for it.

Read the table by job, not by feature count. If most of your day is email, Slack, and docs on one Mac, dictation quality decides the choice. If you often hunt for settings in apps you rarely open, screen help decides it.

Here is how screen help works. Ask where something is, and Lazur draws a circle and a label around it. Ask how to do something, and it walks you through one click at a time, waiting for each click. You do the clicking. That is the main reason to pick it over a dictation-only tool.

Stay with Wispr Flow if one of these is true:

- You dictate on Windows, iPhone, or Android as well as on a Mac.
- Your organization already runs Wispr Flow's Growth or Enterprise plan through an admin portal.
- You only want dictation, and screen help would sit unused.

## What the switch costs each month

Compare monthly list prices, then decide whether you will use the extra job. Wispr Flow Pro costs $15 per user per month when billed monthly, or $12 per user per month when billed annually. Lazur Pro costs $18 per month when billed monthly, or $15 per month when billed annually, with 150 AI actions.

On both billing options, Lazur is the higher list price. Whether that gap is worth it depends on how often you ask for help. Count how many times last week you needed help finding a setting, a menu item, or a button. If the count is close to zero, the cheaper plan is the better fit.

Three pricing details change the math:

- **The Lazur trial needs no card.** It starts the first time you open the app. Do not open it until test day, or the trial clock runs while you wait.
- **Wispr Flow's Free limit is not published.** Its pricing page says “limited words per week” with no number. Do not budget around a figure from a third-party blog.
- **The 150 AI actions are the capped part of Lazur Pro.** Dictation is listed as unlimited. Check the pricing page for how the two fit together before you commit.

Dictation users and assistant users pay for different things. If you only dictate, you pay more for a feature you may skip. If you ask screen questions most days, the extra cost is easier to justify.

## How to measure your own edit time before you switch

Speed claims on marketing pages mislead. Edit time is what you feel every day, so measure your own messages instead of a demo.

The best published test I found is a study by Ruan and colleagues, posted as arXiv:1608.07323. It compared speech and keyboard entry for short messages on touchscreen devices, in English and Mandarin. English speech averaged 153 words per minute. Keyboard typing averaged 52. That is 2.93 times faster.

Accuracy went the other way. During entry, speech had a 5.30% corrected error rate, against 11.22% for the keyboard. In the final text, speech had a 1.30% uncorrected error rate, against 0.79% for the keyboard. The study is from 2016, revised in 2018, and it used touchscreen phones, not Macs. Treat it as a rough guide, not a verdict.

Here is what those figures mean for one message. Take a 150-word Slack update.

- Typing at 52 words per minute takes about 2.9 minutes.
- Speaking at 153 words per minute takes about 1 minute.
- At the study’s 1.30% uncorrected rate, about two words in that 150 could still need a fix.

Speaking wins the drafting step. Proofreading still counts, so time the whole job, not only the first draft.

Run this test for one week:

1. Pick ten real messages. Use a mix of replies, Slack updates, short notes, and one document section.
2. Start a timer when you begin. Stop it when the text is ready to send.
3. Log the seconds and the number of fixes for each message.
4. Repeat the same kinds of message in the other app, on the same days if you can.

Compare the two totals and the fix counts. Ignore your best day. Find your typing speed with the [typing speed test](/tools/typing-speed-test). Then check your dictation accuracy with the [dictation accuracy checker](/tools/dictation-accuracy-checker), using one of your test messages.

## Step-by-step: switch from Wispr Flow to Lazur on Mac

Follow the order. Step 3 is the one you cannot undo.

1. **Copy your vocabulary first.** Deleting a Wispr Flow account removes local and cloud data, including transcripts, audio, and usage history. Paste your names, product terms, and common phrases into a note before you delete anything.

2. **Check how you pay.** On Mac, open Wispr Flow and go to Settings > Plans and Billing > Manage subscription. That opens Stripe’s portal. Team, Growth, and Enterprise plans use Manage billing in the admin portal instead. If you subscribed through the App Store or Google Play, cancel there, because deleting your account does not cancel those.

3. **Keep Wispr Flow until the test ends.** Deleting a personal account cancels its Stripe subscription right away, with no refund. Do not delete the account during your test week. Delete it only after you have decided.

4. **Install Lazur and open it on test day.** Get the app from the [download page](/download). The 3-day Pro trial starts the first time you open it, and no credit card is needed.

5. **Grant the permissions.** Lazur listens and looks at your screen only while you hold the shortcut. Allow microphone access when macOS asks. Allow screen recording if you want screen questions. You can review both under System Settings > Privacy & Security.

6. **Settle the shared key.** Lazur dictates while you hold fn. Open System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation and check the Dictation shortcut. If it also uses fn, change one of them. Quit Wispr Flow during the test, so two dictation apps never listen for the same key.

7. **Learn the renamed Wispr settings.** If you keep Wispr Flow for comparison, note its new names. Smart Formatting became Auto Cleanup on April 24, with four levels under the Style tab. Flow Notes became Scratchpad on May 1. The current desktop build is Flow 1.6.1324, from the October 30, 2026 changelog entry.

8. **Run the ten-message test for a week.** Use Lazur for email, Slack, notes, and one document. Keep Wispr Flow installed until both totals are in. Cancel it only after you have decided.

## Privacy: what leaves your Mac with each app

Lazur listens and looks only while you hold the shortcut. Audio and the screenshot go to AI providers so they can answer, and they are not stored. Lazur says its content is never used to train AI models. The [Lazur home page](https://www.lazur.app/) covers the basics.

Wispr Flow’s changelog says model training is off for Growth and Enterprise organizations by org policy, starting October 2. That is an admin setting for teams. The pages I checked do not describe personal plans the same way, so read Wispr Flow’s privacy terms before you pay.

If you want the least outside processing, Apple’s own dictation is one option. On macOS 27 with Apple Intelligence, dictation uses an on-device model for spelling, punctuation, and capitalization. That needs an M3 or later Mac with at least 12GB of unified memory, and it works in English only. Keyboard settings show whether transcripts are processed on device. Apple’s Dictation also stops after 30 seconds without speech. You can start it from Edit > Start Dictation, the Microphone key, or the shortcut you set in System Settings > Keyboard. The [Apple dictation guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/use-dictation-mh40584/mac) has the details.

## Mistakes people make when switching

- **Judging on day one.** The first session feels slow because the key and the commands are new. Give it three working days before you decide.
- **Cancelling before the test ends.** Keep both apps until your timings and fix counts show a clear winner.
- **Forgetting the store subscription.** An App Store or Google Play plan keeps billing after you delete the account. Check your Apple or Google subscriptions once you leave.
- **Losing your vocabulary.** Add your names and terms to Lazur’s personal dictionary, using the list you copied in step one.
- **Expecting clicks.** Lazur points and guides. It does not do the clicking for you.
- **Forgetting Windows users.** If one teammate works on Windows, Lazur is not an option for that person.

## Team rollouts and pilots

A short pilot works better than a memo. Wispr Flow’s changelog lists self-serve Growth plans for teams from August 28, and a 14-day team trial for new members from August 21. Confirm both on its pricing page before you plan around them.

I could not find team terms on Lazur’s pricing page. Ask the vendor directly before you plan a rollout.

For a pilot, pick three people for one week. Have each run the ten-message test. Then check the Macs. Lazur needs macOS on Apple silicon or Intel. Apple’s on-device dictation needs an M3 or later with 12GB or more.

## Where Lazur fits, and what to do next

Dictation is the part most people try first. Lazur also handles everyday voice actions. Those include timers, Apple Reminders, Calendar events, notes, music and volume, opening apps and links, web search, and Shortcuts. Research runs in Lazur’s own Chrome tabs through the Lazur for Chrome extension, then comes back with a summary and a report.

Here is an example. Say you need an export option in an app you open twice a year. Hold ⌥ ⌘ and ask where it is. Lazur circles the control and labels it. You click it. If you get stuck, ask Lazur to walk you through the rest, one click at a time.

Know the limit before you switch. Lazur does not click buttons, edit files, or operate other apps for you. It is not a plugin or add-in for any app. If you want a tool that finishes tasks without you clicking, this is not that tool.
