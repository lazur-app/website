You can use dictation on Google Docs by opening your document in a browser and selecting Tools > Voice typing, or by using the macOS system-wide dictation feature. While both methods allow you to enter text by voice, they offer different levels of control and formatting capabilities for your workflow.

## Understanding Built-in Google Docs Voice Typing

Google Docs includes a native voice typing tool that is surprisingly capable for basic tasks. To access it, open your document in Chrome, Edge, or Safari and navigate to the Tools menu. From there, select Voice typing. You can also trigger this quickly by pressing ⌘ + Shift + S. Once the microphone icon appears, click it to start speaking.

This tool is unique because it understands specific formatting commands. You can say things like 'bold that,' 'select paragraph,' or 'go to end of line' to manipulate your text without touching the mouse. This is a significant advantage if you are drafting a document and want to keep your hands on the keyboard for quick edits. Note that these commands are currently restricted to English. If you try to use them in other languages, the tool will likely transcribe your commands as literal text rather than executing them.

One major limitation is the browser-based nature of this feature. Because it lives inside the Google Docs tab, it stops recording the moment you switch to another window. If you frequently jump between Slack, your email, and your document, you will find yourself constantly restarting the microphone. This friction can break your flow and make the tool feel like a hindrance rather than a helper.

## Using macOS System Dictation

If you prefer a more consistent experience, you can use the built-in macOS dictation system. You enable this in System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation. Once turned on, you can trigger it in any application by pressing the shortcut you assigned, such as the Fn key or a double-tap of the Control key.

Unlike the Google Docs tool, this version is system-wide. It does not care which app you are using or which tab is active. You can dictate into Google Docs, then switch to your email client to reply to a message, and then return to your document without ever turning the microphone off. This creates a much more fluid writing environment for those who work across multiple apps.

However, there is a trade-off. macOS dictation is designed for raw transcription. It does not recognize the formatting commands like 'bold that' or 'create a list' that Google Docs provides. You will get clean text, but you will still need to handle the formatting manually. For many users, this is a fair trade for the reliability of a system-wide tool.

## The Role of AI-Powered Dictation Apps

Third-party AI dictation tools have emerged to bridge the gap between simple transcription and intelligent writing assistance. Apps like NovaVoice or various Whisper-based clients take a different approach. They function as an overlay, capturing your voice and processing it through an AI model before the text ever hits your document.

These apps often excel at cleaning up your speech. They can automatically remove filler words like 'um' or 'uh' and apply correct punctuation as you speak. This results in a much more polished first draft compared to the raw output of built-in dictation. Furthermore, many of these apps include a personal dictionary. If you work in a field with specialized jargon or unique names, you can add them to the dictionary to ensure the AI gets them right every time.

Using these tools often feels more natural. Because they are designed to understand the intent behind your speech, you spend less time correcting errors after the fact. While they may require a subscription, the productivity gains for heavy writers are often worth the cost. You are trading a small monthly fee for hours of saved editing time.

## Stats on Productivity and Dictation

It is helpful to look at the numbers to understand why voice input is becoming popular. The average person types at about 40 words per minute. In contrast, the average person speaks at 125 to 150 words per minute. This difference is the primary driver of the efficiency gains seen by voice users.

Studies suggest that most people see a net productivity gain of 2 to 2.5 times over typing. Even when you factor in the time spent correcting minor errors, the speed of speech is difficult to match with fingers on keys. For a writer producing 3,000 words per day, this can save roughly 45 minutes every single day. Over the course of a year, that adds up to approximately 275 hours of saved time.

Despite these benefits, surveys indicate that only about 25 percent of people currently use voice input with AI tools. The technology is available, but many people have not yet integrated it into their daily habits. If you are looking to increase your output, shifting some of your writing tasks to voice is a clear path forward.

## Choosing the Right Tool for Your Workflow

Deciding which tool to use depends on your specific needs. If you work exclusively in Google Docs and need to perform complex formatting while you talk, the built-in tool is your best option. It is free, integrated, and handles commands well.

If you find yourself writing across many different apps and value a consistent shortcut that never breaks, the macOS system-wide dictation is superior. It is reliable and works everywhere, even if it lacks the 'smart' features of the Google Docs tool.

If you are a professional writer or someone who dictates long-form content, consider a dedicated AI dictation app. The ability to automatically clean up your speech and maintain a personal dictionary can save you significant time in the editing phase. The initial setup time to train your dictionary is a small price to pay for the long-term gains in text quality.

## Common Mistakes When Dictating

One of the most common mistakes is speaking too quickly or unclearly. AI models are good, but they are not perfect. If you find that your text is riddled with errors, try slowing down your pace. Focus on enunciating your words clearly, especially when you are using technical terms or proper nouns.

Another mistake is ignoring the environment. Background noise is the enemy of accurate dictation. If you are working in a busy cafe, the microphone will pick up the clatter of dishes and other conversations, which can confuse the transcription engine. Use a noise-canceling microphone or find a quiet space to record your thoughts. Even a simple pair of wired headphones with a decent microphone can perform better than the built-in laptop microphone in a noisy room.

Finally, do not expect perfection on the first try. Dictation is a skill that improves with practice. You will get better at phrasing your thoughts for the AI to understand, and the AI will get better at recognizing your unique speech patterns over time. Give yourself a few days to adjust to the workflow before deciding if it is right for you.
