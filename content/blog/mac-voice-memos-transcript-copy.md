To transcribe a Voice Memo on Mac, open the recording in Voice Memos and click the Transcription button, then select the text, Control-click it, and choose Copy. If the button is missing, your region, your Mac, or your language may not support transcription, so this guide also covers the fixes.

## How to transcribe a Voice Memo on Mac

Voice Memos shows a transcript at two points. The first is during a recording. The second is after you stop.

During a recording, click the Transcription button to see the text as you speak. The waveform disappears, and the word being spoken gets highlighted. Click the button again to go back to the waveform. That toggle helps when you want to watch the text and the audio at the same time.

After a recording, click the memo in your list, then click the Transcription button. The transcript replaces the waveform in the same window. From there you can read it, scroll it, or copy the parts you need.

Most people expect the text to appear on its own. For new recordings, it doesn't. You have to click the button each time, which is easy to forget when you're in a hurry.

Here is a realistic case. Say you record a ten-minute call with a supplier. You don't need the text during the call, so leave the waveform on screen. When you hang up, open the memo, click Transcription, and skim for the price and the delivery date. Copy only those lines into your notes. That's faster than replaying the whole call.

One limit to plan for. Apple's Voice Memos guide doesn't describe speaker labels in the transcript. If two people talk, don't count on the text showing who said what. Mark the speakers yourself in the document you paste into.

## How to copy a Voice Memos transcript into another app

Apple's steps take four moves. Select the words you want. Control-click the selection. Choose Copy. Then paste into the app you need. Apple's examples are Mail and a plain text file.

Select only what you need. A full transcript pasted into an email is hard to read, and a short excerpt is easier to check. The table below shows three common destinations and what to check in each.

| Where you paste | Good for | Check before you use it |
|---|---|---|
| Mail | Sending a quote or a summary to a colleague | Names, numbers, and dates, since other people will rely on them |
| TextEdit or another plain text file | Keeping a searchable archive of memos | Line breaks, which may need cleaning up |
| Pages | Building a document from the transcript | Headings and formatting, which you add yourself. See [AI assistant for Pages on Mac](/apps/pages) for voice help in Pages. |

The standard ⌘C shortcut copies selected text in most Mac apps, so it's worth trying after you select. Apple's guide describes the Control-click route, so use that if you want to follow Apple's exact steps.

Apple's Voice Memos guide doesn't describe a file export for the transcript. The documented route is copy and paste. If you need a .txt or .docx file, paste the text into a document and save it there. Plan around that instead of waiting for an export option.

## A worked example: one interview, start to finish

Take a single interview you recorded for a short article. It runs about forty minutes, and the speaker talks about pricing, a product launch, and a hiring plan. You want the pricing quotes and nothing else.

Start by opening the memo and clicking the Transcription button. Scroll to the first mention of price. Read two or three lines around it, so you know the context. Then select the quote, Control-click it, and choose Copy. Paste it into a document with the time you think it came from, which you can check later with the playhead.

Next, search the memo for the word price. The search field finds the memo, and the transcript shows each match. Select the first match and listen to the audio at that point. If the transcript says three dollars and the speaker said thirty, fix your copy. That single check catches the errors that cost you the most.

Finally, repeat the search for the speaker's company name, which appears often and is easy to misspell. Save the document before you do anything else. Only then should you think about deleting the memo, and only if the article is done.

## How to search and check a transcript

The Voice Memos search field matches recordings whose title or transcript contains your term. That means a memo called Call 3 still shows up when you search for a client's name that was said inside it. Apple's guide confirms this behavior.

Selecting a term in the transcript moves the playhead to that point in the waveform. Use it as a check. Say the transcript shows Ortiz, and you're not sure of the spelling. Select the word, listen at that point, and fix your copy if the audio says something different.

Here is a worked example. You record six interviews for one article. Search for budget, and every memo with that word in its transcript appears. You can jump straight to each mention, listen, and compare it with your notes.

Search has a limit. It only finds what the transcript got right. If the transcription heard Ortiz as or tees, searching for Ortiz finds nothing. Try a shorter fragment of the word too, and read the transcript around any name you care about.

## Check a transcript quickly

Work through these steps before you send or publish anything.

1. Play the first thirty seconds while you read along. This tells you whether the transcript is close enough to trust for the rest of the memo.
2. Select each name, number, and date, and listen at that point in the audio.
3. Fix errors in the document you paste into. Apple's guide doesn't describe editing the transcript inside Voice Memos, so don't plan on it.
4. Search the memo for the names you care about. This catches repeats the transcript may have spelled differently.
5. Save the final text before you delete the recording. The audio is your only check if a sentence still looks wrong later.

## Why Voice Memos transcription isn't offered

Three things commonly explain a missing button. Check them in order. The first one is the fastest to rule out.

Your region comes first. Apple's Voice Memos guide says audio transcription isn't available in all countries or regions. If you live in an unsupported region, the feature may not appear, and Apple's guide offers no workaround.

Your Mac or your language comes second. Apple's Notes guide says the Transcription button goes missing when the Mac doesn't meet the minimum requirements or doesn't detect a supported language. Apple's Voice Memos pages don't give a minimum macOS version or chip. The Notes guide is the closest Apple statement, and it says transcription needs an M1 chip or later. To check your chip, click the Apple menu > About This Mac.

Your recording's age comes third. Apple says that if you open a recording made in macOS 14 or earlier, or on a device that doesn't support transcription, Voice Memos transcribes it automatically when it contains speech. An old memo without a transcript may just need a moment. A silent memo gets no transcript, and that's normal.

The table shows what each symptom usually means.

| What you see | Likely cause | What to do next |
|---|---|---|
| No Transcription button at all | Mac below the minimum, unsupported language, or unsupported region | Check your chip under Apple menu > About This Mac, then try Notes |
| An old memo has no transcript | Made on macOS 14 or earlier, or on a device without transcription | Open the memo and wait, then confirm it contains speech |
| Transcript in the wrong language | Language not detected or not supported | Re-record in a supported language, and check the Notes list below |
| Summary option is missing | Apple says transcript summaries aren't available in all languages or regions on macOS 27 | Check your language and region before you look for it again |

Apple's Voice Memos guide offers versions for macOS 27, macOS Tahoe 26, and macOS Sequoia 15. Match the guide version to your system before you follow its steps.

## Notes as a backup for transcription on Mac

If Voice Memos has no button, Apple Notes is the next place to try. Apple documents audio transcription in Notes on Mac computers with M1 or later. Its guide, Record and transcribe audio in Notes on Mac, lists ten language entries.

Those entries are English, with localized versions for Australia, Canada, New Zealand, South Africa, the UK, and the United States. They also include Spanish, Portuguese, Italian, French, German, Japanese, Korean, Simplified Chinese, and Traditional Chinese.

Voice Memos doesn't publish a language list of its own. Treat the Notes list as the best Apple statement you have. Then test your language with a short recording of a few sentences before you rely on the feature for an important interview.

If Notes shows no Transcription button either, the same causes apply. Your region, your chip, or your language is the likely issue. Search Apple's Notes guide for record and transcribe audio to find the exact steps for your version.

## Pick the right route for your memo

Not every memo needs the same route. Use this rule of thumb to decide where the words should come from.

- A memo you recorded in Voice Memos, on a supported Mac in a supported region: click Transcription, then copy the lines you need.
- An old memo from macOS 14 or earlier: open it and wait for the automatic transcript.
- A new memo with no button: check your chip and region, then record a short test in Apple Notes.
- A recording in a language the Notes list doesn't show: plan for manual work, since Apple's guides don't say what happens with unlisted languages.

The route matters most for work you hand to someone else. A transcript you paste into a client email carries your name, so check it twice. A transcript you keep for yourself can be rougher, as long as search helps you find the parts you need.

A pattern that works well for meetings goes like this. Record in Voice Memos during the meeting. Transcribe after the call. Paste the excerpt you need into your notes. Delete the raw memo only when you're finished with the audio, because the recording is the only proof of what was said.

## Common mistakes with Voice Memos transcripts

Trusting the first draft is the biggest one. Apple's support pages don't cover accuracy. Check names, numbers, and dates against the audio before you send anything. The playhead trick from the search section is the fastest way to do that.

Deleting the memo too early is the second. Copy the text into a document first, then delete the recording only after the text is final. The audio is the only record of what was said.

Expecting a button on every Mac is the third. Check the three causes above before you decide the Mac is broken. Most missing buttons come down to region, chip, or language.

Recording in a noisy room is the fourth. Background noise makes speech harder to transcribe, and no setting cleans it up after the fact. Move closer to the speaker, or pick a quieter room before you hit record.

Assuming privacy is the fifth. Apple's Voice Memos guide doesn't say where transcription runs. Don't record a sensitive interview until you know where the text is processed.

Assuming a summary is included is the last. Apple says features such as summarizing transcripts aren't available in all languages or regions on macOS 27. Check before you plan your workflow around one.
