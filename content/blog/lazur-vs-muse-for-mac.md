Muse for Mac is Meta's agent for handing whole tasks across your files and Apple apps, and Lazur is an AI buddy that helps while you work. Choose Muse when you want a job finished across your personal data, and choose Lazur when you want live help in the app in front of you.

Both tools run on a Mac and both use AI, but they answer different questions. This page compares them using public sources only: Meta's launch coverage, Meta's help center, and Lazur's own website and privacy policy. We make Lazur, so read the Lazur sections with that in mind. Where a detail is unclear or unverified, we say so.

## What is Muse for Mac?

Muse is Meta's personal agent. Meta released the Mac app in September 2026, and TechCrunch covered the launch on September 18. On the Mac, TechCrunch reports that Muse \"can interact with your files, messages, calendar, notes, and mail, all within their native applications.\"

That access is the point. You ask Muse to do a job, such as sorting files, pulling dates from your calendar into a note, or drafting a reply from a message thread. It works across those apps to finish the job. Muse is built to take actions, not only to answer questions.

Meta also says it asks first. TechCrunch reports that \"the app will always ask for your approval before it performs sensitive actions, Meta says.\" It also reports that you opt in to what Muse can access. In coverage by 9to5Mac, Zuckerberg wrote \"You control what it can access.\" Read those permission prompts when they appear. They are your main brake on what the agent does.

The Mac app is also a window onto a cloud service. Macworld's reading of Meta's setup is that \"the actual agent itself and all the models it uses run on Meta's servers in the cloud.\" Meta's open-weights model, Muse Spark Open, can run on Apple silicon. Macworld says that takes two high-memory Mac Studios, not a typical Mac. For most people, Muse runs in the cloud.

## What is Lazur?

Lazur is a voice assistant for the Mac that lives in the notch at the top of your screen. Hold ⌥⌘, the Option and Command keys together, and ask about anything on screen. Lazur answers out loud. It looks at your screen only while you hold the shortcut.

It does four jobs that matter for this comparison:

- **It answers.** Ask what an error message means or what a setting does, and you hear the answer.
- **It points.** Ask where something is, and Lazur draws a circle and a label around it on your screen.
- **It guides.** Ask how to do something, and Lazur walks you through it one click at a time. It waits for each click before the next step.
- **It handles everyday tasks by voice.** Examples include timers, Apple Reminders, Calendar events, notes, music and volume, opening apps and links, web search and Shortcuts.

Lazur also dictates. Hold fn and speak, and clean, formatted text lands at your cursor in any app. You can add snippets, a personal dictionary and clipboard slots.

Lazur does not click buttons, edit files, or operate other apps for you. It is not a plugin for any app. That limit shapes most of the comparison below.

## Muse is an agent, Lazur is a buddy

The simplest way to split them is by who does the work. With Muse, you hand off the job and check the result. With Lazur, you keep doing the job and ask for help when you hit a snag.

Take a common Friday afternoon. You need to send a status update to a client. The notes are in Notes, the meeting is in Calendar, and the thread is in Mail. Muse is built for that kind of request. It gathers context across apps, drafts the result, and asks for approval before anything sensitive happens.

Now take a different afternoon. You are in Xcode, and a build error points to a line you do not understand. You want to know what the message means and where the fix usually lives. Hold ⌥⌘ and ask. Lazur answers out loud, and it can circle the menu or panel you need. You still make the change. For more on that workflow, see [Lazur in Xcode](/apps/xcode).

A rule of thumb helps here. If the job is \"do this for me across my stuff,\" start with Muse. If the job is \"help me do this in front of me,\" start with Lazur.

## Where Lazur is the better fit

Lazur suits a few kinds of day better than Muse does.

- **You are learning a new app.** Ask where the export option lives, and Lazur circles it. For a longer walkthrough, ask Lazur to guide you one click at a time.
- **You are stuck on a dialog or a setting.** You get a spoken answer without leaving the window you are in.
- **You want quick commands by voice.** Set a timer, add a reminder or open a link without switching context.
- **You type all day.** Hold fn and talk. Dictation is unlimited on both paid plans, so the cost does not climb with use.

Lazur is a weaker choice when you want it to handle work across your mail and messages on its own. It will tell you how, and point you to the right spot. Then you do the click.

A common mistake is to expect Lazur to do the clicking for you. It won't. If you want an agent that acts, Muse is the category Meta is pitching.

## Where Muse is the better fit

Muse suits a different set of jobs.

- **One request touches several Apple apps.** Examples include a reply that needs a calendar check, a note and an email draft.
- **You want a cloud agent to work through your personal data.** You can start a task, then go do something else while it runs.
- **You already use Muse on your phone.** 9to5Mac reports the Muse iPhone app is only available in the US for now. Muse on the Mac is the natural companion, if it is available where you live.

Muse's launch coverage talks about actions and approvals. It says little about walking you through an unfamiliar app. Do not judge it on that. Judge it on whether it finishes the jobs you give it.

## How to test both in one afternoon

You can learn more in an afternoon than from any comparison. Use real work, not demo prompts.

1. Pick one task that spans several apps, such as collecting notes and writing a reply. Run it through Muse. Count how many approval prompts you see and how long the result takes to check.
2. Pick one snag from an app you use daily. Hold ⌥⌘ in Lazur and ask about it. Note whether the spoken answer, or the circle on screen, got you moving faster.
3. Write down which tool saved time for each job. If the answer is split, that is your answer. Use both.

Keep notes as you go. A short list is enough. The goal is to see which tool fits your real day, not which one has the bigger launch.

## Privacy: where each one sends your data

Privacy is where the two designs differ most. Lazur's privacy policy, last updated October 30, 2026, says the microphone listens only while the shortcut is held. It says Lazur \"never records or watches in the background.\"

Lazur's policy also says audio goes to Groq and OpenAI for transcription, and voice recordings are not kept on its servers. When you ask about the screen, one screenshot goes to Google's Gemini. The policy says screenshots are not kept after processing. Dictation and command history stays on your Mac by default, and cloud history is optional.

Lazur also uses analytics. The policy names PostHog, and it lists Google Analytics and DataFast on the website. If that matters to you, read the policy before you install.

Muse works differently. The agent and its models run on Meta's servers, according to Macworld. Once you opt in, Muse can reach your files, messages, calendar, notes and mail. The sources behind this page do not say how long Meta keeps that data. Read Meta's terms before you grant access.

macOS adds its own layer. The first time an app reads your screen or records audio, macOS asks for permission. You can review those grants under System Settings > Privacy & Security. Check the list now, and remove anything you no longer use.

Three questions cover most privacy decisions for any AI assistant. Where does the AI run? What leaves your Mac? What does the company keep? Ask those questions of any tool before you install it, not only these two.

## Pricing and usage limits

Lazur has a 3-day Pro trial with no credit card. The trial starts when you first open the app. After that, Pro costs $18/month, or $15/month billed annually, and includes 250 AI actions a month. Power costs $120/month, or $96/month annually, and includes 1,000 AI actions a month. Dictation is unlimited on both plans. Current plan details are on the [pricing page](/pricing).

Muse is free to start, with weekly usage limits. Meta's help page says Muse is free with a usage limit, but it does not state the free cap. When you hit the limit, you can upgrade or wait. Meta gives no schedule for the allowance to refresh.

The paid tiers in Meta's help center are Power at $20/month, with 500M Muse tokens per week, and Maximum at $100/month, with 3B Muse tokens per week. Meta also says its allowance increases as you upgrade. Muse and its subscriptions are in limited testing and are not available in all locations yet.

Do not compare the two prices as if they measure the same thing. Lazur counts AI actions. Muse counts tokens. A busy week of quick questions uses up a monthly action count. A long agent task can use a large share of a weekly token allowance. Check each provider's current plan page before you commit.

Search results for \"Muse Mac\" can surface other apps with Muse in the name. Before you download, check the publisher. On this page, Muse means Meta's app.

Lazur has a lookalike too. Lazzy AI, at getlazzy.app, is a separate floating macOS AI agent. It connects to many models and has a free tier. It is not the same product as Lazur. Check the publisher and the website address before you install.

## Mistakes people make when choosing

These are the errors that cause the wrong purchase.

1. **Treating them as the same kind of tool.** Muse acts across your apps with approvals. Lazur answers, points and guides, and you do the work.
2. **Expecting Lazur to operate apps.** It does not click buttons, edit files or run other apps for you.
3. **Assuming Muse runs on your Mac.** Macworld reports the agent and its models run in Meta's cloud.
4. **Assuming Muse is available everywhere.** Meta says it is in limited testing and not in all locations.
5. **Comparing prices by the number alone.** Actions and tokens are different units, so $18 and $20 do not buy the same thing.

If you avoid these five, the choice usually gets easy.

## Can you use both?

Yes. Nothing in the public sources says the two conflict. Use Muse for long jobs across your personal data. Use Lazur for quick questions and dictation while you work.

The cost is the catch. Two tools mean two sets of usage limits to track. Set a rough budget for each before you rely on either one for daily work.

If ChatGPT is your default assistant, compare it on its own terms first. See [ChatGPT on Mac vs Lazur](/apps/chatgpt) for how the two divide the work.
