The dictation button on an Apple keyboard is typically found as a microphone icon on the F5 key or triggered by pressing the Fn or Globe key twice. You can use this feature to turn your speech into text in almost any application on your Mac. Modern Apple keyboards provide specific ways to trigger this feature, even if they do not have a dedicated button labeled for it. Understanding these triggers will help you switch between typing and speaking without losing your train of thought. ## Locating the Dictation Button on Your Keyboard The placement of the dictation function depends on your specific keyboard model. On newer MacBooks released in 2021 or later, look at the top row of your keyboard. You will see a function key, often F5, which features a small microphone icon. This is your dedicated dictation trigger. A single press and release of this key will start the listening process. If your keyboard is an older model or a compact Magic Keyboard without the microphone icon, you have other options. The default behavior for most modern Macs is to trigger dictation by pressing the Fn key or the Globe key twice in quick succession. This action mimics the dedicated button and activates the same system service. If you are unsure which key is your Fn or Globe key, look for the globe symbol on the bottom left of your keyboard. ## How to Enable and Configure Dictation Settings Before you can use the dictation button, you must ensure the feature is enabled in your system settings. Open the Apple menu and select System Settings. If you are on an older version of macOS, this will be labeled System Preferences. Navigate to the Keyboard section in the sidebar. Look for the Dictation heading. You will see a toggle switch to turn it on. Once enabled, you can customize the trigger. Click the Shortcut pop-up menu to choose a different key combination if the default does not suit your workflow. You can also select your preferred language here. Apple Dictation supports over 60 languages and dialects. This broad support makes it accessible for users across different regions. ## Managing Dictation Performance and Efficiency Research suggests that using a dictation shortcut can save heavy typers over 30 minutes per day. Over the course of a year, this could add up to more than 90 hours of saved time. These efficiency gains come from the ability to speak faster than you can type. When you start dictation, your Mac listens for your voice and converts it to text in real time. The system does not have a hard time limit. It will continue to transcribe as long as you are speaking. It only stops automatically after 30 seconds of silence. If you need to pause, you can simply stop speaking. The system will wait for your next input. ## Privacy and Processing on Apple Silicon For users concerned about privacy, Apple has integrated on-device processing for Macs with Apple Silicon. This means your voice data is processed locally on your device. It does not need an internet connection to function. This approach enhances your privacy and ensures that your dictation remains on your machine. The system is designed to be fast and responsive, regardless of your network status. ## Troubleshooting Common Dictation Issues If your dictation shortcut stops working, the first thing to check is your Accessibility settings. Ensure that Voice Control is not enabled. Voice Control and standard Dictation cannot run simultaneously. If both are toggled on, the system may prioritize one over the other, causing your shortcut to fail. Another common issue is the accidental triggering of Siri. If you press the microphone key and Siri appears, you are likely holding the key down too long. A quick, light tap is all that is required. If you prefer to avoid Siri entirely, you can adjust your Siri settings in the same System Settings menu. ## Integrating Dictation into Your Workflow You can continue to type on your keyboard while dictation is active. The cursor will pause during typing and resume when you stop. This allows you to manually fix words without ending the dictation session. This flexibility is useful for long-form writing or drafting emails. You can also use the menu bar to start dictation manually. Select Edit > Start Dictation in most apps to begin. This is a good fallback if your keyboard shortcuts are not working. This feature is enabled by default. If you find it adds punctuation where you do not want it, you can disable Auto-punctuation in the Dictation settings. You can also dictate specific punctuation marks by saying them out loud. For example, saying "comma" or "period" will insert the character into your text. This manual control gives you precision when you need it. ## Comparing Dictation Options For a deeper look at how Apple's built-in tools compare to third-party options, you can read our comparison on [Apple Dictation alternatives for Mac in 2026](/alternatives/apple-dictation).
