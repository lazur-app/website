If Dictation on your Mac is failing, it is rarely due to a single broken component. Instead, it is typically a configuration conflict, a permission issue, or a temporary system state. When your Mac ignores your voice or fails to type what you say, the cause is often hidden in a setting you changed months ago or a background process that has stopped responding. This guide walks you through the systematic steps to restore your voice input capabilities.

## Quick Diagnostic Checklist

Before you dive into complex system settings or terminal commands, perform these three basic tests to isolate the problem. These steps help determine if the issue is global or app-specific.

First, open a fresh document in TextEdit. Try to use Dictation there. If it works in TextEdit but fails in your primary application like Slack or Chrome, the issue is almost certainly microphone permissions. macOS manages microphone access on a per-app basis. If you have not granted specific permission to that app, it cannot "hear" your voice input.

Second, check your trigger. If nothing happens when you press your shortcut key, try starting the process manually. Go to the menu bar of your active app, select Edit, and click Start Dictation. If this works, your keyboard shortcut is likely conflicting with another application. Many productivity tools use global shortcuts that can intercept your keystrokes.

Third, toggle the feature. Go to System Settings, select Keyboard, and find the Dictation section. Turn it off, wait ten seconds, and turn it back on. This simple action clears most temporary stuck states within the macOS speech recognition engine.

## Common Causes and Solutions

Identifying the root cause requires checking several layers of the operating system. Use this table to match your symptoms to the most likely fix.

| Issue | Solution |
| :--- | :--- |
| Voice Control Conflict | Turn off Voice Control in System Settings > Accessibility > Voice Control. |
| Missing App Permissions | Go to System Settings > Privacy & Security > Microphone and verify the app access. |
| Wrong Mic Selected | Check System Settings > Keyboard and ensure the correct microphone source is selected. |
| Frozen Daemon | Open Terminal and run 'killall corespeechd' to restart the process. |
| Shortcut Conflict | Check settings in apps like Raycast or BetterTouchTool for conflicting hotkeys. |

## The Voice Control Conflict

Many users enable Voice Control to navigate their Mac hands-free, but they forget that it takes exclusive control of the microphone. If Voice Control is active, standard Dictation will often be disabled or unresponsive. To check this, navigate to System Settings, click on Accessibility, and then select Voice Control. If the toggle is on, turn it off. This is the most common hidden cause of dictation failure. Once disabled, try your dictation shortcut again.

## Microphone Permissions by App

macOS is strict about which applications can access your hardware. If you find that dictation works in some programs but not others, you likely denied microphone access to that specific app when you first opened it. To fix this, navigate to System Settings, click Privacy & Security, and select Microphone. You will see a list of apps that have requested access. Ensure the switch next to your target application is turned on. If it is already on, try toggling it off and back on to force a permission refresh.

## Checking Your Microphone Source

Your Mac might be trying to listen through a device that is not actually in front of you. If you have a webcam, an external monitor, or an audio interface connected, macOS might have defaulted to that input. Go to System Settings, select Keyboard, and look for the Dictation section. Check the Microphone source dropdown menu. Ensure the selected device matches the microphone you are actually using. If you are unsure, try switching it to 'Automatic' or selecting your internal MacBook microphone explicitly.

## Restarting the Speech Recognition Daemon

Sometimes the background process responsible for speech recognition, known as corespeechd, stops responding. This can happen after a long period of system uptime or following a macOS update. You do not need to restart your entire computer to fix this. Open the Terminal app, which you can find in your Utilities folder. Type the following command exactly: `killall corespeechd` and press Enter. This command forces the system to stop the hung process and restart it immediately. Most users find that dictation starts working again within seconds of running this command.

## Shortcut Conflicts

If you have customized your dictation shortcut, it might be fighting with other software. Many power users employ tools like Raycast, BetterTouchTool, or Karabiner-Elements to manage their workflows. These apps often override default system shortcuts. Check the settings in any app that manages global hotkeys. If you have assigned a command to your dictation trigger, change it to something unique that does not conflict with your other tools. A simple test is to change the default dictation shortcut to something else entirely, such as pressing the Command key twice, to see if the problem persists.

## Understanding the 30-Second Timeout

Apple Dictation includes an architectural inactivity timeout. If the system detects 30 seconds of silence, it automatically stops the session. This is not a bug, but a feature designed to save battery and processing power. If you are being cut off mid-sentence, it is likely due to a poor internet connection or a temporary pause in your speech. If you are using server-based dictation, a drop in your Wi-Fi signal can cause the session to terminate abruptly. Ensure you have a stable connection if you are dictating long documents.

## Dealing with Corrupted Cache

If dictation fails consistently after a major macOS update, the speech recognition cache may be corrupted. This cache stores language models and voice profiles. While a standard restart usually fixes minor glitches, persistent failures might require you to clear this data manually. You can access the cache folder by opening Finder, pressing ⇧ ⌘ G, and typing `~/Library/Caches/com.apple.SpeechRecognitionCore`. Move the contents of this folder to the Trash and restart your Mac. The system will rebuild these files the next time you use dictation.
