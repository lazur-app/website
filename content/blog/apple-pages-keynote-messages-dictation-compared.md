Apple Dictation uses the same system-wide engine for Pages, Keynote, and Messages. While the activation steps are identical, the way each app handles your voice input varies based on its internal features and formatting rules. ## How to Enable Dictation on Your Mac Before you begin, you must ensure your system is configured correctly. Go to the Apple menu and select System Settings. Navigate to Keyboard and scroll down to the Dictation section. Toggle the switch to on. You can choose your preferred microphone source and set a keyboard shortcut. The default is often pressing the Fn key twice. This shortcut works in every app, including Pages, Keynote, and Messages. ## Using Dictation in Pages and Keynote When you open Pages or Keynote, the dictation engine does not change. Click into any text box or document area where you want to add text. Press your chosen shortcut, such as Fn twice, and start speaking. You will see a microphone icon appear. When you are finished, press the shortcut again or click Done. In Pages, if you have Track Changes enabled, every sentence you dictate will appear as an insertion. This can make your document look messy if you are doing a lot of drafting by voice. You may want to turn off Track Changes before you start a long dictation session. Keynote presents a different challenge. If you are dictating into speaker notes, ensure the notes pane is active. If you click the slide canvas by mistake, your dictation might not appear where you expect. Always verify your cursor position before you start talking. ## Dictation in Messages The Messages app handles dictation more like a casual conversation. Since messages are usually short, the 30-second silence timeout is less likely to be an issue. However, you should still be careful with punctuation. While Apple includes auto-punctuation, it is not perfect. You might need to add manual commands like period or question mark to ensure your tone comes across clearly. Unlike Pages, Messages does not have complex formatting like headers or footnotes. This makes it a safer place to test your dictation speed and accuracy. ## Understanding the 30-Second Silence Limit The most common frustration for new users is the 30-second silence limit. If you stop to think about your next sentence for more than 30 seconds, the system stops listening. This is not a bug. It is a design choice to save battery and system resources. There is no way to turn this off in settings. If you need to pause frequently, you should get into the habit of toggling dictation off and on manually. This prevents the system from cutting you off in the middle of a thought. ## Performance and On-Device Processing Modern Macs with Apple silicon handle dictation on-device for many languages. This is a major benefit for privacy and speed. You do not need an active internet connection for your Mac to transcribe your voice. If you have an older Intel-based Mac, the experience might be different. You may notice a slight delay as the audio is processed. Keep your microphone clear and avoid noisy environments to keep accuracy high. ## Faster with Lazur When you need more than just raw text, ask Lazur to walk you through it. If you say something wrong, do not try to correct it by speaking over it. The dictation engine will simply add your correction as new text. Stop the dictation, delete the error manually, and start again. This is much faster than fighting with the software. Also, be mindful of background noise. Even with advanced noise cancellation, a loud office can confuse the transcript. If you are in a busy area, use a dedicated headset to improve your input quality. ## Final Considerations for Your Workflow Dictation is a tool for drafting, not for final editing. Use it to get your ideas onto the screen quickly. Once the text is there, switch to your keyboard to refine the formatting and structure. By separating these two steps, you will work much faster and with fewer errors in your final documents.
