You can use dictation on an Apple laptop by navigating to System Settings to enable the feature and then pressing the designated shortcut key. This built-in tool converts your speech into text in any application that accepts input, such as Mail, Notes, or web browsers. ## How to Enable Dictation on Your Mac Activating the feature is a one-time task that takes less than a minute. Open the Apple menu in the top left corner of your screen and select System Settings. Click on Keyboard in the sidebar. You may need to scroll down to find the Dictation section. Toggle the switch to On. A confirmation prompt will appear. Click Enable to confirm your choice. You can also select your preferred language here. If you plan to use multiple languages, ensure they are all selected in the list. On Apple Silicon Macs, the system will download the necessary language models for on-device processing. This means your audio does not leave your machine, which is a significant privacy benefit. ## Using the Dictation Shortcut Once enabled, you need a way to trigger the microphone. Look at your keyboard for the Fn or Globe key. By default, pressing this key twice or holding it down will start the dictation process. You will see a microphone icon appear on your screen when the system is listening. Start speaking clearly at a normal pace. You do not need to shout. The Mac will convert your words into text in real time. To stop dictating, press the same shortcut again or hit the Escape key. If you stop speaking for 30 seconds, the system will automatically turn off the microphone to save battery and processing power. ## Improving Your Dictation Accuracy While Apple Dictation is capable, it performs best when you speak with consistent rhythm. Avoid mumbling or speaking too quickly. If you have a noisy office, consider using a headset with a dedicated microphone. This helps the system isolate your voice from background noise. If you find the default shortcut inconvenient, you can change it. Go to System Settings > Keyboard and look for the Dictation shortcut dropdown. You can choose a custom key combination that feels more natural for your workflow. ## Dictation in Different Applications You can use dictation in almost any app that has a cursor. This includes Apple Notes, Pages, Microsoft Word, and even browser-based email clients. When you are in a text field, activate the shortcut and start talking. The text will appear exactly where your cursor is positioned. If you are writing a long document, try to dictate in smaller chunks. This makes it easier to review and correct any errors. If you find yourself needing more advanced voice control, you might want to look at [Apple Dictation vs AI dictation on Mac](/blog/apple-dictation-vs-ai-dictation) to see if a dedicated tool fits your needs better. ## Common Mistakes to Avoid Many users try to speak like a robot, which often results in lower accuracy. Speak naturally as if you were talking to a colleague. Another common mistake is failing to dictate punctuation. You can say period, comma, question mark, or new paragraph to format your text while you speak. If you forget to say these, you will have to manually edit the text later. For a full list of commands, check our guide on [dictation commands punctuation Mac](/blog/mac-dictation-punctuation-commands). ## When to Use Third-Party Tools While Apple's built-in tool is great for basic tasks, it may struggle with highly technical jargon. If you work in a specialized field, you might find that specialized tools offer better recognition for your specific vocabulary.
