To start dictating on your Mac, navigate to System Settings, select Keyboard, and toggle the Dictation switch to the on position. Once enabled, you can trigger the feature in any text field by pressing the designated microphone key or your custom keyboard shortcut. ## Enabling Dictation on Your Mac The first step is to ensure the feature is active. Open the Apple menu and click System Settings. From the sidebar, select Keyboard. Scroll down until you find the Dictation section. Toggle the switch to on. A prompt will appear asking you to confirm. Click Enable to finish the setup. This process is standard across all recent versions of macOS. Once enabled, your Mac is ready to listen for your voice input in any app, from Mail to Notes. ## How to Start and Stop Dictating After enabling the feature, using it is straightforward. Place your cursor in any text field where you want to input words. Press the Microphone key on your function row. If your keyboard does not have a specific microphone icon, you can use the default shortcut, which is often pressing the Command key twice or a custom combination you set in the Keyboard settings. Once the microphone icon appears on your screen, start speaking. Your Mac will convert your speech into text in real time. To stop the process, press the Escape key or the Microphone key again. The system also stops automatically after 30 seconds of silence. This prevents the Mac from recording unnecessary background noise. ## Improving Accuracy and Performance While built-in dictation is powerful, its performance depends on your environment. Speak at a normal pace and volume. Avoid mumbling or speaking too close to the microphone. If you are in a noisy room, the accuracy might decrease. macOS includes automatic punctuation by default. It inserts commas, periods, and question marks as you speak. If you find it adds punctuation in the wrong spots, you can disable this feature in the Keyboard settings. Simply go to the Dictation section and toggle off the Auto-punctuation option. ## Dictation vs. Voice Control It is important to distinguish between Dictation and Voice Control. Dictation is designed specifically for text entry. Voice Control is an accessibility feature found in the Accessibility settings that allows you to navigate your entire operating system using voice commands. If you enable Voice Control, standard Dictation is often disabled or replaced by the more advanced Voice Control engine. If you only need to type text, stick with the standard Dictation feature. ## Limitations to Keep in Mind The built-in system is excellent for short bursts of text. However, it does not support custom vocabulary. If you work in a field with specialized terminology, the Mac might struggle to spell those words correctly. Additionally, the 30-second silence timeout can be frustrating for long-form writing. If you need to pause to think, you will have to reactivate the microphone frequently. ## Faster with Lazur: Dictate If you find yourself needing to dictate longer passages or require better control over your snippets, consider using Lazur. Visit [the download page](/download) to get started. ## Frequently Asked Questions About Dictation Many users wonder if they can use dictation in specific apps. The answer is yes. Because it is a system-wide feature, it works in any application that accepts text input. This includes web browsers, email clients, and word processors. Another common question relates to privacy. On Macs with Apple silicon, dictation is processed on-device. This means your voice data is not sent to Apple servers, provided you have not enabled the Improve Siri & Dictation setting. For Intel-based Macs, audio is processed in the cloud, which requires an active internet connection. Ensure you are aware of your specific hardware capabilities to get the most out of the privacy features.
