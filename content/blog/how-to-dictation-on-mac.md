You can use dictation on Mac by enabling it in System Settings and pressing the designated keyboard shortcut. Once active, your Mac converts your voice into text in any application where you can type. 

## How to enable dictation on your Mac

To begin, open the Apple menu in the top left corner of your screen and choose System Settings. Navigate to the Keyboard section in the sidebar. You may need to scroll down to find the Dictation header. Toggle the switch to On. A prompt will appear asking you to confirm your choice. Click Enable to finalize the setup. This process is the same for all modern versions of macOS.

## Configuring your dictation settings

Once enabled, you should customize the settings to match your workflow. In the same Keyboard menu, look for the Language dropdown. Select the language you speak most often. If you are multilingual, you can select multiple languages here. Next, choose your primary microphone from the list. If you use an external headset or a dedicated USB microphone, select it to ensure the best audio capture. You can also set a custom keyboard shortcut. The default is often pressing the Fn or Globe key twice. You can change this to any combination you prefer, such as holding the Command key twice. Make sure to toggle Auto-punctuation to On as well. This feature automatically adds commas and periods as you speak, which saves you time during editing.

## How to start and stop dictating

To use dictation, click into any text field where you want to write. Press your chosen keyboard shortcut. You will see a microphone icon or a pulsing blue cursor appear on your screen. This indicates the Mac is listening. Speak clearly and at a normal pace. You do not need to over-enunciate. When you finish your thought, press the shortcut key again or hit the Escape key. The icon will disappear, and your text will remain in the field. If you pause for more than 30 seconds, the system will stop listening automatically.

## Understanding dictation accuracy and limitations

Apple Dictation is powerful, but it is not perfect. It can struggle with technical jargon, specific proper nouns, or heavy accents. If you find the output is not accurate, check your microphone placement. Ensure you are close enough to the mic and that your room is quiet. Background noise is the most common cause of transcription errors. If you need to dictate specialized terms, you might need to speak them slowly or manually correct them after the fact. Remember that Apple provides enhanced features on M3 or newer processors with at least 12 GB of memory. These machines handle punctuation and complex sentences much better than older hardware.

## Privacy and data handling

Your privacy is important when using voice features. On modern Apple Silicon Macs, much of the processing happens on-device. This means your audio does not necessarily leave your machine. You should verify your privacy settings by going to System Settings, then Privacy and Security, and finally Analytics and Improvements. Look for the Improve Siri and Dictation setting. If you prefer that your data is not sent to Apple for analysis, ensure this is toggled off. This keeps your voice data local to your machine.

## Dictation versus voice control

Do not confuse standard dictation with Voice Control. Voice Control is an accessibility feature designed to help you navigate your entire operating system. It allows you to open apps, click buttons, and scroll through pages using only your voice. When you have Voice Control turned on, standard dictation is usually disabled or behaves differently. If you only want to turn speech into text, stick to the standard dictation feature found in the Keyboard settings.

## Troubleshooting common issues

If dictation is not working, start by checking your microphone settings. Ensure the correct input device is selected in System Settings. Sometimes, a simple restart of the application you are using can fix issues where text is not appearing. If you are using a dedicated microphone key, remember that a single press activates dictation, while a long press might trigger Siri. If you have trouble with specific punctuation, ensure that auto-punctuation is enabled in the Keyboard settings. For persistent issues, check the Apple support page for your specific version of macOS.

## Best practices for clear results

To get the most out of your dictation, try to speak in complete sentences. Avoid filler words like um or ah, as the system will attempt to transcribe those as well. If you have a specific style you prefer, try to maintain a consistent rhythm. If you are dictating a long email or a report, break it into smaller chunks. This makes it easier to review and correct any mistakes. Always take a moment to proofread your text, as even the best systems can misinterpret words. Practice will help you learn how the system interprets your specific voice and cadence. Over time, you will find that you can dictate much faster than you can type.
