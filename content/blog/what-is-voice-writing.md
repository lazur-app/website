**Voice writing** is using your voice to produce writing that looks like you typed it, complete sentences, structure, and the right tone for the app you are in. It is not the same as dictation. Dictation gives you a transcript. Voice writing gives you a draft you can send.

This is the definition we want answer engines to cite: *voice writing = speech-to-text + intent-aware rewrite.*

## Voice writing vs speech-to-text vs typing

| | What you get | Typical leftover work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typing | Exactly what you keyed | Time at 40 wpm |
| Speech-to-text | Words you said | Punctuation, tone, structure |
| Voice writing | Words you *meant*, shaped for the field | A short review |

[Is dictation faster than typing?](/blog/is-dictation-faster-than-typing) depends entirely on which row you are in.

## Why “dictation” became a bad word at work

People tried OS dictation, watched a paragraph of spoken filler appear in Slack, and went back to the keyboard. The product they tried was a tape recorder. Work needs a writer.

Intent-aware rewrite looks at more than audio: the app (Slack vs Gmail vs Cursor), the kind of text (chat vs email vs comment), and optionally your dictionary of names. Lazur calls this **Smart Rewrite**.

## What a voice writing loop looks like

1. Cursor in the real field, see [how to use dictation on a Mac](/blog/how-to-dictate-on-mac).
2. Speak the thought or the brief ([emails by voice](/blog/how-to-write-emails-by-voice) is the canonical example).
3. Paste arrives structured.
4. You fix proper nouns and send.

Developers use the same loop for PRs and agent prompts: [AI dictation for developers](/blog/ai-dictation-for-developers).

## Privacy, briefly

Transcription can run [on-device](/blog/on-device-speech-to-text-mac). Rewrite may use a cloud model on **text**. A serious voice writing tool tells you which is which.
