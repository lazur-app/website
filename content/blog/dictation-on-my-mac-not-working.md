If dictation on your Mac is not working, you can usually fix it by toggling the Dictation setting off and on again in System Settings. This simple action often clears stuck background processes that prevent the microphone from listening to your voice. When you find that dictation on your Mac is not working, do not assume your hardware is broken. Most problems stem from software conflicts, privacy permissions, or incorrect input configurations that take only a few minutes to resolve.

## Reset the Dictation Service

The fastest way to fix a frozen dictation feature is to perform a hard reset of the service. Navigate to the Apple menu and select System Settings. Click on Keyboard in the sidebar. Scroll down to the Dictation section. Toggle the switch to Off. Wait for at least 10 seconds before turning it back on. This forces the system to re-initialize the speech-to-text engine and often resolves temporary glitches.

## Check Your Microphone Privacy Settings

If dictation on your Mac is not working only within specific applications, you likely have a permission issue. macOS protects your privacy by requiring explicit permission for apps to access your microphone. Go to System Settings, then click Privacy and Security. Select Microphone from the list. Look for the application where you are trying to dictate. If the toggle is off, turn it on. If the app is not in the list, try restarting the application and triggering the microphone access request again.

## Verify Your Input Source

Sometimes the system selects the wrong microphone input, leaving you wondering why dictation on your Mac is not working. Go to System Settings and click Sound. Select the Input tab. You will see a list of available microphones. Choose the one you intend to use. Speak into the microphone and watch the Input level meter. If the bar does not move when you talk, your Mac is not receiving audio. Check your cable connections or ensure your Bluetooth headset is connected properly.

## Identify Voice Control Conflicts

Voice Control is a powerful accessibility feature, but it behaves differently than standard Dictation. If you have Voice Control enabled, it takes exclusive control of your microphone to listen for commands. This often disables standard text dictation. To check this, go to System Settings, then Accessibility. Click on Voice Control and ensure it is turned off. If you need both, you might find they conflict when trying to use them simultaneously.

## Use Terminal to Restart the Speech Engine

If the standard settings do not work, you can manually restart the background process responsible for speech recognition. Open the Terminal app from your Applications folder or via Spotlight. Type the following command and press Enter: killall corespeechd. This command stops the speech daemon, and the system will automatically restart it. This is a common fix for when the microphone icon appears but no text is transcribed.

## Understanding the 30-Second Timeout

Many users worry that dictation on your Mac is not working because it stops abruptly. The system is designed to stop listening after 30 seconds of silence to conserve resources. This is not a malfunction. If you pause to think for more than half a minute, you must press your shortcut key again to resume. This behavior is standard across all recent macOS versions.

## When to Consider Third-Party Tools

Apple's built-in dictation is excellent for casual use, but it lacks advanced features for professional workflows. If you need custom vocabulary, persistent transcript history, or better grammar correction, you might outgrow the native tool. Dedicated apps offer these capabilities, though they operate differently than the system-level dictation. Always check the developer's website for specific requirements and pricing before switching.

## Language and Region Settings

If your dictation is transcribing words incorrectly, check your language settings. Go to System Settings, then Keyboard, and click on Dictation. Ensure the selected language matches the one you are speaking. If you are bilingual, you can add multiple languages here. Some languages require a larger download for on-device processing. Ensure your system has finished downloading these assets before attempting to use them offline.
